## City Context: Ajman, United Arab Emirates

Ajman is the smallest emirate in the UAE by area, but it holds a strategic position on the Arabian Gulf coast, approximately 30 km north of Dubai and 10 km south of Sharjah. The city is known for a quieter, more relaxed pace compared to the mega-cities nearby, while still offering access to major employment centres and leisure facilities.

The population of Ajman is around 500,000, with a high proportion of expatriates and students from across the Middle East, South Asia, and Africa. The cost of living is generally lower than in Dubai, making it a practical base for students who want to be within commuting distance of Dubai but pay lower rent and daily expenses.

The neighbourhood of Al Jerf, where Ajman University is located on University Street, is a residential and commercial area. It has grocery stores, cafes, pharmacies, and bus stops within walking distance. The university is close to the Ajman Corniche, a waterfront promenade popular for jogging and evening walks. The nearby Ajman City Centre mall offers shopping and dining.

Transport infrastructure includes public buses operated by the Ajman Transport Authority and inter-emirate buses that connect to Sharjah and Dubai. Taxis are widely available and affordable compared to Dubai. Many students rely on a combination of walking, shared taxis, and bus services.

## Walk vs Transit Trade-off in Ajman

Ajman University’s location on University Street – Al Jerf 1 places it in a moderately walkable area. Within a 10-minute radius you will find a few supermarkets, pharmacies, and small eateries. However, many residential areas popular with students lie along Sheikh Maktoum Bin Rashid Street or near the Corniche, which are 20–40 minutes on foot.

**Choosing to walk** saves you money on transport and keeps your daily routine simple. If you can find housing within a 15-minute walk, you avoid traffic and parking costs. The trade-off is that the immediate vicinity of campus has a limited selection of restaurants and shops; for a full supermarket or a larger mall you may need a short bus ride.

**Choosing transit** gives you access to a wider range of housing options at lower rents. Many students live in the Al Nuaimiya area (about 3 km from campus) or near the Ajman Industrial area, where rents can be 20–30% cheaper than in Al Jerf. Public buses run every 15–30 minutes along major routes, and a monthly bus pass costs around AED 80–100. Shared taxis (often called “service taxis”) charge AED 3–5 per ride within the city. A daily commute of 20–30 minutes one way is common.

The trade-off is time and reliability. Traffic in Ajman is rarely heavy, but bus schedules can be less frequent in the evenings. If you have late classes or clinical rotations (common for medical students), a walking-distance apartment may be worth the premium.

## Shortlist Logic: How to Choose Your Housing

When building your shortlist of potential rooms or apartments near Ajman University, consider these factors in order of priority.

### 1. Distance to campus

Given the positive reviews about administrative staff availability, being close to campus helps you attend in-person meetings, last-minute orientation sessions, and peer study groups without stress. Aim for a 15-minute walk as your upper limit if your budget allows. If you are in the College of Medicine, note that lectures and labs are concentrated in specific buildings; check which building your department uses and map walking time from each property.

### 2. Budget and included costs

Rent is only one part of your monthly outlay. Utilities (water, electricity, cooling) in Ajman typically cost AED 300–500 per month for a studio, higher in summer. Internet can add AED 200–300 per month. Ask the landlord or operator: “What is included in the rent?”. If a property is listed at AED 25,000 but bills are separate, the total annual cost could be AED 30,000 or more.

### 3. Lease length and flexibility

Most residential towers in Ajman require a one-year lease. Student-specific housing operators sometimes offer 9-month or semester-based leases, but they are less common. If you are only staying for one academic year, consider subletting from a graduating student or finding a room in a shared apartment. Confirm early termination policies.

### 4. Safety and security

Al Jerf is generally safe, but check whether the building has 24-hour security, CCTV in common areas, and secure entry. Female students may want to search for female-only floors or buildings. Reading recent Google reviews of the building can reveal issues like broken gates or faulty locks.

### 5. Furnishing and move-in condition

Fully furnished apartments are common and can save you the hassle of buying furniture. However, check the quality of the bed, air conditioner, and kitchen appliances. Many landlords provide only basic items. If you need a desk or a study lamp, confirm they will be present.

### 6. Access to public transport

Even if you plan to walk, having a bus stop or taxi point within 5 minutes gives you a backup for days when the weather is too hot (summer temperatures exceed 45°C) or you are running late. For students who intend to visit Dubai on weekends, a 20-minute bus ride to Union Bus Station in Sharjah connects to Dubai Metro.

### 7. Community and social life

The reviews for Ajman University mention a friendly atmosphere. Living in a building with other students — even from different universities — can help you build a peer network. Some residential towers have common rooms or rooftop gardens where students gather. Ask the operator if there are other students already living there.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much should I expect to pay for a studio within walking distance of Ajman University?

Unfurnished or older units can be found from AED 20,000. **All figures are indicative — confirm with the operator** as rates change based on demand and season. ### Is it better to live in Al Jerf or in a cheaper area farther away?

Al Jerf offers convenience and time savings. If your budget is tight, living 3–4 km away in Al Nuaimiya or near the Corniche can lower rent by 15–25%. The trade-off is a 20–30 minute commute by bus or shared taxi. For medical students with irregular hours, proximity often justifies the extra cost. Review your class schedule before deciding.

### What should I check before signing a lease?

Visit the property during daylight hours. Confirm the air conditioner works, the internet provider covers the building, and there are no signs of pests. Ask about security deposits (usually 5% of annual rent) and whether they are refundable. Request a written inventory of furniture and appliances. If you are sharing, get a clear agreement on utility splitting and cleaning duties.
