## Campus Reviews and Neighborhood Context

The American University in Dubai (AUD) holds a Google rating of 4.3 out of 5, based on 405 reviews. While many students praise the institution’s facilities and academic atmosphere, others highlight issues that you may want to consider when choosing a place to live nearby.

One reviewer, Arman Safaei, gave a 5‑star rating and described AUD as “an incredible place for innovation and collaboration … more than just a university; it’s becoming a hub where professionals, students, and creatives come together to exchange ideas.” This suggests a campus culture that values cross‑disciplinary interaction and external partnerships.

Another 5‑star review from Edarabia UAE notes that the university “follows an American‑style higher education model and serves a diverse international student body. The university emphasizes industry connections, academic support, and a m…” – the text cuts off, but the implication is that career‑oriented support is a recognised strength.

Syed Naved Haider, also rating 5 stars, describes AUD as an “awesome big university crowded with students but quiet and disciplined. Beautiful building & great qualified faculty, clean and good atmosphere to study but expensive though.” The mention of “expensive” hints at the cost of living in the surrounding area.

On the other hand, Umid BRO gave a 1‑star review, stating: “Unfortunately, my experience with the American University in Dubai was disappointing. The scheduled Zoom meeting never happened, and even after visiting the campus twice, I couldn’t get clear answers to my questions. Emails were also left unanswered. I expected a more professional approach.” This negative experience relates to pre‑enrolment communication, which can affect how quickly you settle into accommodation arrangements.

What do these reviews mean for housing? They show that AUD attracts a diverse, internationally‑minded student body, some of whom value industry connections and collaboration. The campus environment is described as disciplined but busy. At the same time, administrative responsiveness can vary. When looking for housing, you may want to prioritise providers that offer clear, prompt communication and reliable leases, especially if you are coordinating your move from abroad.

## Dubai as a Student City: Key Considerations

Dubai is a city of distinct districts. AUD is located in Al Sufouh, part of the Dubai Media City area, along Sheikh Zayed Road. This places it near major employment zones, the Dubai Marina, and the Palm Jumeirah. Public transport includes the Dubai Metro (Red Line), the Dubai Tram, and several bus routes. The nearest metro station to AUD is about 2 km away (you can take a tram or bus, or walk 25 minutes), while the Dubai Tram’s Al Sufouh stop is closer to the campus.

Living costs in Dubai vary substantially. Studio apartments near campus typically range from around 3,500 AED to 6,000 AED per month, but student‑friendly options such as co‑living spaces or serviced apartments may be available at different price points. The following guide uses indicative price data from a student‑housing platform to help you plan a budget.

## Walk vs. Transit: How Far from Campus?

AUD’s address is on Sheikh Zayed Road in Al Sufouh. The closest residential building among the four sampled listings is **Al Sufouh Suites – Al Sufouh**, which is in the same neighbourhood. You could walk to campus in around 10–15 minutes, or take a short tram ride. This option removes the need for a car or daily transit pass.

**Makkan** and **Yugo Dubailand** are located further east and south, respectively. Yugo Dubailand is a purpose‑built student accommodation complex but is roughly 20 km from AUD. Commuting by bus or metro would take 45–60 minutes each direction. You would benefit from lower rent but spend more time travelling.

**Staybridge Suites – Al Maktoum Airport** is even farther (about 30 km southwest), near the Dubai World Central area. It offers long‑stay rates and is often used by students who have vehicles or work in the southern industrial zones. For a main‑campus student, this is less practical unless you are located near the Dubai Metro Red Line extension (the airport station is served by a different line) and are prepared for a long commute.

If you prioritise walking, search for apartments in Al Sufouh itself (e.g. Al Sufouh Suites) or in the adjacent Dubai Media City and Dubai Marina (within a 20‑minute walk or short tram ride). If you can accept a commute, you can access a wider range of budgets and room types.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**1. Is it cheaper to rent a room in a shared apartment or choose a serviced studio near AUD?**  
It depends on the specific building and lease duration. Shared rooms in areas like Dubailand can go below 1,000 AED per month (indicative), while a serviced studio in Al Sufouh may cost 3,500 AED or more. However, a shared apartment may require a separate transport pass (around 350–600 AED per month for metro/bus). Add utility splitting costs. Compare the total monthly cost: a more expensive studio within walking distance could end up being similar to a cheap room plus long commute. Always run the numbers with actual quotes.

**2. How does public transport work for students in Dubai?**  
Dubai’s public transport is integrated and relatively reliable. Students can purchase a Nol card (blue card for metro, tram, bus) and load credit. A monthly pass covering all zones costs about 350 AED. The Dubai Tram runs along Al Sufouh and connects to the Dubai Marina and Jumeirah Beach Residence. Buses from Dubailand to the nearest metro station (e.g., Dubai Internet City) take roughly 30–45 minutes. Keep in mind that summer temperatures (May–September) can make walking or waiting at bus stops uncomfortable, so air‑conditioned transport is essential.

**3. What lease lengths are typical for student housing near AUD?**  
Leases vary widely. Short‑stay serviced apartments (Al Sufouh Suites, Staybridge Suites) often offer weekly or monthly contracts. Makaan is known for monthly rolling contracts. Yugo Dubailand typically offers semester‑long or full‑academic‑year tenancies (e.g., 10 or 12 months). Always clarify the minimum stay, and whether you can sublet or break the lease early – especially if your study plans change.
