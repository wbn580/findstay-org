## What Campus Reviews Reveal About Student Life

The American University of the Middle East (AUM) holds a QS ranking of 563 and a Google rating of 4.2 from 422 reviews. Student feedback highlights both strengths and practical concerns that directly affect housing decisions. One Computer Engineering student wrote: “The university offers excellent facilities, modern labs, and well-equipped classrooms that make learning practical and enjoyable. The professors are knowledgeable, approachable.” This positive assessment of campus quality suggests that spending time on campus itself is valued – but getting there comfortably is equally important.

Another reviewer noted a parking issue: “I took my daughter to take the aptitude test. We arrived according to the instructions, but we were not allowed to enter the shaded parking spaces under the pretext that they are designated for students. I was forced to stand in the outdoor parking spaces under the sun.” This indicates that student-only parking exists, but visitors or those without a permit may face discomfort. For students living off‑campus, having a vehicle does not guarantee shaded or convenient parking unless you qualify for a student permit. A third reviewer reflected a more critical view: “Pros: Beautiful architecture Nice plants Huge campus … Their target is fame, social media clout and impressing teens with soccer nights open for all, concerts.” While this comment questions institutional priorities, it also confirms that the campus is large and visually appealing – factors that make proximity to campus more desirable if you want to enjoy those facilities regularly.

These reviews collectively point to one clear message: the campus environment is a major asset, but access and logistics (parking, heat, commuting) matter. Your housing choice should reduce friction between your residence and the campus gate.

## City Context: Kuwait

AUM’s address is 250 St, Kuwait – located in the Egaila area of Kuwait City. Kuwait City is the capital and largest city of the country, with a hot desert climate where summer temperatures regularly exceed 45 °C (113 °F). The city is car‑dependent; public transport is limited, and most residents rely on private vehicles or ride‑hailing services. Traffic congestion can be heavy during peak hours, especially on major arteries like the Fahaheel Expressway and the 5th Ring Road.

For students, this means that housing within a short walking distance (10–15 minutes) offers a real advantage in terms of comfort and time savings. However, walkable neighborhoods near AUM are limited primarily to the Egaila and surrounding residential blocks. Areas farther away, such as Salmiya or Kuwait City centre, require a vehicle or taxi and add 20–40 minutes each way depending on traffic. The availability of air‑conditioned walkways is rare, so even a 15‑minute walk in summer can be physically demanding.

## Walk vs Transit: Choosing Your Commute

Given the climate and parking constraints revealed in the reviews, the trade‑off between walking and using transport is fundamental.

**Walking** works best if you live in Egaila itself or in the residential compounds directly adjacent to the 250 St campus. The huge campus mentioned in a review means that even after arriving at the university gate, you may need to walk across a large site to reach your classroom. Living within a 5‑ to 10‑minute walk of the main entrance reduces total commute stress. However, walking in the summer months (May–October) requires shade and hydration; some students choose to drive the short distance simply to stay in air conditioning.

**Transit** in Kuwait means either a private car, taxi (Careem or Uber), or a shared ride. The university provides student parking in shaded lots, as noted in the reviewer’s complaint about visitors being denied access. If you obtain a valid student parking permit, driving becomes feasible. Yet parking spaces may fill up early, and the outdoor lots are unshaded. For students living more than 15 minutes away by car, daily commuting can become a significant time and cost burden. Ride‑hailing fees from central Kuwait City to AUM are approximately 5–8 KWD per trip (indicative — confirm with operator).

For those without a car, the bus network (CityBus and KPTC) serves major routes but does not always run near the university gate. Most students who rely on public transport combine buses with a short walk or taxi from the nearest stop.

## Shortlist Logic for Finding a Place

Because this dataset does not include a specific list of nearby residences, you will need to conduct your own search. The following logic can help you prioritise options based on the information we have:

2. **Check for student‑friendly leases** – Kuwaiti rental contracts often require a full year’s commitment. Ask whether the operator offers semester‑based or student‑specific leases. All prices and terms are indicative — confirm with the operator.
3. **Prioritise shaded parking if driving** – If you choose a place farther away, inquire whether the building provides covered parking. Otherwise, you may face the same sun exposure described in the visitor review.
4. **Read recent tenant reviews** – For each shortlisted building, search for independent feedback about maintenance, noise, and landlord responsiveness. The review culture at AUM shows that honest (and sometimes critical) opinions exist; apply the same scrutiny to housing.
5. **Visit during peak heat** – If possible, view the property in the afternoon (2–4 pm) to experience the commute and the building’s cooling performance. This is especially important for walkable units.
6. **Confirm utility bills** – In Kuwait, electricity and water are heavily subsidised for citizens but can be expensive for expatriates if the rental agreement excludes them. Always ask: “Are utilities included?” and get the answer in writing.
