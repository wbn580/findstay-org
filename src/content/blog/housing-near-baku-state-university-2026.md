If your accommodation is very close to the campus, you can quickly communicate in person when problems arise; if you live far away, it may add unnecessary trouble.

## Baku City Background and Student Life

Baku is the capital of Azerbaijan and the largest city on the Caspian Sea. It blends Soviet-era architecture, modern glass skyscrapers, and stone-paved streets in the old city. The student body is fairly international, with many coming from Turkey, Iran, Russia, and South Asian countries. The cost of living in the city is mid-to-high among CIS countries, but still low compared to major European cities.

In terms of public transportation, the Baku Metro covers the core areas, with a single-trip fare of about 0.4 manat (2025 standard, indicative price—check with operator). The main campus is near the “28 May” and “Ganjlik” metro stations, but note: the exact walking distance depends on which entrance you choose. Taxis are cheap, with short trips within the city usually not exceeding 5 manat (indicative—check with operator), but congestion is significant during peak hours.

For living costs, grocery prices are relatively stable, and a single person’s basic monthly expenses (excluding accommodation) are about 300–500 manat (indicative—check with operator). Housing is the largest variable.

Note: Whether the above prices include tax and whether water, electricity, and heating are included must be confirmed in writing with the landlord in advance. Baku winters are cold, and heating costs are a hidden expense.

## Trade-offs Between Walking and Public Transportation

The university is located in the city center, and accommodation within walking distance is concentrated around Zahid Xəlilov Street and near the “28 May” metro station. The benefits of walking to campus are obvious: you can arrange your time flexibly and avoid morning metro crowds. The residential buildings near the university are mostly Soviet-era “khrushchevka” apartment blocks, some of which have been renovated, with large variations in soundproofing and insulation.

But the walking range means higher rent. Within the core walking circle (no more than 15 minutes), a one-bedroom monthly rent may reach 600–1000 manat (indicative—check with operator). If your budget is limited, you can extend your search along the metro line. The Baku Metro runs until late at night, with short intervals between trains during peak hours (3–5 minutes), but cars can be very crowded. Commuting from the terminal station to the university generally takes 20–40 minutes.

Trade-off points:

- Budget priority: Choose neighborhoods 3–5 stations away from the city center on the metro line, such as near “Neftchilar,” where rent may be 20–30% lower.
- Time priority: Choose housing within a 10-minute walk of the university to reduce commuting stress.
- Comfort priority: Avoid old houses next to main roads; spend time checking the heating and window seals during viewings.

1. **Determine the commuting radius**: On Google Maps (or a local mapping platform), set a 15-minute walking circle and a 30-minute public transport circle centered on Baku State University’s address.
2. **Check transport nodes**: Prioritize housing within a 10-minute walk of a metro station or bus hub. Baku bus routes are unstable; the metro is more reliable.
3. **Check the actual condition of the apartment**: Be sure to do a video tour or on-site inspection. Pay attention to the electrical system and heating in old buildings. If the rent is unusually low (e.g., below 200 manat), there is likely a serious problem.
4. **Ask about contract terms**: Baku rental contracts usually require one month’s deposit plus one month’s rent, or two months’ deposit plus one month’s rent. Make sure to clarify whether subletting is allowed and the notice period for early termination. Do not sign a blank contract or one in Russian/Azerbaijani without understanding the key terms.
5. **Find a roommate**: If you don’t know any potential roommates, try looking in university WeChat groups (if recommended by classmates) or on dormitory bulletin boards. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What additional fees do I need to pay when renting near Baku State University?

In addition to monthly rent, you usually need to pay a refundable deposit equivalent to 1–2 months’ rent. Utilities (water, electricity, gas, heating, and internet) are generally the tenant’s responsibility, averaging about 80–150 manat per month (indicative—check with operator). Some landlords include property management fees in the rent; make sure this is written into the contract. Also, if you find housing through an agent, you will pay one month’s rent as a commission.

### Is the summer break (months 6–8) suitable for subletting or ending a lease?

Baku summers are hot, and the number of tourists in the city center increases, raising short-term rental demand, but long-term rental demand drops. If you plan to go home or travel, you can consider subletting your room to short-term students taking summer courses. You must obtain written permission from the landlord before subletting, or your deposit may be withheld.

### Do I need to live in an area with a high concentration of international students?

Baku does not have a strictly defined “student district,” but the streets near the university (such as the Soviet-era apartment area along Zahid Xəlilov Street) attract many international students. The advantage of living in these areas is that it is easier to find subletting information and second-hand furniture; the disadvantage is that some buildings are poorly maintained. It is recommended to view at least 3 different properties before deciding, and not to rely solely on photos.
