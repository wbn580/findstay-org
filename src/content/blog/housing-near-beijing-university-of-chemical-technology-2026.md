## Walk versus transit trade-off

Deciding between walking distance and commuting by public transport involves balancing cost, time, and convenience.

**Walking distance (≤20 minutes on foot)**  
Living within a 15–20-minute walk of BUCT means you can easily attend classes, use the library, and access campus facilities without depending on schedules. Typical catchment areas include the immediate neighbourhoods around the university — often older apartment complexes or university-provided housing. Rent is generally higher per square metre, and spaces may be smaller. Availability can be limited, especially at the start of semesters when demand peaks.

**Transit commute (20–50 minutes by subway/bus)**  
Renting in areas such as Huilongguan, Tiantongyuan, or further south along Line 5 or Line 13 can lower rent by 20–40% compared with campus-adjacent options. However, you must factor in travel time, subway fares, and the reliability of the transport network. The Beijing subway is extensive but can be crowded during peak hours. A 40-minute door-to-door commute is manageable for many students, but those with early morning classes or lab work may prefer shorter travel.

The trade-off also includes lifestyle: living further out may give you access to larger apartments, quieter streets, or lower food costs, while living close to campus exposes you to student events and a shorter time to the canteen.
