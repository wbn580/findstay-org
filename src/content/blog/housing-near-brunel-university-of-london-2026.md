From this experience I tried to take my own life numerous times.” This points to safety and mental health concerns. If you are considering off-campus housing, feeling unsafe on campus could push you toward neighborhoods where you feel more secure, even if that means a longer commute.

While these four reviews are a small sample of 870 total, they are recent (within the last five months) and uniformly negative. In a student housing context, they reinforce the need to:

- Verify the university’s current support for international students before signing a lease.
- Look for housing in areas with good street lighting, public transport, and community feel — especially if you have safety concerns.
- Clarify whether the university provides any long-term email or support after graduation, if you plan to stay in the area.

### Uxbridge and London – City Context

Brunel University is located in Uxbridge, a town in the London Borough of Hillingdon, about 15 miles west of central London. The address is Campus, Kingston Ln, Uxbridge UB8 3PH, UK. Uxbridge has a compact town centre with shops, restaurants, and a tube station (Metropolitan line) that connects to central London in roughly 45–60 minutes. The area is greener and quieter than inner London, with parks and the Grand Union Canal nearby.

For students, Uxbridge offers a mix of suburban convenience and easy access to London. The cost of living is generally lower than in Zone 1–2, but rents have risen in recent years due to demand from both students and commuters. The university has its own halls of residence on campus, but many students also rent privately in Uxbridge town, Hillingdon, Ickenham, or Ruislip. For those who need part-time work, Uxbridge has retail and service jobs, while central London offers wider opportunities.

### Walk vs Transit Trade-Off

The university’s main campus is within walking distance of Uxbridge town centre (about 15–20 minutes on foot) and close to the Uxbridge tube station (Metropolitan line, also connecting to the Piccadilly line via Rayners Lane). The trade-offs are:

![Brunel University of London](https://img.unistay.cn/housing/p/amber-1275932.jpg)

- **Walking distance (0–1.5 km from campus)**: Properties on Kingston Lane, Cleveland Road, or near the town centre are walkable to lectures. You save on transport costs (no bus or tube fare) and can easily access campus facilities. However, rents in this radius are often higher, and availability can be tight. Noise from the town centre may be an issue for some.
- **Short commute (1.5–5 km, bus or cycle)**: Areas like Hillingdon, Ickenham, or Ruislip Manor are a short bus ride or cycle. Buses run frequently to the university. Rent tends to be slightly lower than in the immediate campus area. You will spend about 10–20 minutes on transit each way, plus the cost of a bus pass (around £25–35 per week for a student bus pass, or per-trip fares).
- **Longer transit (tube ride of 10–30 minutes)**: Consider stations on the Metropolitan line such as Northwood, Moor Park, or even Harrow-on-the-Hill. These areas offer more affordable housing and larger rooms, but journey times to Uxbridge (via tube then a short walk or bus) can add 30–60 minutes daily. If your timetable has gaps, this may be manageable, but factor in tube delays and the cost of a Travelcard (around £50–70 per week for Zones 3–6).

Walkers save time and money but pay higher rent. Transit riders spend more time and money on travel but may get lower rent and a quieter neighborhood. Your choice should depend on your tolerance for commuting and whether you plan to frequently go into central London for work or leisure.
