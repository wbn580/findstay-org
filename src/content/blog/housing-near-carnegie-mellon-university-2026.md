## Campus Reviews: What Students and Visitors Say

Carnegie Mellon University (CMU) sits at 5000 Forbes Avenue in Pittsburgh, a private research university ranked 52nd globally by QS. The campus itself draws consistent praise. One reviewer, Taylor Fotheringham, who visited for a non-academic tour, described it as “absolutely a gorgeous campus with buildings that left me speechless.” The comment highlights that even a casual visitor can appreciate the architecture and curated spaces, such as the special botanical book collection she saw.

Current and former students focus on the university’s academic intensity and specialization. Niraj Pande wrote: “Fantastic University! One of the top ranked universities in the United States, especially in Computer science and Robotics. Also one of the most expensive universities! The campus is decent. The location is pretty good. It is very well connected to almost all the locations of the city due to its cen…” That last word is cut off, but the general point is clear: CMU’s central location in Pittsburgh means students have good access to the rest of the city.

Dariusz Prokopowicz, speaking from professional circles, called CMU “a premier crucible of interdisciplinary innovation, where the fusion of technology and the arts creates a truly unique intellectual ecosystem.” This echoes CMU’s well-known strengths in computer science, engineering, robotics, and the fine arts, which draw a diverse student body from around the world.

Another reviewer, Eva Gao, noted: “An institution that truly reflects the values that drive America’s strength and competitiveness. It prepares students for the demands of the modern workforce, with fewer distractions of political ideology and more focus on excellence and innovation.” Whether you agree with that framing or not, the intensity of the academic environment is a factor students should consider when choosing where to live. Living close to campus – or at least with a short commute – can help manage the workload.

The university’s Google rating is 4.7 from 680 reviews, which is very high. Many reviews mention the campus’s beauty, the high cost of tuition (and housing), and the challenging but rewarding academic culture.

## Pittsburgh Context: A City That Works with the University

CMU is located in the Oakland neighborhood of Pittsburgh, a major educational and medical hub that also hosts the University of Pittsburgh (Pitt) and the University of Pittsburgh Medical Center (UPMC). The area is walkable, bikeable, and well served by Pittsburgh’s bus system.

Pittsburgh itself is a mid-sized city with a relatively low cost of living compared to other major U.S. metros like New York, San Francisco, or Boston. That said, housing near a top private university can still be expensive. The local rental market includes a mix of older houses converted into apartments, purpose-built student housing high-rises, and single-family homes.

The city has a strong industrial history (steel, manufacturing) but has reinvented itself around technology, healthcare, and education. CMU is a major player in this transformation, attracting talent from all over the world. International students in particular will find a city that is welcoming and diverse, though winters can be cold and snowy.

When looking for housing near CMU, the immediate area (Oakland, Squirrel Hill, Shadyside, North Oakland) offers the shortest commutes. Further-out neighborhoods like the North Shore or South Side can also work if you have good bus access or a car, but parking can be expensive and limited on campus.

## Walk vs Transit: Getting to CMU

CMU’s central campus is at 5000 Forbes Avenue, right in the heart of Oakland. The location is highly walkable for anyone living in the Oakland, Squirrel Hill, or Shadyside neighborhoods. Many students choose to walk between 10 and 30 minutes each way.

**Walking** is the most popular option for students living within a mile of campus. The area is pedestrian-friendly, with sidewalks and crosswalks. Walking also saves money on bus fare or parking.

**Public transit** is provided by the Pittsburgh Regional Transit (PRT) bus system. Several bus lines run along Forbes and Fifth Avenues, connecting Oakland to downtown, the North Shore, and other neighborhoods. A student monthly pass costs around $40–$50 (indicative – confirm with the operator for current rates). Transit is especially useful for those living farther away, such as on the North Shore (where Bracken House North Shore is located) or in the South Side.

**Biking** is also common, though Pittsburgh’s hills can be challenging. The city has been adding bike lanes, and CMU itself has bike racks and a bike-share program.

**Driving** is possible but not recommended for daily commuting. Campus parking is limited and expensive (monthly permits can cost $100–$200, indicative). Street parking in Oakland is mostly metered or restricted.

Given the available sample of nearby residences, you can see a mix of walkable and transit-dependent options:

- **The Bridge On Forbes** – located on Forbes Avenue itself, likely a 5–10 minute walk to most CMU buildings.
- **SkyVue** – also in Oakland, walking distance.
- **HERE Pittsburgh** – likely in the East End (Shadyside or East Liberty), walkable or short bus ride.
- **Bracken House North Shore** – on the North Shore, a longer commute (bus or bike across the river, ~20–30 minutes transit).
