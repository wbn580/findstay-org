## City Context: Rockhampton Living

Rockhampton is a regional city in Queensland, approximately 700 kilometers north of Brisbane. It has a population of about 80,000 people and serves as a service center for the broader Central Queensland region. The city is known for its tropical climate, with warm, humid summers and mild winters. The cost of living in Rockhampton is generally lower than in major Australian cities like Sydney or Melbourne, but it is not as cheap as some smaller towns.

Norman Gardens, where the CQUniversity Rockhampton campus is located, is a residential suburb about 10 minutes by car from the city center. The suburb has a mix of standalone houses, townhouses, and some newer apartment developments. Shops, fast-food outlets, and basic amenities are available within a short drive, but the area is not densely urban. Public bus services connect Norman Gardens to the city center, but frequencies may be limited compared to major cities.

The local economy is driven by mining, agriculture, and tourism. International students often find casual work in hospitality, retail, or seasonal industries. ## Walking vs Public Transit: The Trade-Off

For students at CQUniversity’s Rockhampton campus, the key factor in choosing accommodation is the trade-off between walking distance and public transit access.

### Walking Distance (up to 20 minutes)

Properties within a 20-minute walk of the campus are mostly located in Norman Gardens itself. The suburb has footpaths and is relatively flat, making walking feasible for most students. Advantages of walking: no bus schedule to follow, no transport cost, and easy access to campus for evening classes or after-hours study sessions (if a student access card is available). However, accommodation within walking distance tends to be on the higher side of the budget range due to convenience. Availability of shared rooms within walking distance is limited, so students on low budgets might need to search carefully.

### Public Transit (bus)

The Rockhampton city bus network services Norman Gardens, with routes connecting the campus to the city center and surrounding suburbs. A typical one-way trip from the city to the campus takes about 15-20 minutes. Students can purchase a multi-trip ticket or use the public transport smartcard (if available). Disadvantages: buses may run less frequently in the evening and on weekends, and the combined cost of a bus pass (around 20-40 AUD per week) adds to the housing budget. Students living further away (e.g., in Rockhampton city or suburbs like Koongal or Glenmore) might find cheaper rents (100-150 AUD per week for a room) but need to account for transit time and cost.

The trade-off is clear: closer, more expensive housing saves time and transport costs, while cheaper, more distant housing requires a bus and adds to weekly expenditure. A student on a mid budget might consider a bus ride of 10-15 minutes to reach campus, spending 20 AUD per week on transport, which is comparable to walking distance premium.
