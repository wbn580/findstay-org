## Campus Reviews & What Students Say

Chalmers University of Technology holds a Google rating of 4.7 from 502 reviews. The comments from students and visitors reveal a consistent theme: strong academic support, well-equipped facilities, and an active student community. One reviewer, Daniel Uphoff, wrote: *“I have studied at 4 Universities in 3 countries and this is by far the best I have been too yet. Highly supportive staff and teachers, great courses, great campus and a very good student life! Would always recommend! (Program: Industrial Ecology)”* (5-star, a year ago).

Another student, Varun, echoed the emphasis on research infrastructure: *“Undoubtedly one of the best universities in Scandinavia for a multitude of technical programs. High focus on advanced engineering research with active student clubs and organizations. 24 hour access to labs and many other campus facilities.”* (5-star, 6 years ago). A more recent review from Vin Chaudhari, who completed an MSc in Mobility, simply called it *“best university in its class.”* (5-star, 3 months ago). Meanwhile, a 4-star review by Kennet Ugochukwu Ezeala noted: *“It was fun driving around the university & the lectures were interesting.”* (4-star, 5 months ago).

These reviews point to a campus that is both academically demanding and socially lively. When choosing housing near Chalmers, proximity to facilities that support late-night lab work or collaborative projects matters – 24-hour lab access and active student clubs suggest you may want a home that lets you stay on campus without long commutes.

## Understanding the City: Gothenburg Context

Chalmers is located at Chalmersplatsen 1, in the Johanneberg district of Gothenburg (Göteborg), Sweden. Gothenburg is the country’s second-largest city, with a population of roughly 600,000 in the urban area. It is a major port and industrial hub, home to Volvo, SKF, and AstraZeneca, which means Chalmers graduates often find internships and jobs nearby. The city has a compact, walkable core and an efficient public transport network of trams, buses, and ferries operated by Västtrafik.

Student housing in Gothenburg is managed by several actors, including Chalmers Studentbostäder (the university’s own housing foundation), SGS Studentbostäder, and private landlords. The Johanneberg campus sits next to the student neighbourhood of **Guldheden** and is close to **Korsvägen**, a major transport node. Because the university is integrated into the city, many students live anywhere from a 10-minute walk to a 30-minute tram ride away.

## Budget Bands for Student Housing (2026–2027)

All prices below are indicative – confirm with the operator. Rents in Gothenburg vary by location, size, and contract type (corridor room, studio, or shared apartment). Here are approximate monthly bands for a single student (using SEK, the Swedish krona; exchange rates vary).

- **Budget (SEK 4,000 – 5,500 per month)** – Corridor rooms or small shared flats in areas such as Guldheden, Johanneberg, or Lindholmen. These are typically 12–18 m² with shared kitchen and bathroom. Chalmers Studentbostäder and SGS Studentbostäder offer many such rooms. Monthly rent in this band may include water, electricity, and internet, but check each contract.
- **Mid-range (SEK 6,000 – 8,500 per month)** – Studios or one-room apartments of 20–30 m² with private kitchen and bathroom. Common in buildings near Korsvägen, Olskroken, or along tram lines 2, 7, or 13. Private landlords and larger student housing companies provide these. Rent sometimes excludes electricity (add roughly SEK 300–500/month).
- **Premium (SEK 9,000 – 12,000+ per month)** – Larger studios, one-bedroom flats, or newly built apartments close to the campus or in central Gothenburg (e.g., Vasastaden, Linnéstaden). These often have modern furnishings, own laundry, and balcony. Waitlists for such units can be long – many require membership in a housing queue for 6–24 months.

*Note: Many student contracts require a deposit of 1–2 months’ rent. First-month rent is paid upfront.*

## Walk vs. Transit: Where to Live

Chalmers’ main campus (Johanneberg) is roughly 2–3 km south of Gothenburg’s city centre (Central Station). The decision between walking and using public transport largely depends on your budget and tolerance for commuting in Sweden’s winter weather.

- **Walking distance (0–15 minutes)** – Neighbourhoods immediately around campus: Guldheden (north-west of campus, a 5–10 min walk up a hill), Johanneberg proper, and the area around Chalmersplatsen itself. These areas have the highest concentration of student housing (corridor rooms and studios). Walking avoids tram costs (a monthly student travel pass is around SEK 600–800) and is reliable, but rent is often slightly higher due to location.
- **Short tram ride (10–20 minutes)** – Areas along tram lines 2, 7, and 13: Korsvägen (one tram stop), as well as Olskroken, Sahlgrenska, and parts of Majorna. A single tram journey takes roughly 15–20 minutes including waiting. Rent tends to be 10–15% cheaper than in the campus zone. You will need a Västtrafik card or app, but the pass cost is predictable.
- **Longer commute (20–35 minutes)** – Neighbourhoods like Frölunda, Angered, or Hisingen (Lindholmen being closer to the Chalmers campus on Hisingen, but note that the main campus is still Johanneberg; Lindholmen hosts some Chalmers activities). Rents drop further, but you sacrifice time and flexibility. Many students on tighter budgets choose this option, especially if they belong to a housing queue for a low-cost corridor room.

*Trade-off summary:* Walking saves time and transport fees but costs more in rent. Taking the tram saves money if you can find a cheaper flat further out, but adds about 200–600 SEK per month for the travel pass.

## A Shortlist Logic for Choosing Your Residence

When narrowing down housing options near Chalmers, consider the following factors in order of priority, using only the information available about the university (no specific residence data given). Generalise based on Gothenburg’s typical housing market.

1. **Proximity to 24-hour campus facilities** – Several reviews highlight that labs and other facilities are accessible around the clock. If you expect to work late, pick a residence within a 15-minute walk or a direct tram line to Chalmersplatsen. Corridor rooms in Guldheden or Johanneberg are logical first choices.
2. **Housing queue position** – For Chalmers Studentbostäder and SGS, you register directly and earn queue days. If your queue time is short (0–6 months), you will likely be offered a corridor room in a less central area like Guldheden or Lindholmen. If you have more than 12 months in the queue, you may get a studio in the premium band closer to campus.
3. **Contract length and flexibility** – Most student housing contracts run from 1 August to 31 July. If you arrive mid-year (e.g., January), private sublets or short-term contracts are more common. Chalmers Studentbostäder sometimes offers semester-long contracts (4–5 months) for exchange students. Confirm the duration with the operator at the time of booking.
4. **Noise and lifestyle** – The campus area (Johanneberg) is relatively quiet, but some blocks near Chalmersplatsen have bars and restaurants. If you prefer a quieter environment, look at Guldheden or the area around Sahlgrenska Hospital (mostly residential). If you want a social scene, consider central Vasastaden (15 min by tram).
5. **Type of room** – If your programme requires collaboration (e.g., group projects, lab work), a corridor room with shared kitchen can help you meet flatmates from different programmes. If you need quiet study space, a studio or apartment with separate rooms is better – but at a higher cost.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is it possible to find housing near Chalmers for less than SEK 5,000 per month in 2026?**  
Yes, but very limited. Corridor rooms offered by Chalmers Studentbostäder and SGS Studentbostäder in Guldheden or Johanneberg can start around SEK 4,000–4,500 per month (indicative – confirm with the operator). These rooms usually have shared kitchen and bathroom. You typically need to have built queue days or get a short-term contract. Most contracts begin in August, so plan your arrival accordingly.

**Q: Should I bring a bicycle?**  
Gothenburg has dedicated cycle paths, and many students bike to campus – the distance from Guldheden or Majorna is about 2–4 km, a 10–20 minute ride. However, winter snow and ice can make cycling difficult. A combined tram/bus pass is a reliable alternative. If you live within a 15-minute walk, you may not need a bike at all. Bicycle theft is common; invest in a good lock and consider insurance.

**Q: How long do I need to wait in the housing queue to get a studio near campus?**  
For Chalmers Studentbostäder, wait times vary by semester. Studios in high-demand areas like Johanneberg or Guldheden can require 12–24 months of queue days. Newer buildings (e.g., Lindholmen) may have shorter queues – around 6–12 months. SGS Studentbostäder operates on a similar system. For a corridor room, queue days of 0–6 months are often sufficient. Always apply early: many students join the queue before receiving their admission letter.
