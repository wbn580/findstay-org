## What campus reviews reveal about Kiel University

Before searching for housing, it helps to understand what current and prospective students say about Christian-Albrechts-Universität zu Kiel. The university holds a QS World University rank of 618 and a Google rating of 4.0 from 494 reviews. While the overall rating is decent, the written reviews paint a mixed picture – especially around the admissions process and student support.

One reviewer, Melone 01, gave a 1-star rating and stated: *“I’m not studying there yet, but it’s worse than I thought. Most people say that you have to manage everything by yourself with some help, like preliminary meetings. I think that’s generally fine, but they mostly talk about irrelevant things and skip the important topics.”* This suggests that self‑organisation and proactive information gathering are essential – a trait that also applies to finding accommodation in Kiel.

Another 1‑star review by Daizy Doctor criticised the admissions committee for the Master’s in Medical Life Sciences, specifically the interview with a coordinator: *“She asked me to give a brief introduction. Thereafter, she asked me questions about course structure, Kiel Hi…”* (the comment cuts off). Similarly, Daxeesh Jariwala wrote: *“In my experience with the admissions process at the University of Kiel, particularly for the Master’s program in Medical Life Sciences, the focus seems to prioritize political and historical knowledge over subject expertise.”* These comments indicate that some applicants find the process challenging. For housing, this means you should start your apartment search early and independently, without relying on the university to hold your hand.

On the positive side, Min Basnet gave 4 stars and described the institution as: *“A good university in the north of Germany. Wonderful academic environment. May be one of the best for marine geosciences. I feel the faculties are limited, 4 stars only for that. Otherwise, I am happy.”* Kiel’s strength in marine sciences is a draw for many students, and a “wonderful academic environment” often correlates with a pleasant living experience in the city.

These reviews reveal that while the academic side has its attractions, students need to be self‑reliant. The same approach is critical when securing housing: research, flexibility, and early action are key.

## Kiel city context for student housing

Kiel is the capital of Schleswig‑Holstein, located on the Baltic Sea coast. It is a mid‑sized city (about 250,000 inhabitants) with a strong maritime character. The university’s main campus is at Christian‑Albrechts‑Platz 4, right in the city centre, close to the old town and Kiel Fjord.

The city has a well‑developed public transport network of buses and ferries, but many students walk or cycle because distances are short. The student population is around 27,000, which creates a lively atmosphere but also competition for affordable rooms. Most accommodations are concentrated in neighbourhoods such as Südfriedhof, Wik, and Ravensberg, as well as the central area around the Hauptbahnhof.

Housing options include:

- Student dormitories (Studentenwerk SH runs several halls)
- Private shared flats (WGs – Wohngemeinschaften)
- Studio apartments (Ein‑Zimmer‑Wohnungen)

Because the university does not guarantee housing for all applicants (and the reviews suggest limited support), you should plan to search on multiple channels: WG‑Gesucht, local Facebook groups, and the Studentenwerk website. Notice periods are often short, so you can find rooms a few weeks before moving in, but for semester starts (October and April) demand is highest.

## Budget bands for accommodation in Kiel

![Christian-Albrechts-Universität zu Kiel](https://img.unistay.cn/housing/p/amber-1080214.jpg)

Rental prices in Kiel are lower than in Hamburg, Berlin, or Munich, but have risen in recent years. The following bands are typical for the 2026/2027 academic year – all are indicative and must be confirmed with the operator before signing any contract.

**Budget category – low (€250 – €380 per month, all‑inclusive)**

- Student dormitory rooms (single room in a shared flat or corridor)
- Basic private rooms in shared flats in less central neighbourhoods
- Often includes electricity, heating, water, and internet

**Budget category – mid (€380 – €500 per month, warm rent)**

- Larger private rooms in WGs closer to campus
- Studio apartments in buildings with shared facilities
- Some modern dorms with private bathroom and kitchenette

**Budget category – upper (€500 – €700+ per month)**

- One‑bedroom apartments in the city centre or by the waterfront
- High‑spec studios in newly built complexes
- Accommodation with parking, balcony, or additional services

Studentenwerk dormitories are the cheapest option, typically €270–€370. Waiting lists can be several months long, so apply as soon as you receive your admission letter. Private flats listed on WG‑Gesucht or local real‑estate portals often require a deposit (usually two to three months’ warm rent) and a proof of income or a guarantor.

Remember: all prices listed here are indicative. Always confirm the exact rent and inclusions with the housing provider before committing.

## Walk vs transit trade-off for students

Kiel is compact. The main university campus lies at Christian‑Albrechts‑Platz, about a 10‑minute walk from the Hauptbahnhof (main train station). Many lecture halls, the library, and the canteen are within a 15‑minute walking radius of the city centre.

**Walking distance (0–2 km from campus)**

- Neighbourhoods: Altstadt, Vorstadt, Düsternbrook (south‑east of the fjord)
- Avg. commute: 5–15 minutes on foot
- Rent: tends to be higher for private studios; dormitories are rarer in the immediate centre
- Advantage: no public transport cost; easy to go home between lectures

**Cycling distance (2–5 km)**

- Neighbourhoods: Ravensberg, Wik, Südfriedhof
- Avg. commute: 10–25 minutes by bike
- Rent: moderate; many WGs available
- Advantage: wide cycle lanes; you can cover more affordable areas without a bus ticket

**Transit‑dependent (5–8 km)**

- Areas: Kiel‑Süd, Mettenhof, Gaarden
- Avg. commute: 20–35 minutes by bus (MVG lines)
- Rent: lower per square metre
- Trade‑off: a semester ticket (included in the student fee) covers unlimited bus, train, and ferry travel in the region, so transit costs are zero once you enrol. However, late‑evening courses may require careful planning if bus frequency drops.

The trade‑off is clear: paying more for a central location saves time and avoids dependence on schedules. Living further out lowers rent but adds 30–40 minutes round‑trip daily. Most international students report that a location within 3 km of campus is ideal – walkable or a short bike ride, without needing a bus.

## Shortlist logic for choosing accommodation

When narrowing down your housing options, base your decision on these factors, prioritised by your own preferences:

1. **Budget** – Determine your maximum warm rent (including utilities). As shown above, dormitory rooms are the cheapest but hardest to get. Private shared flats are the most common for students. Do not exceed 40% of your monthly income/scholarship.
2. **Distance to campus** – Check the exact address on Google Maps. A 15‑minute walk is perfect; a 25‑minute bike ride is fine; a 30‑minute bus ride might be acceptable if you prefer a quieter neighbourhood.
3. **Contract duration and flexibility** – Dormitories usually require a minimum stay of one semester; private flats often have a minimum of 6–12 months. For exchange or shorter master’s programmes, look for temporary sublets (Zwischenmiete).
4. **Amenities** – Does the room have internet access? Is a washing machine available in the building? Are kitchen utensils provided? Read the listing carefully.
5. **Flatmates** – In WG‑situations, meet the current residents (via video call) if possible. A compatible household reduces stress. Reviews of specific buildings are rare, but you can gauge from the listing description.
6. **Early action** – Given the negative feedback about university support, do not wait for the Studentenwerk to allocate you a room. Apply to several dormitories simultaneously and also search private listings 6–8 weeks before your arrival.
7. **Verification** – Scams exist. Never transfer money without seeing the flat (or a video tour) and signing a contract. Use reputable platforms and ask for references if unsure.

Applying this logic reduces the risk of ending up in a substandard place. As a rule, aim for a room that is in a safe, well‑connected area and costs within your budget. If you are still uncertain, the student councils (Fachschaften) sometimes offer housing advice – ask them after enrolment.

## FAQ

### When should I start looking for accommodation in Kiel?

Begin your search as soon as you receive your admission letter – ideally 6–8 weeks before your intended move‑in. For the winter semester (starting October), rooms are listed from August onwards. For the summer semester (starting April), listings appear from February. Dormitory applications typically open earlier; check the Studentenwerk Schleswig‑Holstein website for exact dates.

### Is it possible to get a student dormitory room without a German bank account?

Yes, but you will need a German bank account to pay the rent after signing the contract. Most dormitories accept international transfers during the application phase, but a German account (e.g., N26, Deutsche Bank) simplifies ongoing payments. Some private landlords also require a SCHUFA credit report – you can obtain this after registering your address in Germany.

### What documents are usually required when renting a private room in Kiel?

Landlords typically ask for:

- Passport or ID
- Student enrolment certificate (Immatrikulationsbescheinigung)
- Proof of income or a parent’s letter of guarantee (Verpflichtungserklärung)
- A completed Self‑disclosure form (Selbstauskunft)
- Deposit (Kaution) of two to three months’ cold rent – transfer to a separate savings account (Mietkautionskonto).

If you are not yet enrolled, some landlords accept a blocked account statement or a scholarship confirmation.
