## Campus Location and London Accommodation Overview

City St George’s, University of London main campus is located at Northampton Square, slightly north of central London, postcode EC1V 0HB, administratively in the London Borough of Islington. It is within walking distance of Angel and Barbican tube stations. This area is a traditional academic and creative intersection in London, with several museums, parks, and cafes nearby, and is within a 20-minute tube journey from both the City of London and the West End.

As a comprehensive university ranked 310th in the QS World University Rankings, City St George’s has a strong reputation in engineering, business, law, and health sciences. With a high proportion of international students, the area around the campus and student hubs has a mature rental market. According to Google Maps ratings (4.2 from 536 reviews), the campus facilities and student services are generally well received.

Typical accommodation price ranges for students in London are between £130 and £400+ per week, depending on room type (ensuite/studio), location, and whether bills are included. City St George’s sits at the border of Zones 1 and 2, so private apartments and university halls within walking distance tend to be more expensive, while more economical options are available with a 30-40 minute commute via public transport.

## Walking vs Public Transport Trade-off

City St George’s campus is located on Northampton Square. The nearest tube stations are **Angel** (Northern Line) and **Barbican** (Circle, Hammersmith & City, Metropolitan Lines), both about 5-8 minutes’ walk away. This means:

- **10-15 minute walking radius**: Covers dining and shopping areas such as Islington High Street and Exmouth Market, as well as several public green spaces and libraries. Suitable for students willing to pay higher rent in exchange for time. Weekly accommodation costs in this area are typically £250 or more.
- **15-20 minute cycling radius**: Covers areas such as Hackney, Bethnal Green, and King’s Cross. London has extensive cycle lanes and convenient bike-sharing schemes. Cycling-commute accommodation options are about 30% more numerous than walking-distance options, and prices can drop to £180-£220.
- **30-40 minute tube commute radius**: Mainly covers outer Zone 2 and Zone 3 areas such as Brent Cross, Hammersmith, and Kingston upon Thames. Although one-way travel time increases, these areas have a larger supply of student apartments and shared houses, with prices as low as £130-£160. On a weekly basis, the annual rent difference could reach £3,000-£5,000.

Public transport costs also need to be considered: Transport for London’s 18+ Student Oyster card offers monthly travel card discounts (in 2026, a Zone 1-2 monthly travel card costs about £90, and Zone 1-3 about £110). If commuting 5 days a week, the monthly transport cost for Zone 2-3 can offset part of the accommodation price difference.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When is the best time to start looking for accommodation?

Student accommodation in London typically moves in in the 9th month (September) of each year, but popular properties open for reservation as early as six months in advance (December of the previous year to March of the current year). If you plan to move in in the 9th month (September) 2026, it is recommended to browse options and pay a deposit between January and March 2026. If you miss the early booking period, it is still possible to find a spot from sublets or newly released properties between June and August, but the range of choices will be narrower.

### Are there supermarkets or clinics within walking distance of the school?

Yes. Within a 5-minute walk from Northampton Square, there are Sainsbury’s Local, Tesco Express, and the large supermarket chain Co-op. Near Angel station, there are two large Waitrose and M&S Foodhall. Students can register for healthcare at the university health centre or at the nearest Clerkenwell Medical Practice, also within walking distance.

### Do I need to pay Council Tax separately?

Full-time students (with at least 21 hours of study per week per course) can apply for a Council Tax exemption from the local council. After enrolment, the university will provide a Student Status Letter, which you can use for the application. Note that if you share a property with non-students, the full property may not be exempt; you need to confirm the apportionment method with the landlord or housemates.
