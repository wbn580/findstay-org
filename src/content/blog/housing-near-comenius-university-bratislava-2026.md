## What campus reviews reveal

Comenius University in Bratislava is the largest and oldest university in Slovakia, located at Šafárikovo námestie 6 in the historic centre of the capital. With a QS World University Ranking of 684 and a Google rating of 4.6 based on 260 reviews, it holds a strong reputation among students, academics, and visitors. Reviews consistently highlight its size, academic breadth, and architectural character.

One reviewer, Dariusz Prokopowicz, calls it “the undisputed matriarch of Slovak higher education, where a venerable centennial tradition seamlessly integrates with contemporary research excellence.” This reflects both the historical prestige and the modern ambitions of the institution. Another reviewer, W. B., describes it as “the best and biggest university in Slovakia,” noting that it “offers education in wide range of fields, such as natural sciences, medicine, law, philosophy, physics, mathematics, humanities and others.” Lenka M echoes the same wording, adding that it “has many beautiful historical buildings and modern buildings all around Bratislava.” Emilian Zaharinov provides a scale reference: “the largest university in Slovakia with over 25K students and 2K faculty members.”

These reviews paint a picture of a large, multi‑faculty university spread across several campuses in Bratislava. The main address sits in the trendy Staré Mesto (Old Town) district, but faculties in natural sciences, medicine, and other fields are located in different parts of the city. This means housing decisions depend heavily on where a student’s lectures and labs actually take place. Knowing that the university is both central and decentralised is the first step to choosing accommodation.

## City context: Bratislava

Bratislava is the capital and largest city of Slovakia, straddling the Danube River and bordering Austria and Hungary. It has a population of roughly 450,000, making it a compact capital compared to cities like Vienna or Prague. The historic centre is walkable and packed with cafes, shops, and cultural venues. Public transport consists of trams, buses, and trolleybuses, all operated under a single integrated fare system. A monthly student pass typically costs around €30–40 (indicative — confirm with the operator). The city is generally safe, and English is widely spoken among younger residents and service staff.

Student life revolves around the Old Town and the “Palisády” area near the university’s main building. However, students also frequent the Petržalka district (south of the Danube) and the residential zones around Mlynská dolina, where several science and technology faculties are located. The cost of living in Bratislava is moderate by European standards, though rental prices have risen in recent years. A single room in a shared flat may range from €300 to €500 per month, while a studio apartment can go from €500 to €700 (indicative — confirm with the operator). Because rents are higher near the centre, many students choose to live a bit farther out and rely on public transport.

## Walk vs transit trade‑off

Comenius University is not a single campus — it consists of multiple faculties scattered across Bratislava. The main building at Šafárikovo námestie 6 is in the heart of Staré Mesto, within easy walking distance of the city’s main train station (Bratislava hlavná stanica, about 15 minutes on foot) and the pedestrian zone. Students whose lectures are held here can live anywhere in the centre or along tram lines 1, 2, 3, 4, and 9.

However, the Faculty of Natural Sciences, the Faculty of Medicine, and parts of the Faculty of Mathematics, Physics and Informatics are located in the Mlynská dolina area, about 3–4 km north‑west of the centre. A student living in Staré Mesto would need to take tram line 4 or 9 (about 15–20 minutes) or bus 30/31. Walking is feasible (about 30–40 minutes) but not convenient for a daily commute, especially in winter.

Similarly, the Jessenius Faculty of Medicine is in Martin (a separate city), and some other faculties have outposts in the suburbs. Therefore, the walk vs transit decision should be based on the specific faculty you attend.

- **Walk** works well if your faculty is in Staré Mesto or Rača (Faculty of Pharmacy). Rentals within a 15‑minute walk of the main building tend to be more expensive, but you save on transport costs and time.
- **Transit** is the better option if you study in Mlynská dolina or other outlying areas. Monthly transport passes are inexpensive for students, and the tram/bus network is reliable. Living near a tram stop or a bus hub (e.g., Bajkalská, Trnavské mýto) reduces commute times.

A practical middle ground is to live along a tram line that runs between the centre and your faculty. Many students rent apartments near Šafárikovo námestie, Račianska, or Mlynské nivy for flexibility.

## Shortlist logic (general guidelines)

Since no specific nearby residences were provided in the dataset, the following approach can help you create your own shortlist:

1. **Identify your main faculty location.** Check your department’s address. If it is in Staré Mesto, prioritize accommodation within walking distance or along tram lines 1–9. If it is in Mlynská dolina, focus on districts such as Mlynská dolina itself, Koliba, or Rača (via tram 4).
2. **Set a budget.** Determine the maximum rent you can afford, including utilities. Use the budget bands above as a starting point, then filter listings accordingly. Always treat online prices as indicative — confirm with the operator.
3. **Check transport links.** Google Maps or the transit authority’s journey planner can show you commute times. Look for places within 30 minutes door‑to‑door.
4. **Read reviews.** If the accommodation provider has online reviews, read them for mentions of noise, cleanliness, landlord communication, and actual utility costs. Beware of overly positive or vague reviews.
5. **Visit or request a video tour.** If possible, see the room or apartment in person or through a live video call. Photos can be misleading.
6. **Ask about contract terms.** Check minimum stay, deposit (usually one month’s rent), notice period, and whether bills are included. “Indicative” prices often change after contract signature — ask for a final written offer.
7. **Consider sharing.** Sharing a flat with other Comenius University students can reduce costs and help you settle in. University‑run dormitories are another option; contact the university’s accommodation office for availability and indicative pricing.

By following these steps, you can assemble a shortlist of potential homes without needing a predefined list of residences. The key is to balance cost, distance, and personal preferences, always confirming all figures with the operator.
