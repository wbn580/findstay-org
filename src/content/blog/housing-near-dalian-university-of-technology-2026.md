## Housing Near Dalian University of Technology (DUT): A Practical Guide

Dalian University of Technology (DUT), ranked 482 (QS 2025), sits in the Ganjingzi district of Dalian, Liaoning Province. On Google, it holds a 4.5‑star rating from 67 reviews. One reviewer, Liying Li, commented: “Love our university for its top-tier resources, facilities, and professors. A great place to start my academic career in environmental science.” Another review by Shaoping Sun noted: “Situated in the coastal city of Dalian, Dalian University of Technology (DUT) has established itself as a premier institution for science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM) education in China. Established in 1949, DUT has a rich history of academic excellence and innovation…”

Living close to campus can save time and commuting cost, but Dalian’s rental market varies widely by distance, property type, and season. This guide focuses on what students typically pay in 2026–2027, how to choose between walking and transit, and how to shortlist options responsibly.

### Campus Location and Local Context

DUT’s main campus is at 2 Linggong Road, Ganjingzi District. The area is largely educational and residential, with a mix of older apartment blocks and newer purpose-built student housing. Dalian is a coastal city in northeast China, with a temperate climate: cold winters (down to −10 °C in January) and warm summers (around 28 °C in July). Public transport includes buses and a metro line; the nearest metro station to DUT is probably **DUT East Gate** (Line 1) or **Hekou** (Line 1), but exact distances vary.

Living in Ganjingzi district keeps you close to campus and local amenities (small supermarkets, eateries, and convenience stores). The city centre (Zhongshan or Xigang districts) is about 30–40 minutes by metro or bus. Rent in Ganjingzi is generally lower than in the city centre, making it a practical choice for budget-conscious students.

### Budget Bands for 2026–2027

All prices below are **indicative — confirm with the operator** (landlord, agent, or housing provider). Rates may shift with market changes, lease terms, and utilities.

- **Low budget (typically shared rooms or university dormitories):**  
  On‑campus dormitory fees for international students at public Chinese universities often range from **500–1,200 RMB per month** (2026–2027 indicative). These include basic furniture, shared bathroom/kitchen, and sometimes utilities. Note: dorm availability is limited and allocated by the university; not all students qualify. Off‑campus shared flats near DUT can cost **800–1,500 RMB per month per person** (indicative) for a room in a 3‑ to 4‑bedroom apartment, often split among tenants.
- **Mid budget (private studio or one-bedroom apartment):**  
  A compact studio or one‑bedroom apartment within 15 minutes’ walk of campus usually runs **2,000–3,500 RMB per month** (indicative). This includes a separate bathroom and kitchenette. Utilities (electricity, water, internet, heating) add roughly 200–500 RMB monthly depending on season.
- **Upper budget (larger apartment or prime location):**  
  A spacious two‑bedroom apartment near DUT or in a newer residential complex (e.g., with a gym, security, and parking) may cost **3,500–5,500 RMB per month** (indicative). Some furnished options for international students or visiting scholars fall into this bracket.

Important: Many lease contracts require a deposit (usually one month’s rent) and payment for the full semester or year. Some agents also charge a service fee of 30–50% of one month’s rent — ask upfront.

### Walk vs. Transit: The Trade-Off

Deciding whether to live within walking distance or accept a longer commute involves several factors:

- **Time**: Walking to the main teaching buildings from a flat on Linggong Road can take 5–15 minutes. A 30‑minute bus or metro ride into the city centre may free up cheaper rent farther away.
- **Cost**: Rent per square metre within a 10‑minute walk of DUT is typically 10–25% higher than in areas 2–3 km away. However, the daily transit fare (bus ~1–2 RMB, metro ~2–5 RMB per trip) adds up to 60–150 RMB per month.
- **Convenience**: If you have early morning labs or a heavy course load, being near campus reduces stress. For students who value city nightlife, chain stores, or dining variety, living closer to the centre may be worth the commute.
- **Weather**: Dalian’s winters are cold and often windy. A 15‑minute walk in January can be uncomfortable, while a bus or metro ride keeps you warm. Conversely, summer heat and humidity may make a short walk manageable but a longer one less pleasant.

A common compromise: choose a flat within 20 minutes’ walk or a 10‑minute bus ride — you get the benefit of lower rent without spending more than 30 minutes each way.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I live in DUT’s on-campus dormitory as an international student?**  
Yes, DUT usually offers on-campus accommodation for international students, but spaces are limited. Apply through the International Student Office after receiving your admission. Dorm fees vary by room type (double, triple, suite) and are generally between 500 and 1,200 RMB per month (indicative, confirm with the university). You may also need to pay a room deposit and follow dorm rules (curfew, guest policy). If you cannot get a dorm room, off-campus rentals are the alternative.

**Q: What documents do I need to rent a private apartment off campus?**  
Typically you need your passport, visa (student visa or residence permit), a copy of your DUT admission letter, and sometimes a guarantor (Chinese citizen or a deposit). Landlords may ask for a property ownership certificate (fángchǎn zhèng) to prove they are the owner. Never rent without seeing the physical flat or having someone you trust inspect it. Avoid paying cash — use bank transfer or Alipay and keep receipts.

**Q: Are there any areas near DUT I should avoid?**  
Ganjingzi district is generally safe. The area immediately around the campus is student‑friendly with plenty of small restaurants and shops. As with any city, use common sense: avoid poorly lit alleys late at night, and be cautious with strangers offering “too good to be true” deals. Check the reputation of specific apartment buildings by asking local students or looking for recent online reviews. No major high-crime zones have been reported near DUT, but always trust your instincts.
