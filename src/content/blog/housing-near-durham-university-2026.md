It is also a world-leading university, consistently ranked in the top 100 globally. Collegiate System: The university’s unique colleg…”*

Another student, Robert Dinca, emphasised the learning environment:

> *“Durham University offers an exceptional academic experience in a stunning historic setting. The teaching is high-quality, the support from staff is excellent, and the college system creates a strong sense of community. An inspiring place to study and grow.”*

Duncan Rogers, a parent reviewer, offered a comparative perspective:

> *“One of the top 3 unis in 2026 - better than Oxbridge in so many levels. No stress, high level teaching, longer terms, fantastic safe city and lots to do. My daughter loves it and has made long live friends. Teachers are great, lots of support all the time.”*

These comments reflect a strong emphasis on community support and personal well-being, factors that can influence housing decisions. The collegiate system means many first-year students live in college-owned accommodation, but returning students and postgraduates typically seek private rentals near the university or in the city centre.

## City Context: Durham as a Student Hub

Durham is a compact cathedral city in northeast England, with a population of around 50,000, including a large student body. The university’s main campus sits on a peninsula formed by the River Wear, with the historic cathedral and castle occupying the centre. The city is largely pedestrian-friendly and known for its low crime rate — a point mentioned in reviews as “fantastic safe city.”

The university address is The Palatine Centre, Stockton Rd, Durham DH1 3LE. Most academic buildings, libraries, and colleges cluster within a 15–20 minute walk of this central area. The closest train station, Durham railway station, is about a 10-minute walk from the main campus, with direct services to London (around 3 hours), Edinburgh (2 hours), and Newcastle (15 minutes).

Local amenities include supermarkets, cafes, restaurants, and a weekly market on the marketplace. The city lacks the scale of larger UK student cities, which means most daily needs are within walking distance. However, longer commutes by bus or car are possible for housing in surrounding villages.

## Budget Bands for Student Housing

![Durham University](https://img.unistay.net/housing/p/uh-s01524977.jpg)

Students looking for housing near Durham University should expect a range of price points, though exact figures depend on property type, contract length, and location. Because rents can shift from year to year, all estimates below are **indicative — confirm with the operator** before signing any agreement.

- **College accommodation (catered or self-catered):** Usually covers utilities and contents insurance. Weekly costs vary by college and room type. These options are typically offered to first-year undergraduates.
- **Private shared houses (3–5 bedrooms):** Common for second-year and third-year students. Monthly rents per person generally fall into a mid-range bracket. Deposits and utility bills are separate.
- **Studio or one-bedroom flats:** Suitable for postgraduates, staff, or students who prefer privacy. These command higher monthly rents and are less abundant than shared houses.
- **Halls of residence (private providers):** Some purpose-built student accommodation (PBSA) is available near the city centre or along the river. Rates are usually all-inclusive.

Because Durham is small, price differences between walking-distance and transit-accessible areas are often modest. Students should always check what is included (utility caps, internet, contents insurance) and whether a guarantor is required.

## Walk vs Transit Trade-Off

The majority of student housing in Durham is within a 30-minute walk of the main campus. Living close to the university reduces travel time and costs but often means paying a premium for convenience. Key trade-offs include:

- **Walking zone (0–20 minutes from the Palatine Centre):** Includes the city centre, Gilesgate, Neville’s Cross, and parts of Elvet. These areas offer easy access to shops and nightlife but can be noisier. Rents here are typically higher per room.
- **Short bus or bike zone (20–40 minutes):** Areas such as Framwellgate Moor, Belmont, and the outskirts of Durham. Buses run regularly to the city centre. Bike parking is available on campus. Rents may be slightly lower, and properties often have off-street parking or gardens.
- **Longer commute (40+ minutes by bus or car):** Villages like Brandon, Bowburn, or Coxhoe. This option suits students with access to a car or those who prefer a quieter environment. Bus passes cost approximately £300–£400 per year (indicative — confirm with operator). Parking on campus can be limited and permit costs apply.

For most students, the walk vs transit decision comes down to budget and lifestyle. The reviews consistently note that Durham feels safe, so walking home at night is generally comfortable. Many students choose to live within a 10–15 minute walk during their first year to maximise social opportunities, then move slightly farther out in later years to reduce rent.

## Shortlist Logic Using Given Nearby Residences

This guide does not have a single list of pre-selected properties, so the shortlisting logic below can be applied to any search for housing near Durham University. Based on the university’s characteristics and review data, students should consider the following criteria:

1. **College affiliation (if applicable):** Collegiate system reviews show that “the college system creates a strong sense of community.” If you are a new undergraduate, your college may offer accommodation for year one. Confirm the location of your college – some are within the city centre, others on the outskirts.
2. **Walking distance to your faculty:** The main campus is on Stockton Road, but some departments (e.g., business, engineering) are located at Mountjoy or the Science Site. Check your faculty address before shortlisting.
3. **Safety and neighbourhood character:** Multiple reviews praise the city’s safety. When shortlisting, check street lighting condition and pedestrian routes at night. Review platforms often include specific comments about areas.
4. **All-inclusive vs. bills separate:** Most student houses in Durham charge rent plus separate utilities. PBSA often includes everything. Calculate total monthly costs before comparing.
5. **Bus route availability:** If you choose a property outside the core walking zone, confirm that at least one bus route runs within 5 minutes of the door and that services run late on weeknights.
6. **Contract flexibility:** Some landlords offer 51-week or 12-month contracts. If you leave for summer break, a shorter 9–10 month lease may be more economical. Check early exit clauses.
7. **Tenant guarantee requirements:** International students without a UK guarantor may need to pay a larger deposit or use a guarantee scheme. Confirm this with the operator before signing.
8. **Reviews from previous tenants:** Look for comments on the condition of the property, responsiveness of the landlord, and billing transparency. A rating of 4.5 out of 5 from 642 reviews for the university does not apply to individual landlords, but it indicates that the student population holds high standards.

Using these filters, you can create a shortlist of 5–10 properties. Visit them in person or on a video tour before committing. Durham’s rental market moves quickly, especially during the summer months, so begin searching by early spring for a September start.

## FAQ

**1. Is college accommodation included in tuition fees?**

No. College accommodation is charged separately from tuition. Prices vary by college and room standard, and details are published on each college’s website. Indicative rates are typically available from the university’s accommodation office — confirm with the operator before applying.

**2. What are the most popular residential areas for students?**  
According to student forums, the most common areas are Gilesgate, Neville’s Cross, Elvet, and the city centre itself. All are within a 15–20 minute walk of the main campus. For lower rents, some students look at Framwellgate Moor or Belmont, which are short bus rides away.

**3. Should I rent a house or purpose-built student accommodation (PBSA)?**  
This depends on your budget and preference for community. Shared houses tend to be cheaper per person and offer more independence, but often require separate utility bills and a group of housemates. PBSA usually includes everything (bills, internet, contents insurance) and provides study spaces and common rooms, but the monthly rent is higher. Always compare the total cost and read the contract carefully — confirm with the operator what is included.
