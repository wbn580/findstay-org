### About Tübingen

Tübingen is a classic German university town with a population of around 90,000, of whom roughly one-third are students. The historic centre – dominated by the famous Hölderlin tower, cobbled streets, and half-timbered houses – is built around the Neckar River. The main university campus (Geschwister-Scholl-Platz) sits at the heart of the old town, making it one of the most walkable campuses in Germany.

The city’s economy is dominated by the university, several research institutes, and a growing biotech sector. This means housing demand is consistently high, especially at the start of each semester. Rental prices have risen over the past decade, but compared to larger German cities like Munich or Frankfurt, Tübingen remains more affordable.

Public transport is limited to buses (there is no tram or metro); a single ticket in 2026 costs around €2.80 (indicative – confirm with operator). Many students rely on bicycles, which are well accommodated with bike lanes and racks throughout the city.

- **Student halls (Studentenwerk-type accommodation):** Typically range from €350 to €550 per month, including heating, water, and internet. Waiting lists are common; apply as early as possible (often 6 months before semester start).
- **Private shared flats (WG):** Monthly costs for a room in a shared apartment usually fall between €400 and €650, depending on size and location. Bills are often separate and can add €50–€100.
- **Studio or one-bedroom apartment:** Expect €600–€900 per month. These are rarer and usually found outside the Altstadt.
- **Temporary furnished rooms (short-term stay):** Can cost €500–€800 per month; popular with Erasmus and exchange students.

**Key rule:** Never transfer money without a signed contract. Use trusted platforms – the university’s housing office often maintains a list of verified offers.

### Walking vs. Public Transport Trade-Off

The university’s main campus is located at Geschwister-Scholl-Platz, which is inside the old town. This makes it extremely convenient for anyone living in the central districts (Altstadt, Südstadt, Lustnau).

- **Walking distance under 10 minutes:** Altstadt and parts of Lustnau. Rent is generally at the upper end of the budget bands (€500+ for a WG room). No transport cost, but noise from bars and tourists can be a factor.
- **Walking distance 10–20 minutes:** Südstadt, Französisches Viertel, and Wanne. More residential and quieter. Rent slightly lower (€350–€500). Still walkable but you might appreciate a bike or bus for rainy days.
- **Transit-dependent (bus ride 10–20 minutes):** Districts like Derendingen, Pfrondorf, or Hirschau. Rent can drop to €300–€450 for a WG room. The bus network is reliable but limited on weekends and evenings. A semester ticket (about €170 per semester, indicative) gives unlimited bus travel within the city.

**Tip:** A bicycle is the most efficient option for distances up to 4 km, and Tübingen’s compact size means most of the city falls within that radius. Bike storage should be checked when viewing a room.

3. **Rent inclusiveness**  
   Look for “Warmmiete” (warm rent) which includes utilities. If not, add €50–€100 for heating, electricity, and water. Always ask about internet – many student apartments include it.
