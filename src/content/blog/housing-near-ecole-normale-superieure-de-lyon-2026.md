## What Campus Reviews Reveal About ENS de Lyon

Before searching for accommodation near ENS de Lyon, it helps to understand what current and former students actually say about the campus experience. Four reviews on Google (rated 4.0 overall, from 155 reviews) give a mixed picture.

Gabriela Moreira (4 stars, 2 years ago) appreciated the teaching but noted practical frustrations: “Nice place, great teachers, they have free classes to learn french! It’s just a little bit confusing because there no signs or anything indicating where’s the room for the class, very crowded the classroom, recommend to get there early to find a table and a chair, class are very interactive!”

Toyese OYEGOKE (5 stars, 6 years ago) focused on academic offerings: “It is science based courses campus of ENS de Lyon. They ran several programs in mathematics, physics, biology and chemistry related courses in both French and English. You can always consider the place for your BSc, MSc or PhD program. It is really a nice place to be.”

Andres Chau (1 star, 9 years ago) warned about administration: “Chaotic admin. My university experience as an exchange student can only be summarised as a bureaucratic nightmare. The staff were also very slow at replying to me by email regarding urgent matters, such as my exam results. It took me 7 emails and 2 weeks for them to send me a simple piece of paper.”

Truc Dupont (3 stars, 7 years ago) commented on campus dining: “The food was awful, meat was uncooked and they kept us waiting for half an hour. But the staff was really nice. I would not go there again, but i’ve seen worse.”

What these reviews tell a prospective student: the academic quality is strong, but the campus can feel confusing and crowded. Administrative responsiveness has been poor in the past (though the review is older). On-site catering is unreliable. These factors make choosing housing wisely important — a good home base can compensate for campus shortcomings. Living close enough to arrive early and secure a seat, or having a kitchen to avoid campus food, are both practical considerations.

## Lyon City Context: A Student City with Options

Lyon is France’s third-largest city and a major student hub. ENS de Lyon is located in the 7th arrondissement, near the university campus area of Gerland. The city has excellent public transport: metro line B stops close to the school (stations like Debourg or Stade de Gerland), plus tram lines T1 and T2. Bicycle routes are extensive, and walking through the neighbourhood is pleasant.

The average monthly rent for student accommodation in Lyon ranges from around €400–€800 depending on size, location, and facilities. ## Walk vs Transit: The Trade-Off

ENS de Lyon sits at 15 parvis René Descartes, a location that is walkable from several surrounding areas but not in the hyper-centre.

**Walking distance** (under 15 minutes): The immediate Gerland neighbourhood (7th arrondissement) and parts of the 8th near Rue de Marseille or Rue de l’Université. Rent here tends to be higher (€700+). Pros: no transport cost, can arrive early to find a table (as Gabriela Moreira suggested), and avoid crowded campus dining by popping home for lunch. Cons: less nightlife, fewer grocery options compared to central Lyon.

**Short transit** (15–25 minutes by metro or tram): Most of the 7th and 8th arrondissements, plus parts of the 3rd (near Jean Macé or Garibaldi), Villeurbanne, or the 6th. Rent drops to €500–€650 for shared flats. The metro line B runs every 4–6 minutes during peak hours, and tram T1 connects Part-Dieu to the campus. Pros: lower cost, more variety in housing types, easier access to city amenities. Cons: you rely on schedule, and the commute adds up over a semester.

**Longer transit** (30–45 minutes): Areas like Caluire, St-Genis-Laval, or Oullins. Rent can be under €450, but the daily time cost is significant. Only recommended if budget is extremely tight or if you prefer quieter suburbs.

Given the complaints about slow administrative replies (Andres Chau’s review), having a home where you can reliably check email and process paperwork is important. A short transit time means you can pop back to your room between classes if needed.
