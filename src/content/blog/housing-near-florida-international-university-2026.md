## Campus Reviews: What Students and Visitors Say

Florida International University (FIU), located in Westchester, FL, maintains a 4.4 Google rating from 1,247 reviews. The feedback reveals a campus with strong amenities but also areas needing improvement.

Toni Thompson gave 5 stars after attending a conference at the **Ernest Graham Center**, describing the food options positively: “The Center includes a one price cafe that offers various food centers. Pizza, Italian pasta and sauces. Delicious tender chicken. Green veggie of beans, and fluffy mashed potatoes. I chose chicken and juicy pin loin.” This suggests that the campus dining facilities can serve as a daily resource for students who live nearby, reducing the need to cook at home.

Gian Franco Ruffinatti gave 4 stars and praised the physical campus: “Overall a pretty good college with plenty of commodities, bathrooms, places for lunch, a ton of classrooms everywhere, a lot of different careers and majors to choose from.” He also flagged a concern: “It is important to note that counseling for students has lacked significantly for me since I started college here.” For students living off campus, limited counseling access may mean they need to budget extra time to seek support elsewhere or arrange appointments carefully around commuting.

Jorge Manuel Ballesteros Reyes (5 stars) noted the ongoing construction of a new pedestrian bridge: “The new 191-foot FIU pedestrian bridge, replacing the 2018 collapsed structure, is currently under construction by the Florida Department of Transportation (FDOT). This $11.4 million, USD-funded project utilizes traditional steel girder construction rather than the previous concrete design.” This bridge will improve safety for pedestrians crossing busy roads near campus – a factor to weigh when choosing a residence on the opposite side of a major thoroughfare.

Scott Liles (1 star) shared a negative experience with the Moss School of Construction: “My experience with FIU’s Moss School of Construction made it very clear that attention to detail and basic professionalism are not priorities here. I emailed the program with simple admissions questions and received an automatic reply directing me to a second contact for immediate assistance. I followed…” This underlines that communication quality can vary by department. For housing decisions, it reinforces the importance of proximity: being on campus allows you to resolve administrative issues in person rather than depending on email.

Overall, the reviews paint FIU as a large, well-equipped university with good facilities but occasional service gaps. Housing should be chosen with an eye on both convenience and the ability to quickly access campus resources.

## City Context: Westchester and Greater Miami

FIU’s main campus (Modesto A. Maidique Campus) sits in Westchester, a suburban area about 10 miles west of downtown Miami. This location offers a mix of residential neighborhoods, shopping centers, and limited nightlife compared to Miami’s core. The climate is subtropical – hot, humid summers and mild winters – which influences walking and cycling habits.

Greater Miami has a high cost of living relative to other U.S. cities. Public transit includes Metrobus and Metrorail, the latter serving a corridor from the airport and downtown to the southern suburbs. The FIU campus is not directly on a Metrorail line; the nearest station is approximately 2–3 miles away, requiring a bus or ride-share connection. Many students rely on cars, but parking on campus can be competitive.

The surrounding Westchester and Sweetwater neighborhoods are largely residential with a mix of single-family homes and apartment complexes geared toward students. Rentals near campus range from older garden-style apartments to newer developments. Crime statistics for the area are generally moderate, but students should research specific blocks. FIU offers a free night safety escort service on campus.

## Walk vs. Transit Trade-off

Choosing between walking distance and transit access depends on your class schedule, lifestyle, and budget.

**Walking distance (0–1.5 miles)** eliminates commuting costs and time. From the reviews, the campus has “plenty of commodities” including dining, bathrooms, and classrooms – meaning if you live close, you may rarely need to leave campus for daily needs. The new pedestrian bridge will further improve connectivity for those walking across major roads. Drawbacks: rentals in immediate proximity are more expensive and may lack parking or green space.

**Transit-oriented living** (near a Metrorail station or along a frequent bus route) offers lower rent in quieter neighborhoods. For example, living near the Dadeland South station (about 4 miles away) gives access to the Metrorail line, but you still need a bus to reach campus. The total commute can be 40–60 minutes one-way. This trade-off suits students who don’t mind a longer trip and want to be closer to downtown or the airport.

**Driving** is the most flexible option but comes with parking fees (around $100–$200 per semester for a permit) and traffic. FIU’s parking lots fill up early in the morning. Many students choose to drive and arrive on campus before 8 a.m. to secure a spot.

The key is to match your housing location with your primary transport mode. If you plan to walk or bike, focus on listings within 1.5 miles of the Modesto A. Maidique Campus and check sidewalk and lighting conditions. If you plan to drive, consider apartments near major roads (SW 8th Street, SW 107th Avenue) with easy campus access. Transit riders should verify bus schedules and frequencies, especially on weekends.

## Shortlist Logic: How to Choose a Residence

When evaluating off-campus housing near FIU, follow a consistent shortlisting process:

1. **Define your maximum commute time.** Use Google Maps to estimate travel time by your chosen mode (walk, drive, bus, bike) during the hour you typically attend class. A 30-minute commute is often a comfortable upper limit for students.
2. **Set a firm budget.** Include rent, utilities, internet, parking, and any application or amenity fees. Multiply monthly costs by 12 to see the total annual housing expenditure. Redline anything that exceeds your financial aid or income.
3. **Prioritize safety.** Check crime maps (available from local police departments) and read reviews on housing platforms. Look for features like secure entry, well-lit common areas, and on-site management.
4. **Inspect the unit and property management.** Visit in person if possible. Ask about maintenance response times, renewal policies, and roommate matching if you plan to share. The negative review from Scott Liles at FIU’s Moss School highlights how poor communication can affect your experience – the same applies to landlords.
5. **Consider lease terms.** Many student-oriented complexes offer 12-month leases ending in May or August, matching the academic year. Shorter leases or month-to-month options may be available but at a premium.
6. **Check the meal plan or cooking setup.** If you plan to use campus dining (as praised by Toni Thompson), living close by makes it easy to grab lunch. If you prefer to cook, ensure the kitchen is adequate.
7. **Verify shuttle or bus routes.** Some residences near FIU advertise a free shuttle to campus – confirm the schedule and stop locations before signing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1: How far is FIU’s main campus from downtown Miami?**  
FIU’s Modesto A. Maidique Campus is located in Westchester, approximately 10 miles west of downtown Miami. Driving takes about 20–30 minutes without traffic, but rush hour can extend that to 45–60 minutes. Public transit via Metrobus and Metrorail may take 60–90 minutes.

**Q2: What public transit options are available for students living off campus?**  
Miami-Dade Transit operates Metrobus routes that serve FIU (especially the MIA Metrobus line connecting to the Metrorail system). The nearest Metrorail station is University Station (on the Orange Line), about 2 miles from campus, which connects to the airport, downtown, and southern suburbs. A free off-campus shuttle is provided by the university along specific corridors; check FIU’s transportation website for current routes and timings.

**Q3: Is it safe to walk around the FIU campus and surrounding neighborhoods at night?**  
The main campus is patrolled by FIU Police and offers a free safety escort service for walks between buildings. The surrounding Westchester area is generally considered safe, but students should exercise caution, especially after dark. Looking for apartments with secure entry, good lighting, and a short, well-traveled walking route to campus is advisable.
