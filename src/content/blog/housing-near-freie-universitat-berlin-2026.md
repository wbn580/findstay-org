### Berlin City Context: Housing Supply and Demand

Berlin is Germany’s most populous city and a magnet for students. In 2026–2027, the rental market remains competitive, especially in well-connected districts. For FU Berlin, the key residential areas are Dahlem, Steglitz, Zehlendorf, and parts of Wilmersdorf and Charlottenburg. These neighbourhoods are predominantly residential, with a mix of pre-war apartments and modern blocks. Rent levels are moderate compared to central Berlin, but proximity to campus commands a premium.

### Budget Bands for Student Housing

Housing costs vary greatly depending on room type, location, and amenities. Below is a breakdown of typical monthly rent bands for the 2026–2027 academic year. All amounts are indicative and should be verified with the operator before committing.

- **Budget (440–550 EUR)**: Shared flats (WG) and small studios in outer districts like Lichtenrade or Marienfelde. At this level, you may need a 30–40 minute commute to FU Berlin by bus and U-Bahn. Some student dormitories also fall in this range, but availability is limited.
- **Mid-range (550–700 EUR)**: Private rooms in shared flats or small apartments in Steglitz, Zehlendorf, or Dahlem itself. Commute times shrink to 10–20 minutes. Many purpose-built student residences, like those on Hohenzollerndamm or Friedrichstrasse, fall in this bracket.
- **Premium (700+ EUR)**: Single studios or one-bedroom apartments in central Dahlem or near the U-Bahn line U3. These offer maximum convenience and often include modern furnishings. Examples such as UNP 10557 and HAB 10557 are likely in this category.

When budgeting, factor in additional costs: utilities (heating, electricity, internet) often run 50–100 EUR per month, plus a deposit of two to three months’ rent.

### Walk vs. Transit Trade-Off

The decision between living within walking distance or relying on public transport involves trade-offs in cost, time, and lifestyle.

**Walking distance (20 minutes or less)** places you in Dahlem, Zehlendorf, or southern Wilmersdorf. Rent tends to be 15–25% higher than in more distant areas. However, you save the cost and time of daily commuting. A walk through the green campus neighbourhood can be a pleasant start to the day. The sample residence “Hohenzollerndamm 58” is on a major street just east of the campus, a 15-minute walk to FU Berlin’s main entrance.

**Transit commuting (20–40 minutes)** opens up cheaper options in Neukölln, Kreuzberg, or even Prenzlauer Berg – though the latter is farther north. The U-Bahn line U3 connects Krumme Lanke (near campus) to the city centre, and buses M11, X11, and 101 serve Dahlem. A student semester ticket makes unlimited rides cost-effective. Among the sample addresses: “Nazarethkirchstraße 51” is located in Wedding (north Berlin), roughly 35 minutes by U-Bahn and bus – a typical budget-friendly choice. “Friedrichstrasse 63” is in central Mitte, a 30-minute commute via S-Bahn and U-Bahn.

Your choice depends on whether you prioritise lower rent and more vibrant city life (transit) or quiet study time and proximity to campus (walking). Most international students at FU Berlin end up with a commute of 20–30 minutes, balancing cost and convenience.
