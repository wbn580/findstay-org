## Hacettepe University Student Housing Guide: What to Consider Before You Move

Hacettepe University is a well-known public research university in Ankara, Türkiye, ranked 571 in the QS World University Rankings. Its main campus (Sıhhiye) is located in the Altındağ district, close to the city centre and major hospitals. According to Google reviews (1670 ratings, average 4.2), the university is often described as a place with “great doctors” and “excellent experience” for exams, though some note difficulties with appointment scheduling. One reviewer, Ashok Bagriya, called it a “life changing University”. These reviews reflect the academic reputation and clinical environment, but they also hint at a dense urban campus where daily life is intertwined with the city’s rhythm. If you are planning to study here in 2026 or 2027, finding suitable housing is a key step. This guide provides a neutral, factual overview of housing options, budget bands, and decision-making logic based on the university’s location.

### City Context: Ankara and the Altındağ Area

Ankara is Türkiye’s capital and second-largest city, with a population of around 5.7 million. It is generally more affordable than Istanbul, but rents have risen in recent years owing to inflation and demand. Hacettepe’s main campus is in the Hacettepe neighbourhood of Altındağ, a central district that also hosts government buildings, hospitals, and older residential streets. The area is well connected by public transport: the Ankara Metro (Ankaray line) and numerous bus routes serve the vicinity. Many students also live in neighbourhoods like Kızılay (the central business district), Çankaya (upscale residential), or Keçiören (more budget-friendly). Because the campus is in a busy, older part of the city, students often need to weigh proximity against cost and quality.

### Budget Bands (2026–2027 Indicative Rents)

All prices below are **indicative — confirm with the operator** (landlord, dormitory manager, or agency) at the time of booking. The figures are based on typical Ankara market ranges for a single person in a shared or private unit, updated for 2026–2027 expectations.

- **Low budget (Shared room in a student house or dormitory):** 2,500–4,000 TRY per month. Often a bed in a double or triple room with common facilities. Located in older apartment buildings within 30–40 minutes’ commute.
- **Mid budget (Private room in a shared flat or a studio near campus):** 4,000–7,000 TRY per month. A private room in a 3–4 bedroom flat or a compact studio. Convenient walking distance or short bus/metro ride.
- **High budget (One-bedroom flat near Hacettepe or in central Kızılay):** 7,000–12,000 TRY per month. Self-contained units with kitchen and bathroom. Usually newer or renovated buildings, close to transport.

Note: Utility bills (electricity, water, gas, internet) are often separate and add 500–1,500 TRY per month depending on usage. Türkiye’s high inflation means rents can change quickly; always ask for current prices and written contracts.

### Walk vs Transit Trade-off

The Hacettepe Sıhhiye campus is compact, and many faculties (Medicine, Pharmacy, Dentistry, Health Sciences) are concentrated there. Students who prioritise minimising commute time should look for accommodation in the following areas:

- **Walking distance (0–15 min)** : The streets around Hacettepe Mahallesi, Altındağ, offer older apartment blocks and some purpose-built student hostels. Living here means you can walk to class, the hospital, and nearby cafés. However, the area is crowded, noise levels can be high, and apartments may lack modern amenities. Rent for a private room in this zone typically falls in the mid to high budget.
- **Short transit (15–30 min by bus or metro)** : Neighbourhoods like Kızılay, Maltepe, or Dikmen. Kızılay is the transport hub, with the M1 (Ankaray) line directly connecting to Hacettepe station (the stop is called “Hacettepe” on the Ankaray). A monthly student transport pass cost around 300 TRY in 2024 (likely higher in 2026–2027). Living in Kızılay gives you better access to shops, nightlife, and other university libraries, but the commute adds 10–20 minutes each way.
- **Longer commute (30–60 min)** : Budget-friendly districts like Keçiören, Mamak, or Batıkent offer lower rents but require a bus + metro combination. Consider the total travel time and cost when budgeting.

**Trade-off summary:** Walking saves time and fare, but you pay more rent and may sacrifice space or quietness. A longer commute gets you more square metres for your money, but you lose study time and flexibility. Most students choose a 20–30 minute transit option to balance these factors.

### Shortlist Logic: How to Choose Among Nearby Residences

Because specific residences near Hacettepe are not listed in this guide, we provide a decision-making framework you can apply when comparing actual listings (e.g., on student housing platforms, dormitory websites, or local rental portals). Use the following criteria to build your shortlist:

![Hacettepe University](https://img.unistay.net/housing/p/amber-1896520.jpg)

1. **Distance to your faculty** – Note that Hacettepe has multiple campuses (Sıhhiye, Beytepe, etc.). The main medical and health sciences campus is Sıhhiye. If your department is at Beytepe (about 10 km west), you need housing near that campus instead. Confirm your faculty location first.
2. **Budget ceiling** – Set a maximum monthly rent (including utilities) that leaves you enough for living expenses. With inflation, ensure your income (scholarship, family support, part-time job) can cover at least 12 months of rent.
3. **Contract type** – Private rental contracts typically require a 1-year commitment and a deposit (one month’s rent). Student dormitories may offer semester-based options. Check whether the price includes internet, electricity, and water.
4. **Safety and neighbourhood** – Altındağ has both quiet family streets and busy commercial areas. Visit the area in person or use street-view maps. Look for well-lit streets, nearby grocery shops, and 24-hour transport access.
5. **Reviews from other students** – Read recent reviews of the building or dormitory. Look for comments on maintenance, noise, and landlord responsiveness. Be critical of reviews that seem promotional.
6. **Shared vs solo** – Sharing with other Hacettepe students can reduce costs and help you settle in faster. However, ensure compatibility regarding cleanliness and study schedules.

Once you have a shortlist of 5–10 options, narrow it down by visiting (or asking a contact in Ankara to check) the actual condition of the unit, the neighbourhood vibe, and the exact commute time during peak hours.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is it better to live in a dormitory or a private flat near Hacettepe University?**  
A: It depends on your preference for privacy and structure. University-affiliated dormitories (run by KYK or private foundations) often provide meal plans, laundry, and a social environment, but you may have curfews and limited kitchen access. Private flats offer more independence but require you to handle bills, cleaning, and furniture. Many international graduate students prefer flats to have more control over their schedule.

**Q: What is the typical rent for a studio apartment within walking distance of the campus?**  
A: In 2026/2027, a studio (1+0, about 20–35 m²) within 15 minutes’ walk from Hacettepe Sıhhiye campus is likely to cost between 7,000 and 10,000 TRY per month (indicative — confirm with the operator). Units in newer buildings with security and central heating will be at the higher end. Always confirm whether the price includes maintenance fees (aidat) and utilities.

**Q: How do I find a roommate or join a house-share already near the university?**  
A: Many Hacettepe students use social media groups (Facebook, Telegram) or student-to-student rental platforms to find flatmates. You can also check noticeboards near campus cafeterias, or ask your department’s international student office for leads. When house-sharing, get a written agreement for rent split, deposit, and house rules.
