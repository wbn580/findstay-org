## Housing near Illinois Institute of Technology: What Campus Reviews Reveal

Illinois Institute of Technology (IIT) is a private research university located at 10 W 35th St in Chicago’s Bronzeville neighborhood. With a QS World University ranking of 591 and a Google rating of 4.3 based on 470 reviews, the institution attracts a mix of domestic and international students. However, several student reviews on Google indicate administrative and academic challenges that may influence where you choose to live while studying here. Below is a neutral summary of what campus reviews reveal, followed by practical housing guidance.

### What Student Reviews Say

Student reviews on Google are mixed, with some expressing frustration with administrative processes. For example, one reviewer noted:

> “Absolutely one of the worst experiences I have ever gone through without even taking a single course! I was accepted into their Master’s program back in early August ’26, meeting all deadlines, and they couldn’t get my account ready in time to register for the fall semester, so I had to defer.”

Another reviewer shared post-graduation difficulties:

> “If you’re reading this please don’t study here. This is one of the worst colleges you can join to study and even after graduating. It’s been months I am waiting on my diploma and haven’t received it. No one answers the call on academic affairs or office of registrar. Feels like waste of money.”

A third commented on the academic experience:

> “I was enrolled for 6 weeks as a masters student and had a poor experience from start to finish. I had 3 academic advisers be unresponsive to me as I enrolled in the program. 2/4 classes had subpar instruction which included classes not occurring from technical difficulties, incorrect homework sets…”

Finally, a prospective student who visited campus said:

> “I was accepted to this university and came very close to accepting. However, after visiting the campus and considering various factors, I ultimately decided not to attend. … my review is solely based on my experience visiting the campus and its facilities; I did not [attend].”

While these reviews reflect individual experiences, they point to possible administrative delays and communication issues. For students planning to live near campus, a stable, nearby residence can help mitigate the stress of commuting if on-campus logistics are slow.

### Walk vs. Transit Trade-off

IIT’s campus is compact and walkable. Many academic buildings are within a 10-minute walk from each other. The surrounding neighborhood – bounded by 31st St to the north, 35th St to the south, Michigan Ave to the west, and Lake Michigan to the east – is mostly residential and commercial along key corridors.

**Walking** is realistic if you live within a 1-mile radius. Areas south of 35th St (toward 43rd St) and east of King Drive remain walkable, but some blocks have limited street lighting. The campus itself is generally considered safe, though students should exercise usual urban caution. Walking directly from a nearby apartment (like The Buckingham or other buildings on State St) saves money on transit and provides flexibility for late-night study sessions.

**Transit** becomes necessary if you choose housing farther out. The CTA Red Line stops at 35th-Bronzeville-IIT station (on the corner of 35th and State St), which is a major hub. Green and Orange Lines also serve the area. Buses such as the #35 (31st/35th) and #39 (Pershing) run east-west and north-south. A commute from a budget-friendly area in, say, Uptown or Edgewater may take 40–50 minutes one way. That adds up to about 7 hours per week – time that could be spent studying or resting.

For many international students, the trade-off is: cheaper rent on the margins vs. time and transit cost. A monthly CTA pass costs about 75 USD (full fare), but students may qualify for reduced fares through the U-Pass program (available only if the university participates – check with IIT’s student affairs). If you plan to rely on transit, confirm that your building is within a 10-minute walk of a station that directly serves the 35th-Bronzeville-IIT stop.
