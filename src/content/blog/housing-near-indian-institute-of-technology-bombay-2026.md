## Understanding the IIT Bombay Campus from Student Reviews

Indian Institute of Technology Bombay (IITB) sits on a vast, self-contained campus in Powai, a north-eastern suburb of Mumbai. The university holds a QS World University rank of 129 and is one of India’s most prestigious technical institutes. Online reviews (4.7 stars from 5,167 reviewers) consistently highlight the campus atmosphere, facilities, and location. Choosing where to live as a student here means understanding what makes the campus special and how the surrounding neighbourhoods support daily life.

A recurring theme in student reviews is the campus’s green, almost resort-like setting. As Ashutosh Wagh wrote: “IIT Bombay, Powai – A sprawling green campus tucked beside Powai Lake — peaceful, beautiful, and surprisingly self-contained. Peacocks roam freely like they own the place (they probably do). World-class labs, a buzzing food court, and infrastructure that puts most small towns to shame.” This review points to a campus that feels like its own small city, with labs, dining, and nature all within the gates. For housing, that means many students prefer to stay near the campus perimeter to easily access those amenities without relying on long commutes.

Another reviewer, Shrikrishna Prabhu, noted: “It’s always a great feeling to be in IIT Mumbai Campus & attend AI Seminar by ISD. BEST part is to sit in the grand LECTURE 3 HALL (like student days) & finally have sumptuous evening Snacks with few friends @Gulmohar Garden Coffee Shop.” This illustrates that the campus has dedicated social and study spaces – Lecture Hall 3 and Gulmohar Garden Coffee Shop are popular meeting points. Living nearby means you can swing by for a seminar, grab a coffee, and return home without losing half your evening to travel.

The campus also hosts major events. Utkarsh Gupta wrote: “I am currently inside the campus of Indian Institute of Technology Bombay, participating in Techfest on 22 December 2025, and the experience has been truly unforgettable. IIT Bombay is not just an educational institute, it is an ecosystem of innovation, discipline, and excellence.” Such events draw students from across the country, and temporary accommodation near the campus is in high demand during Techfest and similar festivals. For long-term residents, proximity to the main gate and transport hubs becomes critical during those peak periods.

Finally, Ameer Shaik’s review about a national-level NEC challenge underscores the collaborative, competitive spirit on campus: “It’s been a 5-month-long journey of NEC, and after that we secured the 156th rank in the Basic Track of the NEC Challenge, which itself is a proud achievement for us.” Students working on team projects or late-night lab sessions often prefer housing that allows a 10–15 minute walk to academic blocks, rather than a longer bus or auto-rickshaw ride.

## City Context: Powai and Eastern Mumbai

IIT Bombay is located at Main Gate Rd, IIT Area, Powai, Mumbai, Maharashtra 400076. Powai is an upscale residential and commercial hub, developed around Powai Lake. The area is known for its green cover, high-rise apartments, and the Hiranandani Gardens commercial complex. It is relatively less congested than central Mumbai, yet well-connected by road and the Mumbai Metro (the Red Line currently extends to Ghatkopar, with the Powai station expected to open in phases – check current status).

The neighbourhood has a mix of students, IT professionals, and families. Because IIT Bombay is a residential campus (many undergraduates live in hostels), external housing is mostly sought by postgraduate students, research scholars, and short-term course attendees who need off-campus options. Surrounding localities include Powai itself, Hiranandani Gardens (a 10-minute walk from the main gate), and further out, Chandivali and Vikhroli.

Cost of living in Powai is moderately high by Mumbai standards, though still lower than South Mumbai or Bandra. A typical meal at a campus canteen costs about ₹80–₹150, while a coffee at Gulmohar Garden Coffee Shop is around ₹50–₹100. For off-campus housing, you will need to budget for rent, utilities, and transport.

## Walk vs Transit Trade-Off

**Walking distance**  
Housing within a 1 km radius of the campus main gate (particularly in Hiranandani Gardens and parts of Powai near the lake) allows you to walk to classes, the library, and the food court in under 15 minutes. This saves both money and time, and you avoid the morning auto-rickshaw scramble. The trade-off: rent is typically ₹1,000–₹3,000 higher per month than comparable units 2–3 km away. Also, not all walking routes are well-lit at night; the campus itself is safe, but the surrounding roads can be quiet. Reviewers often mention the peacefulness of the area, which means less noise but also fewer late-night shops.

**Transit (auto-rickshaw, bus, metro)**  
If you choose cheaper housing in Chandivali, Vikhroli, or beyond, you will rely on shared autos or BEST buses. A 15–20 minute auto ride to campus costs ₹30–₹80 (depending on distance and meter). The Powai–Vikhroli route has frequent autos, but during rains they can be harder to find. The Mumbai Metro (Line 1) connects Ghatkopar to the western suburbs; from Ghatkopar you can take an auto to IIT Bombay (about 15 minutes). The travel time from Chandivali can reach 25–30 minutes in moderate traffic. For students with irregular schedules (late lab sessions, early workshops), walking is more reliable than depending on transit, especially after 10 pm.

**Bicycle**  
Some students use bicycles – the campus is flat and has cycle-friendly inner roads. Bike rentals are available in Powai. If you live 2–3 km away, cycling can be faster than waiting for an auto. Just note that Mumbai’s monsoon makes cycling difficult from June to September.

## Shortlist Logic: How to Choose Among Nearby Residences

When evaluating off-campus housing near IIT Bombay, follow a consistent shortlist process:

1. **Distance to your primary academic building** – If you are in the main teaching area (Lecture Hall Complex or central library), aim for housing on the Main Gate side of the campus. If your department is near the eastern perimeter (like the IDC School of Design), consider Hiranandani Gardens phase 2, which is closer to that gate.
2. **Transport availability** – Check if the street is served by BEST bus or has an auto stand. For 2026–27, note that the Mumbai Metro extension to Powai is still under construction – do not rely on a metro station being open before it is officially inaugurated.
3. **Amenities** – Grocery stores, pharmacies, and laundries should be within a 5-minute walk. Campus has a shopping centre (within the gate) but closing time is around 9 pm. For late-night needs, Hiranandani Gardens has 24-hour convenience stores.
4. **Security and deposit** – Most landlords ask for a deposit of 2 months’ rent. Verify that the building has a security guard and CCTV. Single women students should look for all-female PG facilities with curfew timings clearly stated.
5. **Seasonal issues** – Powai can get heavy monsoon rain (June–September). Ground-floor apartments may experience dampness; choose a first-floor or above unit if possible. Also confirm that the landlord provides a backup water supply during water cuts (common in Mumbai).
6. **Roommate chemistry** – Shared apartments are the norm. Meet your potential flatmates before signing. Reviews mention that campus life is collaborative – a stable home environment helps you focus on assignments and competitions like the NEC Challenge.

By applying this logic, you can narrow down a handful of properties. Visit during daytime and evening to assess noise, lighting, and local traffic.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**FAQ 1: Can international students stay off-campus from the start?**  
Yes. IIT Bombay does not require all students to live in campus hostels. Many international scholars and exchange students choose off-campus housing in Powai or Hiranandani Gardens. Ensure you have a valid lease agreement and register your local address with your department within 7 days of moving in. The university’s International Student Office can provide a list of verified PG accommodations – ask them for the list during orientation in 2026 or 2027.

**FAQ 2: How far is the nearest market from the campus?**  
The campus itself has a small shopping complex with a grocery store, stationery shop, and ATM. For larger supermarkets (like Nature’s Basket or Reliance Smart), Hiranandani Gardens is the closest, about a 10-minute walk from the Main Gate. Powai Plaza (1.5 km north) has a food court, clothing stores, and a multiplex cinema. Most housing options within walking distance also put you within a 500-metre walk to daily essentials.

**FAQ 3: What is the rental agreement process for off-campus housing?**  
Agreements are usually for 11 months (to avoid stamp duty on longer leases). You will need to pay a security deposit (equal to 2 months’ rent) and the first month’s rent upfront. Make sure the agreement includes a clause for returning the deposit upon vacating, deducing only for actual damages. Many landlords in Powai prefer cash or bank transfer monthly; some accept digital payment. Always keep scanned copies of the agreement and rent receipts for visa/extension purposes.
