## Guwahati as a Student City

Guwahati is the largest city in Assam and a major gateway to Northeast India. The city sits on the south bank of the Brahmaputra River, while IIT Guwahati occupies the north bank. This river location means the campus is about 20 km from the main city centre (e.g., Guwahati Railway Station) and 18 km from the airport. Despite the distance, the area around IITG — known as Amingaon and neighbouring localities — has developed a student-oriented ecosystem over the years.

The climate is subtropical: hot and humid summers (April–September), a monsoon with heavy rainfall, and mild winters (November–February). Flooding can occur during peak monsoon months, so ground-floor apartments in low-lying areas should be checked carefully. The city also hosts several other higher-education institutions, contributing to a diverse student population.

Public transport options include city buses, auto-rickshaws, and app-based cabs. However, buses from the city to the north bank are less frequent than those within the city core. Many students rely on shared auto-rickshaws or bicycles for daily commutes.

## Walk vs. Commute Trade-off

### Walking distance

The IIT Guwahati campus itself is large — about 700 acres. Most academic buildings are clustered around the central library and student activity centre, while hostels are on the northern and eastern sides. If you live in a private hostel or apartment just outside the main gate (e.g., near the IIT Guwahati market or Amingaon), you can walk to the campus entrance in 5–15 minutes. From there, you may still need an internal shuttle or bicycle to reach lecture halls, but the walk to the boundary is manageable.

**Pros:** Zero transport cost, flexible timing, safe during daylight. You can use campus facilities (library, sports complex) even late.  
**Cons:** Limited housing options immediately outside the gate. Most properties near the entrance are older buildings or PG accommodations. Prices tend to be higher because of proximity.

### Short commute (cycle or auto)

A 10–20 minute bicycle ride from nearby villages (e.g., Bamunimaidam, Azara) can cut rent by 20–30% compared to gate-adjacent housing. Auto-rickshaws charge around INR 50–100 per trip. Shared auto-rickshaws from Amingaon to the campus gate operate frequently during class hours.

**Pros:** Wider choice of apartments, lower rent, better availability of larger flats.  
**Cons:** Rainy season can make cycling difficult; auto fares add up over a semester. Safety of bicycles and parking should be considered.

### Longer commute (bus or cab from city side)

Living on the south bank (e.g., Uzan Bazar, Paltan Bazaar, Zoo Road) means a 30–45 minute commute each way across the river. Public buses are cheap but crowded, and app-based cabs cost INR 250–400 one way. Very few students choose this unless they have family in the city.

**Pros:** Access to city amenities (shopping, restaurants, healthcare). More furnished apartment options.  
**Cons:** Commute eats 1–1.5 hours daily; crossing the Brahmaputra bridge can be congested during peak hours.

### On-campus housing

IITG provides hostels for undergraduate and postgraduate students. However, for research scholars, faculty, and some married students, the institute has limited staff quarters and married scholar hostels. Allocation depends on availability and priority. Many students prefer off-campus accommodation after the first year due to hostel rules (e.g., curfews, visitor restrictions) and the desire for more independence.

## Shortlist Logic: Choosing Where to Live

Since no specific nearby residences were provided in the data, the following general framework helps you evaluate any property using your own research:

1. **Distance from the main gate.** Google Maps walking time to the IIT Guwahati administrative block (coordinates: 26.1843° N, 91.6916° E). Aim for less than 30 minutes on foot if you don’t own a vehicle.
2. **Safety and security.** Check for street lighting, neighbour density, and local crime reports. Women students may prefer buildings with separate female entrances or that are part of a student-only complex.
3. **Utilities and maintenance.** Water supply (24/7 or timed), electricity backup during load-shedding, and garbage disposal. Many apartments in North Guwahati rely on borewell water; check potability.
4. **Internet connectivity.** Good broadband (JioFiber, Airtel, BSNL) is essential. Some PGs include Wi-Fi in rent. Confirm speeds, especially during monsoon when cable cuts are common.
5. **Meals and kitchen.** PGs sometimes offer vegetarian meals. If you cook, check kitchen access, LPG cylinder arrangement, and whether the apartment allows cooking.
6. **Lease terms and deposit.** Most landlords ask for a 11-month contract. Renewal may include a 10–15% rent increase. Get a written agreement and a receipt for the deposit.
7. **Noise and neighbours.** Campus is quiet at night. Avoid flats near main roads or auto-rickshaw stands if you need silence for study.
8. **Reviews of the specific property.** Ask current tenants. Use Google Maps or student forums to read reviews of the building or PG operator. The data does not include specific residences, but you can find options by searching “PG near IIT Guwahati” or “apartment rent Guwahati north bank”.

When you find a shortlisted property, visit in person during both day and evening. Test water pressure, check for dampness on walls (common in old buildings during monsoon), and ask about repair response times.

## FAQ

### 1. Is it better to stay in an IIT Guwahati hostel or rent outside?

For the first year, on-campus hostels are strongly recommended because of proximity to classes, labs, and social life. After you settle in, off-campus can offer more freedom, better food options, and lower cost if you share a flat. However, hostel rents are subsidised and include mess facilities (indicative — confirm with the operator: typically INR 10,000–15,000 per semester for accommodation plus food). Outside rooms may cost a similar monthly amount but without meals.

### 2. What is the typical rent for a single room near IIT Guwahati in 2026–2027?

As of 2026–2027, a decent single room in a PG within walking distance of the campus gate ranges from INR 7,000 to 12,000 per month (indicative — confirm with the operator). Slightly farther (2–3 km) you can find rooms for INR 5,000–8,000. Prices are expected to rise 5–10% year-on-year with inflation and new construction. Always confirm the exact amount with the landlord before moving in.

### 3. How do I find reliable rental listings without an agent?

Student communities on social media (Facebook groups like “IIT Guwahati Off-Campus Accommodation”) are active. You can also ask seniors or research scholars. Walk around Amingaon and the villages near the north bank gate — many “To Let” boards are displayed outside buildings. Avoid paying any fee to agents without seeing the property. For safety, do not transfer money without a signed agreement.
