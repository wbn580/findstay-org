## What Campus Reviews Reveal About IITH

Indian Institute of Technology Hyderabad (IITH) is located in Kandi, Telangana, approximately 30 km from Hyderabad city centre. With a QS World University Ranking of 664 and a Google rating of 4.5 from 2,695 reviews, the campus attracts students interested in technology and innovation. Reviews highlight modern infrastructure, but also note the campus’s relative isolation and lack of greenery compared to older IIT campuses.

One reviewer, Krishanu Bandyopadhyay, gave 3 stars and wrote: “Buildings of the institute are Modern and looks cool. But it is a concrete jungle with very less Greenery unlike the older OG IITs (myself biased because of my IIT Madras) Relatively small. My department of visit (Computer Science dept.) is decent and well designed. The library is the most attractiv.” This suggests that while the facilities are contemporary, the campus environment may feel industrial to those accustomed to traditional green campuses.

Sheetal jilla attended a conference and rated 5: “I recently attended a 3-day conference/training at IIT Hyderabad, and it was a very well organised experience. The security and access to the campus were well managed, and the parking arrangements were smooth and properly monitored. The classrooms were clean and well-equipped, and the cafeteria was.” The review points to efficient campus management and basic amenities being in good condition.

Sai Kiran Kasireddy gave 4 stars, describing an “impressive campus with cutting-edge infrastructure and a strong focus on innovation in AI, Data Analytics, and emerging tech. Clean, well-organized, and academically vibrant. A top institute shaping future tech leaders!” This aligns with IITH’s reputation for research in emerging technologies.

Another 5-star review by Visvajith Dev mentions international collaboration: “Very big wide japanese joint research center Japan friendship projects ,Japan-India Science Forum, Infrastructure Support: IITH’s development is supported by a ₹1,400 crore (23 billion yen) ODA loan from JICA. Notable Japanese-designed features include campus hostels with radiant cooling and optimize.” This indicates that campus housing incorporates advanced design, which may influence students’ housing preferences.

## City Context: Kandi and Hyderabad

IITH’s campus is situated in Kandi, a semi-rural area in Sangareddy district, Telangana. The nearest major city is Hyderabad, a hub for information technology, pharmaceuticals, and education. Hyderabad offers a wide range of student amenities: affordable eateries, co-working spaces, libraries, and public transport. However, the distance from Kandi to Hyderabad (about 45–60 minutes by road) means that daily commuting is a significant consideration.

For students enrolled at IITH, the primary housing decision is whether to live on campus in university-provided hostels or to seek off-campus accommodation in nearby villages or Hyderabad suburbs. The campus itself is self-contained with academic blocks, libraries, and hostels — as noted in reviews, the hostels feature Japanese-designed radiant cooling and optimised layouts. Off-campus options are limited in Kandi itself, which has basic facilities; more diverse housing is available in areas like Patancheru (10 km away), BHEL Township, or along the Hyderabad–Sangareddy highway.

## Walk vs Transit Trade-off

**Walkability** is limited to the campus itself. The IITH campus spans approximately 600 acres, and most academic buildings, hostels, and the library are within walking distance of each other. However, off-campus locations require public or private transport. Kandi village is about 1–2 km from the main gate, a 15–25 minute walk, but pavements and street lighting are inconsistent. Students relying on walking for daily essentials should consider this limitation.

**Transit options**: Public buses operated by Telangana State Road Transport Corporation (TSRTC) connect Kandi to Hyderabad (via route 218 and others) with buses every 30–60 minutes. Travel time to Secunderabad or Hyderabad central areas can exceed one hour. Auto-rickshaws and shared taxis are available at the campus gate, with fares to Patancheru around ₹100–₹150, and to Hyderabad city centre ₹300–₹500. Many students prefer to own or share a two-wheeler (scooter or motorcycle), which reduces commute time to 35–50 minutes and provides flexibility for grocery runs and social outings.

**Trade-off**: On-campus housing eliminates commute time but may limit access to city amenities. Off-campus housing increases monthly costs and commute time but offers more diverse living options, restaurants, and entertainment. For those with morning classes or evening labs, staying on campus or within a 10 km radius is recommended.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is on-campus housing guaranteed for all students?

IITH provides hostel accommodation to most undergraduate and postgraduate students. According to reviews, the hostels are equipped with modern features such as radiant cooling, a result of the JICA-funded Japan-India collaboration. Allocation is based on availability and distance from campus. Students should apply through the institute’s housing office after admission. ### What are the typical rental costs for a 1BHK near IITH?

Rent for a 1BHK apartment in areas like Patancheru or Kandi village ranges from ₹6,000 to ₹12,000 per month (indicative — confirm with the operator). In Hyderabad suburbs closer to the city, rates increase to ₹10,000–₹18,000. These prices do not include utilities (electricity, water, internet). ### How should I choose between on-campus and off-campus living?

If you prioritise saving time and having immediate access to labs, library, and peer network, choose on-campus. If you need more privacy, lower food costs (by cooking), or want to engage with Hyderabad’s social scene, off-campus in accessible suburbs is viable. Consider your budget: on-campus hostels are cheaper but may have restrictions (curfew, guest rules). Off-campus gives freedom but adds commute and monthly transport costs. For students who own a vehicle, off-campus living becomes more feasible.
