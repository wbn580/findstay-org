## What Campus Reviews Reveal

A quick look at the 4,688 Google reviews for Indian Institute of Technology Roorkee (IITR), which hold an average rating of 4.7 out of 5, gives a consistent picture: the campus is widely regarded as spacious, green, and conducive to focused study. The physical environment matters a lot when choosing where to live, and these reviews directly inform housing decisions — students often prefer accommodation that lets them tap into that calm, disciplined atmosphere.

Dr. Sushma Gairola, a 5-star reviewer, wrote: *“The campus is exceptionally beautiful, well maintained, and full of academic vibrance. The infrastructure, greenery, discipline, and overall arrangements reflect the institute’s legacy of excellence.”* This points to a setting where walking around feels safe and productive. For a student housing seeker, this means living close to campus — ideally within a 15-minute walk — preserves that seamless integration into the academic bubble.

Travellerpro, another 5-star reviewer, added: *“The campus is full of greenery and open spaces, which makes it perfect for studying and relaxing. The atmosphere motivates students to work hard and focus on their goals.”* That “atmosphere” is not just about classrooms; it extends to the neighbourhood. If you rent a room inside or immediately adjacent to the campus, you get the same quiet, green environment. If you live farther away, near the Roorkee city centre or along the Haridwar Highway, you trade some of that serenity for quicker access to shops, eateries, and transport.

Gaurav Kumar J, rating 4 stars, highlighted the heritage: *“Established in 1847, it is not only among the oldest but also a leader in technology, research, and entrepreneurship.”* The old campus layout means many academic buildings and hostels are concentrated in the core, while newer research centres are spread outward. Knowing this helps you decide: staying near the main gate (Roorkee-Haridwar Highway) keeps you close to buses and auto-rickshaws, while staying near the southern or eastern edges may require a longer walk or cycle ride.

Adway Chakraborty summed it up in a sentence: *“India’s premier institution. Has an excellent campus keeping in mind breathing space, architecture, and nature.”* The “breathing space” is a recurring theme — students value the lack of crowding. When you search for housing near IITR, prioritise properties that offer similar open feel, not cramped multi-storey blocks. The reviews strongly suggest that living in a dense, noisy area would clash with the campus character.

## City Context and Budget Bands

Roorkee is a town in Uttarakhand, not a large metro. Its economy revolves around the institute and a few industrial units (like the Roorkee engineering works). The cost of living is lower than in Delhi or Dehradun, but comparable to other small north Indian university towns. Most IITR students live in on-campus hostels (allocated by the institute), but a growing number — especially research scholars, married students, and those in short-term programmes — seek private rental accommodation outside.

Because the data does not include specific residence names or rental prices, the following budget bands are based on typical Roorkee market norms (2026‑2027 range). **All prices are indicative — confirm with the operator.**

- **Budget (₹5,000–₹10,000 per month)**  
  Single room in a shared flat or a small studio near Jagjeetpur, the area just west of the campus. Most rooms are unfurnished; you may need to buy a bed and a table. Expect fans, occasional power cuts, and bucket baths in older buildings.
- **Mid-range (₹10,000–₹18,000 per month)**  
  Furnished single-bedroom independent flats or larger shared apartments within 1–2 km of the main gate. These often have a private bathroom, a small kitchen, and a balcony. Water supply is usually from borewells, so check for consistency.
- **Premium (₹18,000–₹30,000 per month)**  
  Semi-furnished or fully furnished 1‑BHK or 2‑BHK flats in gated societies along the Haridwar Highway (e.g., near the Roorkee railway station). Air conditioning, geyser, and backup generator are common. These are typically leased by faculty or long‑term visitors; student leases may require a one‑year commitment.
- **Luxury / short‑stay (₹30,000+ per month)**  
  Serviced apartments or guest houses that cater to visiting professors and conference delegates. Amenities include Wi‑Fi, housekeeping, and security. Rarely used by regular students due to cost and distance from academic blocks.

Roorkee experiences hot summers (up to 40°C) and chilly winters (down to 5°C). Air conditioning (or at least a good cooler) is essential from April to July; a room heater or warm blankets are necessary from December to February. Many budget rentals lack heating, so factor in that trade‑off.

## Walk vs. Transit Trade‑off

![Indian Institute of Technology Roorkee (IITR)](https://img.findstay.org/housing/p/amber-288672.jpg)

IITR’s main campus spreads over about 365 acres. The academic core — lecture halls, libraries, and the main administrative building — is located near the centre. Residential neighbourhoods immediately adjoining the campus include:

- **North / Jagjeetpur side**: Walkable to the western gate (close to the sports complex and mathematics department). Auto‑rickshaw fare to the main gate is ₹20–₹30 (indicative). Streets are narrow, but the area is quiet and popular with research scholars.
- **East / Roorkee Railway Station area**: About 3.5 km from the main gate. Best accessed by cycle‑rickshaw (₹30–₹50) or shared auto. You get more rental options and cheaper groceries, but you lose the “green and peaceful” factor described in the reviews.
- **South / Haridwar Highway**: Directly adjacent to the main gate. High foot traffic, many fast‑food stalls, and good connectivity to Haridwar and Dehradun via state buses. Noise levels are higher, but convenience is unmatched for daily errands.

**Walkable radius**: Within 1.5 km of the main gate you can reach all core academic buildings in 15–20 minutes on foot. Beyond that, cycling becomes common. Bicycles can be bought used for ₹1,500–₹3,000 (indicative), and most landlords allow parking inside the compound. If you rely solely on public transport, budget an extra 30–45 minutes per day for commuting and ₹2,000–₹3,000 per month on auto/rickshaw fares.

**Transit alternatives**: The Roorkee railway station connects to Delhi (about 5–6 hours by express train) and Dehradun (1.5 hours). City buses run along the Highway but are infrequent. Ola/Uber operate in town, but minimal; auto‑rickshaws are the main mode. For students who want to avoid walking long distances, living on the Highway (south) is the pragmatic choice, despite the trade‑off in noise.

Most landlords expect a minimum of 11 months, aligning with the academic year. Shorter stays (3–6 months) are possible but usually come with a 10–20% rent premium. **2. How do I commute from off‑campus housing to IIT Roorkee if I live far from the main gate?**  
Shared auto‑rickshaws run from the railway station and Jagjeetpur junction to the main gate every few minutes (₹10–₹20 per ride, indicative). Bicycles are a practical alternative — the terrain is flat, and parking is free inside the campus. A second‑hand cycle costs roughly ₹1,500–₹3,000 (indicative). The institute does not run shuttle buses for off‑campus students.
