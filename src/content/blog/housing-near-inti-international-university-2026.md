The campus is located at Persiaran Perdana BBN Putra Nilai, Bandar Baru Nilai, in Negeri Sembilan, Malaysia. With a Google rating of 3.8 based on 320 reviews, current and former students have shared both praises and frustrations that can help you choose where to live. This guide breaks down what the reviews tell us, the local context, typical price ranges, the walk-versus-transit trade-off, and a logical way to shortlist properties.

### City Context: Nilai and Bandar Baru Nilai

INTI International University is in Bandar Baru Nilai, a township within the Nilai municipality in Negeri Sembilan, about 45 km south of Kuala Lumpur. Nilai is a growing education hub, hosting several private universities and colleges. The area is relatively suburban, with a mix of shop lots, eateries, and residential developments. The nearest major transport hub is Nilai Railway Station (KTM Komuter), approximately 3–5 km from the campus, connecting to KL Sentral in about an hour.

The local economy is student-driven. You will find many budget-friendly restaurants, convenience stores, and printing shops within walking distance of the campus. The neighbourhood is generally safe during the day, but like any suburban area, you should be cautious at night.

### Budget Bands for Student Housing

Rental prices in Nilai are generally lower than in Kuala Lumpur, but they vary by property type, distance from campus, and facilities. Below are common budget bands based on typical listings in the area. All prices are indicative — confirm with the operator before committing.

- **Shared room (3–4 occupants)**: RM 300 – RM 500 per month. Common in terraced houses or purpose-built student apartments. Includes basic furniture and sometimes utilities.
- **Private room (single occupancy)**: RM 500 – RM 800 per month. More privacy; often includes air conditioning, desk, and wardrobe. Shared common areas (kitchen, living room, bathroom).
- **Studio apartment**: RM 800 – RM 1,200 per month. Self-contained unit with private bathroom and kitchenette. Usually in newer apartment complexes with security.
- **Entire apartment (2–3 bedrooms)**: RM 1,200 – RM 2,000 per month. Suitable for groups of friends splitting the cost.

*Note: These figures are estimates based on 2025–2026 market trends. Always ask the landlord or property manager for a written quotation including deposit, utilities, and maintenance fees.*

### Walk vs. Transit Trade-off

**Walking distance (under 1 km, 10–15 minutes)**  
Pros: No transport cost, no waiting time – you can leave your room 10 minutes before class. Avoids timetable-change anxiety. Fresher air, quieter streets.  
Cons: Higher rent for properties directly adjacent to campus. Fewer late-night food options in immediate vicinity (though plenty within a 20-minute stroll).  
Many purpose-built student accommodations are clustered around Jalan INTI and Persiaran Perdana BBN. It is easy to find a room within a 10-minute walk.

**Short commute (1–3 km, 5–10 minutes by bike/scooter, 15–20 minutes by bus)**  
Pros: More affordable rooms in older housing schemes. Larger living spaces or quieter surroundings.  
Cons: Need to arrange personal transport (bicycle, scooter, or car) or rely on infrequent local buses. The bus to campus may not run very late. Also, road traffic can be moderate during peak hours.

**Longer commute (over 3 km, requires car or train)**  
Pros: Cheapest rents in areas like Nilai town centre or nearby villages. Access to Nilai Railway Station for trips to KL.  
Cons: Significant time and money spent on transport. Motorbike or car required. In rainy seasons, the commute can be unpleasant. Not recommended if you have early morning or late evening classes.

Given the timetable instability noted in reviews, walking-distance housing is strongly advised unless you have a very flexible schedule.

### Shortlist Logic: How to Evaluate Nearby Residences

![INTI International University](https://img.unistay.net/housing/p/amber-3151391.jpg)

You do not need to settle on a specific property immediately. Use the following logic to create a shortlist based on the data and reviews.

1. **Proximity filter**: Limit your search to residences within a 15-minute walk from the main campus entrance. Use Google Maps walking time (not driving). Ask current students in Facebook groups or campus forums for distances they consider walkable.
2. **Cleanliness and maintenance**: The positive reviews consistently praise cleanliness. Inspect the property in person before signing. Check the condition of bathrooms, kitchen, and common areas. Ask whether the landlord provides regular cleaning service.
3. **Landlord responsiveness**: The negative reviews about timetable changes and certificate mailing point to administrative frustration. While your landlord won’t control the university’s timetable, a responsive landlord can help with mail handling, maintenance, and deposit return. Look for properties managed by reputable operators with online presence (but do not use brand names). Prefer those who reply quickly to queries.
4. **Internet reliability**: Most coursework involves online submissions and research. Ask current tenants or check reviews (if any) about Wi‑Fi stability. Since many student apartments include internet, confirm the speed and whether there is a data cap.
5. **Security**: Nilai is generally safe, but standard precautions apply. Look for gated properties with CCTV, secure access doors, and adequate lighting in hallways.
6. **Utilities inclusions**: Clarify whether rent covers water, electricity, and internet. In many budget shared rooms, utilities are split per person; factor this into your total monthly cost.
7. **Flexible tenancy**: Given that your timetable might change, try to negotiate a shorter initial lease (e.g., 6 months with option to renew) rather than a full year. This allows you to move if your needs change.
8. **Reference recent tenant reviews**: Since the university’s Google reviews reflect campus experience, also search for reviews of specific housing complexes on Google Maps or local platforms. Focus on comments about noise, cleanliness, and landlord behavior. Be wary of properties with no reviews at all.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does it take to walk from nearby student housing to INTI campus?**  
Most purpose-built student apartments and terraced houses within Bandar Baru Nilai are between 5 and 20 minutes on foot. The exact time depends on the street: properties on Jalan INTI can be as close as 3 minutes, while those on the outskirts of the township may take 15–20 minutes. Use a mapping app with walking mode to verify. Always ask the landlord for a walking route estimate before signing.

**Is public transport reliable between Nilai town and the university?**  
Nilai has a local bus service (e.g., Nilai Bus) and the KTM Komuter train at Nilai Railway Station. The train runs to KL Sentral approximately every 30 minutes during peak hours, but the station is 3–5 km from campus, requiring a taxi or ride-hail for the last mile. Buses are less frequent after 7 pm and irregular on weekends. For daily commuting, a bicycle or motorbike is more practical. If you plan to rely on public transport, check the bus schedule in advance and consider living within walking distance of a bus stop that serves the campus.

**How can I avoid the problems mentioned in reviews, such as certificate delivery issues?**  
The certificate complaint highlights a landlord/ address problem, not a campus one. To safeguard important mail: (1) Use a mailbox that is locked and protected from rain. (2) Provide the university’s address as an alternative for document delivery if permitted (some universities will hold certificates for collection). (3) Inform your landlord that you expect official mail and ask them to notify you upon delivery. (4) During semester breaks, arrange for a trusted friend to collect your mail if you are away. For rental contracts, include a clause about mail handling and responsibility if items go missing.
