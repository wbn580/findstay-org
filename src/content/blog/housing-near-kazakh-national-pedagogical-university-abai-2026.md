### Walk vs. Transit Trade-Off

The university’s central location gives students three main commuting patterns:

- **Walking (distance ≤ 1.5 km):** Properties within a 15–20 minute walk include apartments on Dostyk Avenue, as well as on parallel streets like Tole Bi, Makatayev, or Abay Avenue. Walking saves time and money but comes with a price premium. Rents in this radius are typically in the high-budget range or the upper end of mid-budget. The trade-off: you avoid morning traffic and bus delays, but you pay more for square footage and may need to accept older buildings (many Soviet-era blocks).
- **Public transit (1.5–5 km):** Almaty’s metro line 1 (north-south) and bus routes (e.g., 29, 60, 66) connect surrounding districts. A 20–30 minute bus or metro ride expands affordable options to neighborhoods like Zhetysu (south-east) or Bostandyk (south-west). Students taking transit save 30–50% on rent compared to the walking zone. The trade-off is a fixed monthly transport cost (about 5,000–8,000 KZT for a transport card) and schedule dependency, especially in winter when waiting outside is less pleasant.
- **Cycling or micromobility:** Almaty is bike-friendly in warm months, and e-scooter rentals are available. Areas 2–4 km away become easily reachable. However, winter snow and ice make cycling impractical from November to March.
