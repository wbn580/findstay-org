### City Context: Jeddah as a Student City

Jeddah is the second-largest city in Saudi Arabia and a major port on the Red Sea. The climate is hot and humid most of the year, with mild winters. KAU’s main campus is located on Idara Street, in the Al-Salamah district, about 15 km north of the city centre. This area is well-developed, with shopping malls, hospitals, restaurants, and public parks within a few kilometres. The Corniche (waterfront) is roughly 8 km away.

Transport in Jeddah relies heavily on private cars or ride-hailing services (e.g., Uber, Careem). Public bus services exist but are limited and not always student-friendly. Many students, especially internationals, choose to live within walking distance or a short ride to avoid daily traffic. The university operates its own shuttle for some routes, but details should be confirmed upon enrolment.

Neighbourhoods near KAU include Al-Salamah, Al-Mohammadiyah, Al-Zahra, and Al-Naeem. These are primarily residential areas with a mix of villas and apartment buildings. Rents vary based on size, furnishing, and distance to the campus. Note that Saudi Arabia does not have a formal student housing system like purpose-built student accommodation (PBSA) found in some other countries; students typically rent private apartments or houses, often sharing with flatmates.

### Walk vs Transit Trade-off

Choosing between walking distance and commuting involves balancing cost, time, comfort, and lifestyle.

**Walking distance (≤ 15 minutes)**

- Pros: No transportation cost; freedom to attend early or late classes without worrying about traffic; easy access to campus facilities (library, gym, dining); safer for female students (privacy concerns in public transport).
- Cons: Typically higher rent; less choice of flats; may be noisy if the building faces a main road; limited grocery stores within immediate walking distance (car needed for big shopping).
- Typical areas: Directly on Idara Street, or in the first block off the street. Many students rent in the Al-Salamah residential complex adjacent to the university.

![King Abdul Aziz University (KAU)](https://img.unistay.net/housing/p/amber-1096802.jpg)

**Transit commute (5–15 minutes by car/taxi)**

- Pros: Wider range of housing options often at lower cost; quieter residential settings; better access to supermarkets, parks, and amenities.
- Cons: Monthly ride-hailing cost (SAR 400–800 if commuting daily); time spent in traffic (Jeddah congestion can add 10–20 minutes); need to plan around prayer times when traffic reduces.
- Typical areas: Al-Mohammadiyah (3 km), Al-Zahra (4 km), and Al-Naeem (5–7 km). Bicycle or e-scooter use is possible but not common due to heat and lack of dedicated lanes.

Reviewer Jesmin Akter mentioned the “excellent” teaching environment; if you plan to spend long hours on campus, walking proximity saves energy. Conversely, if you value a more spacious home and do not mind a short drive, the suburbs offer better value.

### FAQ

**1. Are there student dormitories at King Abdul Aziz University?**  
Yes, KAU provides on-campus housing for male and female students, but rooms are limited and primarily allocated to international scholarship recipients or those living far from Jeddah. You must apply through the Deanship of Student Affairs. Priority is given to new students each academic year. Costs are subsidised and often include meals, but exact fees should be confirmed directly with the university. Off-campus private rentals are the more common option.

**2. Is it safe to walk around campus and nearby areas at night?**  
Jeddah city is generally safe, and the KAU campus has 24/7 security. The surrounding Al-Salamah district is a quiet residential area with moderate lighting. However, temperatures remain high until late evening, so walking in summer (June–September) is uncomfortable. Female students may prefer using ride-hailing after dark for extra privacy. Always exercise usual caution.

**3. What are the typical utility costs for a rented apartment near KAU?**  
For a one-bedroom apartment in a mid-range building, electricity (AC is the main cost) ranges from SAR 300–700 per month in summer and SAR 100–300 in winter. Water is typically around SAR 50–100. Internet (fibre optic) adds SAR 150–250. If utilities are not included in the rent, budget an extra SAR 500–1,000 per month. Confirm with the landlord whether you pay directly to providers or through a flat fee.
