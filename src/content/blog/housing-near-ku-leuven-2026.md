## What Student Reviews Reveal About KU Leuven

KU Leuven, ranked 60th in the QS World University Rankings, hosts a campus in the centre of Leuven, Belgium (Oude Markt 13). Its Google rating of 3.9 from 343 reviews points to a polarised student experience. Two themes emerge clearly from the reviews: strong academic quality on one side, and serious institutional criticism on the other.

Positive feedback highlights the undergraduate experience. Júlia Ligero Muñoz wrote: “High quality undergraduated programs. Professors are nice, helpful and experienced. Great campus. Best city ever for an Erasmus. Pangaea is a must visited place for relax, work and social networking.” This review, posted seven years ago, still reflects the university’s reputation for strong programmes and a vibrant student city.

However, several recent reviews raise concerns about safety and institutional response. Jtm T commented two months ago: “I considered going to this university but after finding out the atrocities that were allowed to occur and continue to occur at this place I no longer feel safe being any where near this institute. 18 men took away an innocent man, yet they are allowed to walk free and not even suspended.” Similarly, sabrina stewart wrote six months ago: “This university did nothing to help that young man. Just racist and unjustly, god sees all. Justice for Sanda Dia. He deserved so much better💔.” Gozel Annageldiyeva’s one-star review mentioned difficulties with the admissions process for their child, who is a multilingual applicant.

These reviews indicate that while KU Leuven’s academic offering is respected, prospective students should also weigh the university’s handling of incidents and admissions. This context matters when choosing housing: feeling safe and supported in your neighbourhood is as important as lecture quality.

## Navigating Leuven: City Context for Students

Leuven is a compact student city about 25 km east of Brussels. The historic centre revolves around the Oude Markt (where the university address lies), which is lined with bars and cafes. Most university buildings are within a 15–20 minute walk of each other. The city’s population is roughly 100,000, with a high proportion of students — around 60,000, including those at other institutions.

Public transport includes buses run by De Lijn, which connect the city centre to residential suburbs and neighbouring towns (including Brussels). Leuven’s train station provides direct services to Brussels (30 minutes), Antwerp (40 minutes), and other cities. Bicycles are extremely common; the flat terrain makes cycling a practical option year-round.

For students, the main residential zones are:

- City centre (within the ring): highest density of student housing, short walks to campus, but generally more expensive and noisier.
- Suburbs such as Heverlee (south), Kessel-Lo (east), and Wilsele (north): quieter, cheaper, but require a bus or bike commute (10–20 minutes).
- Neighbouring towns like Herent and Wijgmaal: more affordable, but require a train or long bus ride.

## Walk vs Transit Trade-off

Given Leuven’s size, walking is the default for anyone living inside the inner ring (the R30 belt). Most lectures, libraries, and the student union Pangaea are reachable within 20 minutes on foot. Students living in Heverlee (home to the Arenberg Science Campus) can also walk to science and engineering buildings in 15–20 minutes.

Transit becomes relevant when housing is in suburbs outside the ring. Bus lines 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and many more run from the station to Kessel-Lo, Heverlee, and Wilsele. A single bus ticket costs around €2.50 (2027 price – indicative, confirm with operator), or a monthly pass is around €30–€50. Walking times from bus stops to campus are typically under 10 minutes.

Cycling is the most efficient hybrid: a 10-minute bike ride can cover a 30-minute walk. Bikes can be rented from De Lijn Blue-bike stations (€4 per day) or purchased second-hand for €50–€100. The trade-off: walking increases rental costs but saves time and transit fares. Transit expands affordable housing options but adds 15–30 minutes per trip.

## FAQ

**Q1: How far in advance should I look for housing near KU Leuven?**

Start your search 3–4 months before the semester begins (May–June for an October start). Many university-managed rooms open applications in April. Private listings appear heavily from July onwards. The earlier you apply, the better your chance of securing a place within walking distance, which can alleviate concerns raised in reviews about feeling unsafe.

**Q2: Are there safe and affordable neighbourhoods close to the Oude Markt campus?**

Yes. Heverlee (south of centre) is popular with students and considered safe, with a mix of student houses and apartments. Kessel-Lo (east) is quieter and slightly cheaper, with good bus connections. Within the inner ring, streets near the station and the Muntstraat area are busy and well-lit but can be noisy. Avoid extremely low-rent listings far from main roads, as they may lack lighting or quick access to public transport.

**Q3: What should I check before signing a lease to avoid the problems mentioned in the reviews?**

Verify the contract’s notice period, deposit terms (usually 2–3 months’ rent), and whether utilities are included. Ask about maintenance responsiveness — a slow landlord can exacerbate stress. If possible, talk to current tenants or check online reviews of the building. Visit the neighbourhood at different times of the day, especially after dark, to gauge safety. The university’s ombuds service can also assist with disputes.
