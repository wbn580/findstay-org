### City Context: London as a Student City

London is one of the world’s most expensive student destinations, but it also offers unmatched cultural, professional, and social opportunities. LSE’s campus is in the Holborn area, part of the City of Westminster and the London Borough of Camden. The nearest tube stations are Holborn (Piccadilly and Central lines), Temple (Circle and District lines), and Covent Garden (Piccadilly). Numerous bus routes serve the area. Most lectures and libraries are within a 10‑minute walk of Holborn station.

Accommodation options near LSE fall into two broad categories: purpose‑built student accommodation (PBSA) and private rentals. PBSA is popular because contracts are aligned with the academic year, utilities are often included, and buildings offer communal spaces, gyms, and study rooms. As of early 2026, the student‑housing platform lists 53 properties in London with prices starting from £130 per week – indicative – and ranging upward to over £400 per week. The sample of nearby residences includes One Penrhyn Road Kingston, Fusion Brent Cross Town, LH‑County Hall, urbanest Westminster Bridge, and Scape Hammersmith.

### Walk vs. Transit Trade‑off

LSE’s location means you can walk from many parts of central London. The campus is within 20 minutes’ walk of most Zone 1 landmarks: Covent Garden, Leicester Square, the Strand, the Houses of Parliament, and the South Bank. For students living in the immediate vicinity – Holborn, Bloomsbury, Covent Garden, or Waterloo – the daily commute can be a 5‑ to 15‑minute walk. This saves both time and money on Tube fares (a single Zone 1–2 journey costs around £2.80 with an Oyster card in 2026).

However, walking‑distance properties (within a 15‑minute radius) tend to be more expensive. If you choose a residence like urbanest Westminster Bridge (10‑minute walk) or LH‑County Hall (15‑minute walk), you pay a premium for convenience. On the other hand, properties farther out – such as Scape Hammersmith (25 minutes by Tube) or Fusion Brent Cross Town (35–40 minutes) – can be significantly cheaper. The decision comes down to your budget and how much time you are willing to spend commuting.

London’s public transport is extensive. The Tube runs from about 5:00 a.m. to midnight (with night services on some lines at weekends). Buses run 24 hours. A monthly travelcard for Zones 1–2 costs about £150 in 2026; for Zones 1–3 it is around £175. If you live in Zone 3 or beyond, factor in the higher travel cost when comparing rent.

When shortlisting, consider these factors:

- **Walking distance to campus**: If you have early lectures or late library sessions, a short walk reduces stress.
- **Tube/bus proximity**: Check the nearest station and travel time to Holborn or Temple.
- **Contract length**: PBSA contracts are typically 44 or 51 weeks. Some offer shorter stays for summer schools.
- **Included utilities**: Verify whether bills, contents insurance, and Wi‑Fi are included.
- **Safety and area**: Central London is generally safe, but research the neighbourhood after dark.

A practical approach is to create a shortlist of three to five properties, visit (or take a virtual tour) if possible, and compare the total cost (rent + travel + bills) for the academic year.
