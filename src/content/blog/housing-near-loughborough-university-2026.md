## Housing Near Loughborough University: What Campus Reviews Reveal

Loughborough University sits on a 300-acre campus in Leicestershire, England. The university holds a QS World University Ranking of 225 and has a Google rating of 4.6 from 818 reviews. Student feedback consistently highlights the scale of the campus and its sporting facilities, while some reviews raise concerns about administrative and accessibility experiences. For anyone planning to study here in 2026 or 2027, understanding the housing landscape — from on-campus options to private rentals in town — is essential.

### What Recent Reviews Tell You About Campus Life

Reviews offer practical insights that can shape your housing decisions. A long-standing positive review notes: *“It has the largest campus covering more than 300 acres of property. It is so big that there is actually a bus lane running through the campus. Allow amber time to reach your location if you are new to the campus.”* Another reviewer writes: *“Amazing university landscape and great community feeling. An extraordinary amount of sports facilities and grounds which makes it extremely appealing to student athletes. Great accommodation options available as well. Very close to the town which is mostly student populated.”* These observations underline two key points: the campus is large enough that you may need time to walk between lectures, and the town is close and student-oriented.

On the other hand, a more recent critical review states: *“Unfortunately, the accessibility arrangements were disappointing. Parking was extremely difficult to arrange as a disabled visitor attending with a carer. I had to make phone calls and provide a significant amount of information before parking could be organised, which felt unnecessarily complicated.”* Another negative review reads: *“While the facilities are undoubtedly world class, institutional bullying is rife, and the staff will close ranks to protect their own. I was forced to terminate my studies early, and was then threatened with legal action for outstanding fees - for services I never received.”* These accounts, while representing individual experiences, suggest that if you have specific accessibility or administrative needs, you should clarify support arrangements before committing to housing that relies on university-managed services such as parking or contract handling.

### City Context: Loughborough as a Student Town

Loughborough is a market town in the East Midlands, roughly a 20-minute train ride from Leicester and about two hours from London by train. The town’s population is around 60,000, with a significant student presence. The town centre has grocery stores, banks, restaurants, and a few entertainment venues. Most student-focused housing is within walking distance of the university campus, which lies on the eastern edge of town. Because the town is relatively compact, many students choose to walk or cycle between accommodation and campus. The university operates a regular bus service (the “campus bus”) that runs through the campus and into town; a bus pass is available for students who live further out.

### Budget Bands for 2026/2027 Accommodation

Rental prices in Loughborough vary depending on property type, location, and facilities. All figures below are **indicative — confirm with the operator** when you make enquiries.

**Low budget (under £100 per week):** Shared houses or older flats in the town centre or near the Ashby Road area. Basic furnishings, communal living, and often a longer walk to campus (25–35 minutes). Some rooms in shared houses in the student corridor between the university and the town fall into this band.

**Mid budget (£100–£140 per week):** Purpose-built student accommodation (PBSA) or newish private halls within 15–20 minutes’ walk of campus. Typically en-suite rooms with shared kitchen/living areas. Many of the larger PBSA providers operate in Loughborough — you can compare options on independent student housing platforms.

**Higher budget (£140–£180+ per week):** Studios or one-bedroom flats, often in modern complexes with gyms, common rooms, and on-site management. Some of these are located very close to campus (5–10 minutes’ walk). A handful of luxury schemes with premium finishes may exceed £180 per week.

Rents are usually quoted as weekly amounts, and tenancy lengths often run 44–51 weeks. Be sure to check whether bills (utilities, internet, contents insurance) are included — most PBSA contracts are all-inclusive.

### Walk vs. Transit: What Works for Different Types of Students

The campus is large: from the western edge (near the town) to the easternmost academic buildings can take 25–30 minutes on foot. The university’s own bus service runs a circular route through the campus, with stops at key buildings and near accommodation blocks. If you live in town, you can catch the bus from the town centre to the campus hub. Many students cycle — the town and campus are both flat, and there are bike racks throughout.

**If you prefer walking:** Look for accommodation within the “student corridor” — the area between the town centre and the university, roughly along Ashby Road, Epinal Way, and Park Road. Walking time to most lecture buildings from these areas is 10–20 minutes.

**If you rely on transit:** Accommodation further out (e.g., parts of the town centre or near the railway station) may require a bus or cycle. The campus bus runs frequently during term time, but service reduces outside peak hours and during holidays. Check the bus schedule for 2026/2027, as routes can change.

**If you drive or have a car:** Parking on campus is limited and expensive — a permit may cost several hundred pounds per year. The negative review above flags the difficulty for visitors with disabilities, so don’t assume you can park easily. If you need a car for medical or family reasons, consider living in a house with off-street parking and using the bus or walking to campus. Note that many PBSA properties have no parking at all.

### Shortlist Logic: How to Choose Among Nearby Accommodation Options

Because no single accommodation provider dominates Loughborough, your shortlist should be based on your priorities. Here is a logical framework to narrow down options, using common types of housing available near the university.

![Loughborough University](https://img.unistay.net/housing/p/amber-1318448.jpg)

**1. On-campus university halls**  
These are typically reserved for first-year undergraduates and international students, though some graduate accommodation exists. They are the most convenient — you are within a 5–10 minute walk of most teaching buildings. However, availability is limited, and you must apply through the university’s accommodation service early. Contracts are usually 39–40 weeks. Indicative rent for 2026/2027: £120–£180 per week (self-catered en-suite). Confirm exact pricing with the university.

**2. Private-purpose-built student accommodation (PBSA)**  
Several large PBSA operators have properties in Loughborough. They offer en-suite rooms or studios with all-inclusive bills, common areas, and laundry facilities. Most are located on or near Ashby Road, a 15–20 minute walk from the campus centre. Some are newer and come with gyms, cinema rooms, and study spaces. The trade-off: cost is usually higher than shared houses, but you get fewer surprises regarding bills and maintenance. For 2026/2027, expect £110–£160 per week for en-suites, £150–£200 for studios. Always verify with the operator.

**3. Shared houses (private rental)**  
Many students rent houses in clusters near Ashby Road, Forest Road, and the town centre. These are often older properties with 4–6 bedrooms, a shared kitchen and living room, and a garden. Rent can be lower than PBSA (£80–£110 per week per person), but you must arrange bills yourselves, and the condition of the property varies. Deposit protection, gas safety certificates, and tenancy agreements should be checked carefully. View properties in person or via video call. The walking distance to campus from these areas ranges from 10 to 30 minutes.

**4. Studios and one-bedroom flats**  
If you prefer complete privacy, studios and one-bedroom flats are available in modern blocks (e.g., near the town centre). These are the most expensive option (£150–£200 per week) and usually have shorter walking distances to town amenities rather than to campus. You may need to use public transport or cycle.

**Shortlist decision matrix (narrative summary):**

- If budget is your primary concern and you can tolerate a longer walk or bus ride, shared houses in the student corridor offer the lowest rent.
- If convenience and all-inclusive bills matter most, on-campus halls or PBSA close to the campus bus route are good choices.
- If you require a quiet study environment or have specific accessibility needs, look for newer PBSA with lifts and adapted rooms — but check with the operator about accessibility arrangements, as the negative review suggests this can be problematic.
- If you will have a car, prioritise houses with off-street parking and be prepared to buy a campus parking permit; avoid PBSA that charge high fees for parking or have none.

Always confirm the exact rent, contract length, and what is included before signing. Use an independent student housing platform to compare multiple listings, and read recent reviews from current or former tenants (past six months) to gauge management responsiveness.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: When should I start looking for accommodation for the 2026/2027 academic year?**  
A: On-campus accommodation applications usually open in late spring (April/May) for the following September intake. Private PBSA rooms go on sale as early as November the previous year — many operators release rooms for the 2026/2027 academic year from late 2025. Shared houses are typically advertised from January to June. To secure your top choices, begin research 10–12 months before your move-in date.

**Q: Is it cheaper to live in town or near campus?**  
A: Rent in the town centre is generally similar to the student corridor, but you may need to add transport costs (bus pass or bike). The town centre has more nightlife and shops, but walking to campus takes 25–35 minutes. The student corridor (Ashby Road / Epinal Way) has the highest concentration of student housing and is closer to the university. Comparing total cost (rent + transport + time) usually favours the corridor if you walk.

**Q: Can I get a guarantor if I am an international student?**  
A: Many private landlords and PBSA operators accept international students with a full year’s rent paid in advance, or through a guarantor service that charges a fee (typically 3–5% of the annual rent). Some university halls allow overseas students to use the university as a guarantor for a limited number of rooms. Always ask the provider about their policy for international applicants.
