## What Campus Reviews Reveal

Middle East Technical University (METU), ranked 269 globally by QS, is a public research university in Ankara, Türkiye. Founded in 1956, it focuses on engineering, natural sciences, and graduate programs. With a Google rating of 4.6 from 446 reviews, the campus experience is generally positive, though some issues surface. A reviewer named Ns MS wrote: “Best campus ever in ankara and Türkiye to be honest.” MUHAMMED ALREFAEI echoed that sentiment, describing it as “a public research university located in Ankara … established with the aim of providing a high-quality education in engineering and natural sciences.”

However, not all feedback is glowing. Muhammad Talha gave a neutral 3-star review, stating: “Middle East Technical University is a public technical university … emphasizing research and education in engineering and natural sciences, offering about 41 undergraduate programs within 5 faculties, 105 masters and 70 doctorate programs within 5 graduate scho[ols].” This factual description hints that the academic offering is solid, but the review lacks the enthusiasm of the 5-star ones. A more critical voice came from saadoon eyada, who rated the university 1 star: “I tried to visit the university and meet one of the professors … but unfortunately they didn’t let me enter the university. My question is why? Is it university or prison? I don’t advise to study it this university.” This reflects strict campus access policies that may affect visitors or prospective students.

For students planning to live near METU, these reviews highlight two things: the campus is well-regarded for its environment, but access restrictions mean you’ll likely need to arrange meetings with faculty in advance. As a student, you will be on campus daily, so the security and tranquility are benefits, not barriers.

## City Context: Ankara

Ankara is the capital of Türkiye, located in the central Anatolian region. It is a planned city with wide boulevards, government buildings, and a mix of modern and historical districts. The university’s address is Üniversiteler, Dumlupınar Blv. 1/6 D:133, 06800 Çankaya/Ankara. This area is in the Çankaya district, known for being an upscale residential and academic zone. Many embassies, foreign missions, and families live here, so the neighborhood is safe and relatively green.

Public transport in Ankara involves buses, metro (Ankaray and Batıkent lines), and minibuses (dolmuş). The closest metro station to METU is METU Station on the Metro line (M1), which connects to the city center (Kızılay) in about 20–30 minutes. The campus itself is large (about 11,000 acres), so internal shuttle buses are needed to get between faculties.

The cost of living in Ankara is lower than in Istanbul, but higher than in smaller cities. Rents have been rising in recent years due to inflation, so budget planning is essential. As of late 2025, a one-bedroom apartment near the university could range from 8,000 to 15,000 Turkish Lira per month, while shared accommodation can be cheaper. For international students, payments are often made in Turkish Lira, so currency fluctuations matter.

## Walk vs Transit Trade-off

METU’s campus is spread out, but the main academic buildings and dormitories are clustered. The trade-off between walking distance and taking public transport is clear:

- **Walking**: Living in the Üniversiteler neighborhood (the street where the university is located) or in nearby Oran (1–2 km away) means you can walk to the main gate in 10–20 minutes. Inside, you might need the campus shuttle or a bicycle to reach your department. Walking saves on transport costs and is reliable, but rents in this zone are higher and units fill fast. Some residences are directly across the boulevard, making the walk extremely convenient.
- **Transit**: Living further out in places like Kızılay, Kavaklıdere, or even Batıkent means you’ll take the metro or bus to METU station. Travel time from Kızılay is about 25 minutes by metro, plus waiting. The monthly transport pass for students costs around 400–500 TL (as of 2026, indicative). The trade-off is lower rent (you can find a private room in Kızılay for 6,000–8,000 TL) but you lose flexibility if classes end late or on weekends when metro frequency decreases. Also, during peak hours, buses can be crowded.

For most students, the sweet spot is within a 15-minute walk or a short bus/metro ride (1–2 stops). That keeps you in the Üniversiteler, Bilkent, or Oran districts. If you prefer the city buzz, Kızılay offers more cafes and nightlife, but you’ll commute daily.

3. **Compare rent-to-commute cost**: A rent of 10,000 TL in a walking-distance flat versus 7,000 TL in a flat 5 km away plus 500 TL monthly transport gives a net saving of 2,500 TL. However, the time saved (30 minutes daily) might be worth the higher rent. Calculate how many hours you’d spend commuting per semester: 2 hours/day × 5 days × 15 weeks = 150 hours. 4. **Verify amenities**: Check if utilities (electricity, water, gas, internet) are included in the rent. In Ankara, winter heating is significant (natural gas bill can be 500–1,000 TL per month in a mid-sized flat). Some dorms bundle all utilities. Also, ask about security, laundry facilities, and access to kitchen if needed.
5. **Read reviews**: Use platforms like Google Maps or student forums to see recent reviews of specific buildings. Look for complaints about noise, landlord responsiveness, or internet speed. Since the data provided does not include specific residence reviews, you should read 5–10 reviews per property before deciding.
6. **Compare multiple options**: Create a shortlist of 3–5 places. Contact each operator and ask for a virtual tour if you are not in Ankara. Some operators may ask for a deposit (typically one month rent) to reserve a room.
7. **Consider the contract length**: Most student leases run from September to June. Make sure you can extend if you plan to stay for summer courses. Some dorms close during summer, forcing you to find temporary accommodation.

### 2. Is it better to live in a dormitory or a private apartment?

That depends on your need for privacy and social life. Dormitories often include meals, cleaning, and arranged social activities, which can simplify life for first-year students. Private apartments offer more independence and quiet for studying, but you have to manage cooking, bills, and maintenance. The campus’s own dormitories are particularly convenient because they are inside the gate, but they fill up early — apply as soon as the application window opens.

### 3. Can I walk from the university to the nearest metro station?

Yes. The METU metro station is located just outside the main campus entrance on Dumlupınar Boulevard. Walking from most academic buildings to the station takes 10–20 minutes depending on your location within the campus. Inside, you can also use the free campus shuttle or bring a bicycle. If you live near that station, your commute is essentially eliminated.
