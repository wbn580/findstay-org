Because rental prices fluctuate with demand and season, always confirm the exact weekly rate and any extra fees directly with the accommodation provider before signing a lease.

## Walking vs. Transit: What Suits You?

The Clayton campus sits on a large, well-maintained site with many academic buildings, libraries, and recreational facilities. Living within walking distance — roughly 1–2 km — gives you the shortest commute, often under 15 minutes on foot. Properties in this ring include student accommodation on or adjacent to campus and some private apartments along Wellington Road.

For those who prefer a wider choice or cheaper rent, the 601 shuttle bus connects Clayton train station to the campus every few minutes during peak hours (as noted in the review by Arad). Many students live in suburbs like Oakleigh, Huntingdale, or even further west along the train line. A train ride from central Melbourne takes about 30–40 minutes to Clayton station, followed by the shuttle bus: total door-to-door time from the city can be 45–60 minutes. The trade-off is lower rent against longer travel time.

If you have classes at both Clayton and Caulfield campuses, living near a railway station that connects both (e.g., Caulfield station is on the same line) can be efficient. The shuttle bus network within Clayton campus also covers the major car parks and residential colleges.

## How to Shortlist Your Accommodation

Because no single accommodation type suits everyone, use the following logic to narrow your options:

1. **Set your maximum commute time.** If you cannot afford to lose more than 15 minutes each way, focus on properties within a 1 km radius of the Clayton campus. If 30–40 minutes is acceptable, widen your search to suburbs served by the 601 bus or along the Pakenham/Cranbourne train line.
2. **Determine your budget band.** Use the indicative ranges above and filter listings by weekly rent. Remember to account for utility bills (electricity, gas, water) and internet if not included.
3. **Choose between managed accommodation and private rental.** Managed student apartments (often called purpose-built student accommodation or PBSA) usually include all bills, furnishings, and a support team on site. Private rentals (apartments or houses shared with other students) may be cheaper but require you to arrange utilities and possibly buy furniture.
4. **Inspect the transport links.** Check walking distance to the nearest bus stop, train station, or shuttle stop. Use maps to calculate the actual walking time rather than relying on straight-line distance.
5. **Read recent student reviews.** The reviews quoted above reflect real experiences — look for patterns about noise, management responsiveness, and safety.
6. **Verify contract flexibility.** Some residences offer 6‑month or 12‑month leases; others allow semester‑by‑semester arrangements. If your study plans are uncertain, choose a shorter-term option.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**1. How do I get from Melbourne Airport to Monash Clayton?**  
Take the SkyBus to Southern Cross Station, then a train on the Pakenham or Cranbourne line to Clayton station. From there, the 601 shuttle bus runs to the campus. The total journey takes about 60–90 minutes depending on connections. Alternatively, rideshares cost more but are door-to-door.

**2. Do I need a car if I live near Monash Clayton?**  
No. The campus is well served by public transport, and most daily needs (groceries, pharmacies, cafes) are within walking distance of the university or the Clayton shopping strip. Many students get by without a car, using the free campus shuttle and the train for longer trips.

**3. What is the cheapest accommodation option for a Monash student?**  
The most affordable option is usually a shared house or apartment a 20–30 minute bus or train ride away, where you rent a single room in a multi‑tenant property. Confirm the exact weekly rate with the landlord, and note that shared utility bills can add to the cost.
