## Housing Near National University of Singapore (NUS) – A 2026 Guide

## What Campus Reviews Reveal

The campus environment at NUS is consistently described as clean, well-maintained, and vibrant. Student and visitor reviews on Google (4.5 stars from 2,385 ratings) highlight both the strengths and challenges of the university experience.

One reviewer, Sayhan, gave a 5-star rating and noted: “Very clean and well maintained. I went there for a seminar on 30th May about Machining Learning and AI at NUS School of Computing. The event was held at COM 1 building and was well organised by both NUS and EMERITUS.” This points to strong organisation and modern facilities, particularly at the School of Computing.

Another visitor, G Y, described the Open House at University Town (UTown) as “an extremely vibrant and enjoyable experience” with “so many activities and booths around.” This suggests that the central campus areas are lively and well-equipped for events.

However, not all feedback is positive. George gave a 1-star rating and complained: “Your application website is trash. I don’t know who wrote your website but the person did not pass computer science for sure. Your website logs out every 30 minutes and the problem is you don’t save the progress that students have made.” This indicates that the online application process can be frustrating, a practical consideration for incoming students.

Chandan Kumar, rating 4 stars, observed: “Amazing place for students worldwide. Top notch facilities for everything you care about. Really interesting lot of students to hang around. You’ll love this space, the Campus is attracted all over the city of Singapore.” He also noted a challenge: “For vegetarians, it’s a bit tricky to get the desired food.” This real-world insight matters for students with specific dietary needs.

Overall, the campus earns high marks for cleanliness, facilities, and community atmosphere. The main drawback raised is the website usability during applications, which is worth planning around. When choosing housing, these on-campus qualities suggest that proximity to UTown and the School of Computing corridors could be valuable.

## Singapore City Context

![National University of Singapore (NUS)](https://img.unistay.net/housing/p/amber-3125178.jpg)

Singapore is a compact, well-connected city-state. NUS is located at 21 Lower Kent Ridge Rd, in the southwestern part of the island. The campus is roughly 8 km from the city centre (Raffles Place) and 20–25 km from Changi Airport.

Public transport dominates daily commuting. Singapore’s Mass Rapid Transit (MRT) system includes stations near NUS: Kent Ridge MRT (Circle Line) is at the university’s main gate, and Clementi MRT (East-West Line) is a short bus ride from the campus. Buses serve most campus areas with high frequency.

The rental market in Singapore is regulated and competitive. For the 2026 academic year, indicative prices for rooms in shared apartments near NUS start from 207 SGD per week, though final amounts vary by property condition, location, and lease terms. Budget-conscious students should expect to pay more for single rooms or studios in newer buildings.

## Budget Bands for 2026

When budgeting for housing near NUS in 2026, consider these indicative weekly rent ranges (confirm with the operator):

- **Entry-level (207–350 SGD/week):** Typically a room in a shared HDB flat or older condo. Shared facilities include kitchen, bathroom, and living area. These are often located a 20–30 minute commute from campus.
- **Mid-range (350–500 SGD/week):** A private room in a newer condo or serviced apartment. Usually closer to campus, with a 10–20 minute commute. Some include utilities or cleaning services.
- **Premium (500+ SGD/week):** A studio or one-bedroom unit in a modern building near the university. Commute under 10 minutes. Many include gyms, pools, and concierge services.

For students on a tight budget, sharing a room in a public housing area (like Clementi or Queenstown) can reduce costs, but availability for international students is limited by regulations.

## Walk vs Transit Trade-Off

NUS is spread across multiple sub-campuses: Kent Ridge (main), Bukit Timah (law), and the Duke-NUS Medical School near Outram. The following comparison applies to the main Kent Ridge campus:

### Walking (0–15 minutes)

- **Pros:** No transport cost; predictable travel time; easy access to campus facilities late at night.
- **Cons:** Limited housing stock; higher rent per square foot; less choice of accommodation types.
- **Example areas:** Kent Ridge, Pasir Panjang, Clementi (nearest housing estates). Walking from Clementi MRT to the Engineering faculty takes about 20 minutes, so “walking distance” usually means within 1 km of the campus perimeter.

### Public Transit (15–40 minutes)

- **Pros:** Wider rental options; lower rent compared to within walking distance; access to more amenities (shopping, dining).
- **Cons:** Monthly transport cost (approx. 80–120 SGD for a monthly pass); waiting time; occasional crowding during peak hours.
- **Example areas:** Queenstown, Buona Vista, Holland Village, Tiong Bahru, or even central locations like Newton or Novena (40-minute commute).

### Which to Choose?

If you plan to spend most time on campus (library, labs, clubs) and value convenience, prioritise a place within walking distance or a short bus ride (15 minutes). If you prefer a quieter neighbourhood or a larger room, trade a longer commute for lower rent. NUS runs internal shuttle buses, so living near a shuttle stop can also reduce walk time.
