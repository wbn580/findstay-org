## City Context: Pohang as a Student Destination

Pohang is a coastal industrial city in Gyeongsangbuk-do, about a 3-hour train ride from Seoul. It is home to POSCO, one of the world’s largest steelmakers, giving the city a distinct industrial character combined with seaside charm. The city centre and campus area are compact; most student necessities — supermarkets, cafes, restaurants, and public transport — are within a manageable radius.

Living costs in Pohang are lower than in Seoul or Busan. A typical monthly rent for a single room near campus ranges from approximately 300,000 KRW to 600,000 KRW, though prices can vary. All figures cited in this article are indicative — confirm with the operator. Utilities and internet add around 50,000–100,000 KRW per month. Compared to the capital, you can get a larger room or a better location for the same budget.

The university sits at 77 Cheongam-ro, Nam-gu, about a 20-minute bus ride from Pohang Station and the central business district. The immediate neighbourhood is student-oriented, with many housing options adapted for POSTECH undergraduates and graduate students.

### On-Campus Dormitories

- **Shared room (2–4 persons)**: 200,000–400,000 KRW per month. Includes basic furniture, internet, and sometimes meals. Applications open early each semester; demand is high, so apply as soon as the portal opens.
- **Single room (limited)**: 400,000–550,000 KRW per month. More privacy, but fewer spots. Often reserved for graduate students.

### Off-Campus Private Rentals (within 15-minute walk)

- **Goshiwon (small study room)**: 250,000–400,000 KRW per month. Very compact, often with a desk, bed, and small fridge. Common among Korean students, but can feel cramped.
- **One-room (studio)**: 400,000–700,000 KRW per month. A separate kitchen and bathroom, more space. Key money (deposit) usually 5–10 million KRW, but some landlords accept lower deposits for foreign students.
- **Two-room (shared with flatmate)**: 300,000–500,000 KRW per person per month. Splitting a larger apartment reduces living costs and provides a social environment.

### Areas Further Out (bus commute 20–30 minutes)

- **Jukdo-dong / Hang-gu area**: 250,000–450,000 KRW per month for a one-room. Quieter, but you’ll need a bus to campus. Slightly cheaper than closer zones.

## Walk vs. Transit: How to Choose Your Location

POSTECH’s campus is situated on a slight hill. The core academic buildings, library, and student union are clustered together. Many students prefer to live within a 10–15-minute walk to avoid the morning rush and save on bus fare. Walking routes in the neighbourhood (Cheongam-ro, Jigok-dong) are safe and well-lit at night.

**Walkable area (≤1.5 km from main gate)**:

- Pros: No need for a bus pass; can return to your room between classes; less dependent on bus schedules.
- Cons: Rental prices tend to be slightly higher; the immediate area has limited late-night entertainment options (though cafes and convenience stores are abundant).

**Transit area (bus route 170, 200, or 500 to campus)**:

- Pros: More affordable rooms in residential neighbourhoods like Yangdeok-dong or Yeoram-dong; access to larger supermarkets and a wider variety of restaurants.
- Cons: Bus frequency drops after 10 pm; a monthly bus pass costs about 55,000 KRW (indicative — confirm with the operator). In bad weather, waiting for a bus can be inconvenient.

**Trade-off rule of thumb**: If you have early morning labs or late study sessions, choose walking distance. If you prefer a quieter residential setting and don’t mind the bus, you can save 10–20% on rent.

## Shortlist Logic: Selecting the Right Residence Near POSTECH

When building your shortlist of off-campus housing options, consider these criteria:

1. **Distance to campus**: Filter by walking time ≤10 minutes for convenience, or ≤20 minutes by bus if budget is tight.
2. **Security and maintenance**: Look for buildings with a CCTV, intercom, or a management office. Goshiwon often have shared bathrooms, while officetels provide in-unit facilities.
3. **Contract flexibility**: Many landlords in Pohang offer 6-month or 12-month leases. Exchange students may prefer short-term contracts; some real estate agents specialise in temporary rentals.
4. **Included utilities**: Check whether water, electricity, gas, and internet are included in the rent. Goshiwon often bundle everything; one-rooms usually require separate payments.
5. **Noise and environment**: Visit the unit at different times of day. Close to bars or construction can disturb study.

**Example shortlist process** (generic — no specific operator names):

- Step 1: Decide your maximum monthly rent (e.g., 500,000 KRW including utilities).
- Step 2: Use a map to identify all buildings within 15 minutes’ walk of Cheongam-ro.
- Step 3: Contact three rental agencies or directly walk to buildings with “for rent” signs. Ask about deposit amount (jeonse) vs monthly rent.
- Step 4: Inspect at least two options. Confirm wifi speed — POSTECH campus has strong coverage, but off-campus connections vary.
- Step 5: Sign a contract in Korean (with an English translation if available). Ensure you receive a receipt for the deposit.

Note: The university’s international office can provide a list of verified accommodations or connect you with current students who are subletting. This is a free service — take advantage of it.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**1. When should I apply for on-campus housing for 2026–2027?**  
On-campus dormitory applications usually open in late November (for the spring semester starting in March) and in late May (for the fall semester starting in September). Specific dates are released on the POSTECH housing portal. Demand is high, so submit your application within the first 48 hours of the window. Some rooms are reserved for international exchange students, but you still need to apply early.

**2. How much deposit do I need for off-campus private housing in Pohang?**  
Deposits vary widely. For a one-room, expect to pay 5–10 million KRW (indicative — confirm with the operator). Some landlords accept 3 million KRW if you provide a guarantor (e.g., the university or a Korean friend). Goshiwon typically require no deposit or a very small one (100,000–300,000 KRW). Always get a written receipt and contract.

**3. Is it better to live on campus or off campus for a one-semester exchange?**  
For a single semester, on-campus dormitories are simpler — you pay one fee and have everything included. However, if you want more independence and the ability to cook for yourself, a short-term off-campus rental (month-to-month) can work. Many landlords near POSTECH are used to exchange students. Goshiwon offer the shortest commitment (minimum 1 month). However, confirm that the room is available for exactly your semester dates, as some contracts require a full 6-month minimum.
