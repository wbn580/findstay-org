## What Campus Reviews Reveal

Politecnico di Milano's Google rating (4.4, 1795 reviews) looks good on the surface, but some recent reviews reveal students' true feelings about the campus experience. Buildings and infrastructure are common sources of dissatisfaction. A user who gave a 1-star rating, Blevins Patten, wrote: *“A disastrous experience, to say the least, and a complete lack of respect for students. Calling this facility a ‘university’ is an insult to true academia. Let’s start with the infrastructure: the classrooms are dilapidated, ancient, depressing, and blatantly inadequate for a modern learning environ”* Although the review is strongly worded, it reflects some students' disappointment with the physical condition of teaching spaces. If you plan to rent near campus, classroom condition may not directly affect your housing choice, but commute time and campus distribution are more critical.

Another 3-star review from Darya Dugina points to teaching style: *“Why do professors here try to overcomplicate simple subjects to the point where you lose points because you drew a short line instead of a dot in a drawing, and at the same time oversimplify things so much that you understand what the professor means in five minutes but are still forced to sit there”* This suggests that engineering and design courses have strict detail requirements, which may mean you need to set aside ample quiet study time in your accommodation.

The most valuable reference is a 2-star review from Serhat Ardıç, who compared the two campuses: *“Welcome to Milan! If you are a new international student at Polimi, a great way to kick off your university life is by visiting the Bovisa and Leonardo campuses. Personally, I find Leonardo far superior due to its greenery and central location. Seeing the blue ‘Benvenuti il Politecnico’ sign is an e…”* Despite the low rating, the review directly points out the core difference between the two campuses—the main Leonardo campus (Piazza Leonardo da Vinci) is in central Milan, with plenty of greenery and convenient transport; the Bovisa campus is more industrial, quieter but with relatively limited surrounding amenities. This difference is a primary factor in determining housing priorities.

## Milan's Urban Background

Politecnico di Milano ranks 98th in the 2026 QS World University Rankings, and is the highest-ranked technical university in Italy. The main campus address is Piazza Leonardo da Vinci, 32, adjacent to Milan's Città Studi university district, within walking distance of the Piola station on the green metro line (M2). Milan is Italy's largest industrial and financial center, with a public transport network consisting of metro, trams, and buses covering a wide area.

For international students, the cost of living in Milan is above average in Europe; a single room rent is typically 500–900 EUR/month (2026–2027 academic year data, *indicative — confirm with the operator*), and sharing can reduce costs.

Since Polimi's courses may be distributed across the Leonardo and Bovisa campuses (depending on the program), housing choices should prioritize the campus where classes are held, or choose an area with convenient transport between the two campuses. Milan's M3 yellow line connects Bovisa to the central station, while the M2 green line covers the Leonardo area. Bovisa station (Passante railway and M3 yellow line) is about 20–25 minutes by metro from the Leonardo campus.

## Budget Range

Below are typical budget ranges for student accommodation in Milan for the 2026–2027 academic year, prices are *indicative — confirm with the operator*:

- **Shared room in a shared apartment**: 450–700 EUR/month, usually includes utilities and internet. Common in Città Studi or Porta Romana areas.
- **Private room in shared apartment**: 550–900 EUR/month, depending on size and renovation level. Options for 500–750 EUR can be found near Bovisa.
- **Studio / one-bedroom apartment**: 800–1,300 EUR/month, difficult to find below 900 EUR near core campuses.
- **Residence hall**: 600–1,100 EUR/month, includes furniture, cleaning, and sometimes meals. Application needs to be submitted several months in advance.

Note: Milan municipality charges a registration fee (contratto di affitto registration), usually borne by the landlord, but it is recommended to confirm before signing. Also, the deposit is typically 3 months' rent, which can be used to offset the last month's bill.

## Walking and Transport Trade-offs

Choosing to live within walking distance of campus saves time, but costs are usually higher. Around the Leonardo campus (e.g., Piazza Leonardo da Vinci, Viale Romagna, Via Ponzio), accommodation within a 5–10 minute walk typically has single room rents above 700 EUR. If the budget is limited, consider areas 15–20 minutes walk from the core (e.g., Lambrate or the edge of Città Studi), where prices can drop to 500–600 EUR.

The Bovisa campus is different: the area itself is not the busiest commercial district, but accommodation offers good value. Within a 10–15 minute walk, shared or single rooms can be found for 450–650 EUR. However, if most of your classes are at the Leonardo campus, daily round-trip commuting will take about 40–50 minutes (metro + walking). Recommended trade-off conditions:

- If most classes are at Leonardo, prioritize areas along the M2 green line (e.g., Piola, Loreto, Udine stations).
- If classes are mainly at Bovisa, then living near Bovisa station (within 10 minutes walk) or along the M3 yellow line (Zara, Maciachini stations) will save time.

Serhat Ardıç's review mentions Leonardo campus's 'central location' and 'greenery', indicating greater daily convenience. For grocery shopping, socializing, and other needs, living near Leonardo is obviously more convenient. But the cost is higher rent, and you need to adapt to the dilapidated classrooms—if you are sensitive to the 'dilapidated' environment mentioned by Blevins Patten, perhaps a spacious and comfortable home can better balance the negative experience.
