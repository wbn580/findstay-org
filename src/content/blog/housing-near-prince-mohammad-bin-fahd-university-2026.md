## What campus reviews reveal about living near PMU

Student reviews of Prince Mohammad Bin Fahd University (PMU) highlight specific issues that directly affect housing decisions. One reviewer, **mia mia**, gave a 1-star rating and wrote: “RUN!!! horrible scheduling, they do not care about the professors or the students . the administration sucks, they wont help you with anything especially if you’re a foreigner. they will waste your time in scheduling and never actually help. they don’t even guide the students about their own degree .” This suggests that international students may face administrative hurdles, so living close to campus can reduce wasted commuting time when scheduling issues arise.

Another 1-star review from **night** notes: “The female campus lacks proper seating areas for students to rest or relax between classes, majority of them very uncomfortable. There are no comfortable spaces to sit for long periods, which is disappointing, especially for a well known private university.” This indicates that on-campus rest areas are limited, so having a nearby apartment with a comfortable lounge becomes more important for female students.

A positive review from **Eram Buttar** (5-star) states: “PMU is the most prestigious private university in Saudi Arabia and they prepare their students to make a brilliant career after graduating from here.” While this doesn’t directly mention housing, it implies that students value a focused academic environment, which a quiet, convenient home supports.

Finally, **Dana Salameh** (4-star) comments: “Aint that bad of a place tbh, but to all the girls that judge what others are doing or wearing pls mind ur own business and stop being a pick-me, not being a girls girl is cringe. And to all the weird male students that harass girls pls stop ur being disgusting and no one is into that, thank you.” This points to social dynamics on campus; living off-campus in a secure, well-managed building can provide a refuge from unwanted attention.

## City context: Khobar and the Eastern Province

PMU is located in Al Khobar, a major city in Saudi Arabia’s Eastern Province. The university’s address is 617, Al Jawharah, Khobar, الظهران 34754. Khobar is part of the Dammam metropolitan area, which also includes Dhahran and Dammam. The city is known for its modern infrastructure, shopping malls, and the Corniche along the Arabian Gulf.

Housing in Khobar ranges from traditional apartment buildings to modern compounds catering to expatriates and students. Because PMU is a private university with a relatively high tuition (though not mentioned here), many students are from middle- to upper-income families, and the surrounding housing market reflects this. Rent prices are generally moderate compared to Riyadh or Jeddah, but still significant for students on a budget.

Public transportation is limited in Khobar. Most residents rely on private cars or ride-hailing services (Uber, Careem). There is a bus network operated by the local government, but it may not cover all areas efficiently. For students without a car, living within walking distance or a short ride from the university is a major advantage.

Note that utilities (electricity, water, internet) are usually not included; budget an additional SAR 300–500 per month.

## Walk versus transit trade-off

Living within walking distance of PMU is the most convenient option, especially given the limited public transit and potential scheduling problems mentioned in reviews. A 5–10 minute walk eliminates reliance on taxis, saves time, and avoids the hassle of finding parking. However, apartments in the immediate vicinity tend to be more expensive (high budget band). For female students, the review about uncomfortable campus seating also suggests that a short walk home between classes can provide a break.

For those on a tighter budget, walking farther (15–20 minutes) or using a bicycle can reduce rent significantly. Bicycle parking on campus should be confirmed. Alternatively, using ride-hailing services like Uber or Careem for a 5–10 minute ride costs roughly SAR 10–20 one way. Doing this twice a day adds about SAR 400–600 per month, which should be factored into the total housing cost.

Students with a car have the most freedom to choose cheaper housing farther from campus, but must consider fuel, parking (if charged), and traffic. PMU’s location in Al Jawharah district is near major roads, so driving times of 10–15 minutes can open up more affordable areas like Al Khobar’s older neighbourhoods or even parts of Dammam.
