### City Context: Riyadh

Riyadh is the capital of Saudi Arabia, a sprawling desert metropolis with a growing public transport system. The city is car-oriented, but recent expansions of the Riyadh Metro and buses have improved connectivity. The King Salman Neighborhood, where PSU is located, is an established residential area with a mix of villas, apartment complexes, and some commercial services. ### Budget Bands

Rental prices in Riyadh are typically quoted in Saudi Riyals (SAR) per year. Below are indicative budget bands for student accommodation near PSU. Always confirm with the landlord or operator, as rates change frequently.

- **Budget (SAR 15,000–25,000 per year)**: This range usually covers a seat in a shared apartment (shared bedroom with others) or a small room in a shared villa. Internet and utilities may or may not be included. These options are often farther from campus, requiring a bus or car commute.
- **Mid-range (SAR 25,000–40,000 per year)**: Private studios or one-bedroom apartments in older buildings within a 15–30 minute walk or a short bus ride. You get your own space but modest furnishings. Some compounds in this price range include basic amenities like a gym or parking.
- **Premium (SAR 40,000–60,000 per year)**: Modern one-bedroom or two-bedroom apartments in newer buildings within walking distance (10–15 minutes) of campus. These often come with full kitchens, in-unit laundry, and building security. A few upscale compounds in the King Salman area may charge higher, but provide pools and 24-hour maintenance.
- **Luxury (above SAR 60,000 per year)**: Villas or large apartments for students who want to split with several housemates. Usually located in exclusive compounds with high-end finishes. Not necessary for most students, but available.

*All figures are indicative—confirm with the operator. Expect to pay a deposit (often one month’s rent) and possibly agency fees.*

### Walk vs Transit Trade-off

Your choice between living within walking distance or relying on transit depends on your tolerance for heat, study habits, and budget.

**Walking distance (up to 1.5 km or about 15–20 minutes)**

- Pros: No commute cost, flexible schedule, easy to return to campus for evening study or events. Students can take advantage of the campus grounds for exercise, as noted in reviews.
- Cons: Rent premium; units near PSU are more expensive. Also, Riyadh summers (May–September) can exceed 45°C, making even a short walk uncomfortable. You would need to plan your walks early morning or late evening.
- Best for: Students who value time and have a mid-range to premium budget.

![Prince Sultan University](https://img.unistay.cn/housing/p/amber-1309962.jpg)

**Transit (bus or car, commute 20–45 minutes)**

- Pros: Lower rent for more space. The Riyadh bus network and metro (some lines are operational as of 2026) can connect distant neighbourhoods to PSU. A car gives freedom but adds fuel, parking, and maintenance costs.
- Cons: Time lost in traffic; dependent on schedules or parking availability. Reviews mention challenging workload, so a long commute reduces study and rest time.
- Best for: Budget-conscious students who don’t mind an extra hour daily travel.

For international students without a car, check if your accommodation is near a bus stop that serves the route to PSU. Ride-hailing apps (like Uber and Careem) are widely available in Riyadh and cost roughly SAR 15–30 for a 10–15 minute trip, but daily use adds up.

### Shortlist Logic Using Nearby Residences

Since no specific nearby residence names were provided in the data, we outline a logical approach to building your shortlist using general property types available in the King Salman Neighborhood and surrounding areas. You can apply this logic with any listing you find on housing platforms.

1. **Distance first**: Filter properties within 2 km of PSU’s address (شارع الامير ناصر بن فرحان). Use Google Maps to measure walking distance. Prioritise units that are on the same side of major roads to avoid unsafe crossings.
2. **Reviews of the building**: Look for verified tenant reviews that mention noise, maintenance responsiveness, and safety. A high Google rating for a building (e.g., 4.0+) is a good signal.
3. **Amenities for study**: Seek apartments with a desk area, reliable Wi-Fi (check if the building provides fibre), and 24-hour electricity backup. Reviews from PSU students highlight a heavy workload; a quiet, dedicated study space is essential.
4. **Cost vs. included services**: Ask whether water, electricity, and internet are included in the rent. In Riyadh, summer electricity bills can be high if the unit is poorly insulated. All-inclusive rent reduces monthly surprises.
5. **Roommates**: If you choose a shared apartment, confirm the number of housemates and their schedule. Align with people who also have a demanding academic routine.
6. **Safety and gender-specific housing**: Saudi Arabia has gender-segregated buildings in many residential areas. Verify if the building is for families or bachelors/female-only, especially if you are a single student.

For example, a typical good shortlist might include:

- A studio in a compound 1.2 km east of campus, SAR 30,000/year, all utilities included, with 4.3 Google rating from 50 reviews.
- A shared room in a villa 0.8 km north, SAR 18,000/year, but only water included (electricity extra), 3.8 rating.
- A one-bedroom apartment in a high-rise 2.5 km away (metro station nearby), SAR 35,000/year, electricity and water paid separately, 4.1 rating.

Compare the trade-offs: the studio is priciest but saves commute stress; the shared room is cheapest but requires more coordination; the high-rise offers modern amenities with a metro ride.
