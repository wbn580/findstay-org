## Campus Reviews Reveal: Princeton University’s Environment

Princeton University has a score of 4.7 on Google (2,058 reviews), and many visitors and community members highlight the beauty and historic feel of its campus. One reviewer wrote: “Princeton University is one of the most beautiful campuses I’ve seen. There is a sense of awe at the centuries-old buildings juxtaposed with new construction. You can feel history as you walk the grounds of this incredible campus.” (Jennifer Doyle, one month ago) Another alum’s spouse described: “God I love this place and the reunions are insane.” (James VanRensselaer, three weeks ago)

These comments show that the campus itself is an important factor in choosing housing—it is both a place to study and a space suitable for walking and relaxing. KGQ NYC commented five years ago: “Gorgeous campus for a peaceful stroll. A few gentle hills and plenty of beauty all around. … Many excellent food and shopping choices in town. Definitely one of the most beautiful American college campuses.”

From the reviews, it can be inferred that housing within walking distance of campus allows students and staff to make fuller use of campus facilities and the conveniences of the surrounding town.

## City Background: Princeton Town

Princeton is a small town in New Jersey, and the university is almost integrated into the town center. The town has independent dining, shopping, and cultural venues, such as the art museum and gift shop mentioned in the reviews. According to one visitor: “There is a gift shop as well. I recommend !” (Alexandra Borisevich, three years ago)

Because the university is ranked 25th in the world (QS 2027 data), Princeton attracts scholars and students from around the globe. The town itself is not large, and most daily needs can be met within walking distance or a short bike ride. However, if you need to go to New York or Philadelphia, you can take NJ Transit trains (Princeton Junction station). This duality—a quiet campus town plus transportation connections to big cities—determines the main variable in housing choice: whether to prioritize walking convenience or accept a commute in exchange for lower rent or more space.

## Walking and Transportation Trade-offs

Princeton town is a classic example of a “walkable campus”. Reviews mention “a few gentle hills and plenty of beauty all around” (KGQ NYC), indicating a pleasant walking experience. Living in the town center or on the edge of campus allows you to walk to class, the library, restaurants, and the supermarket without needing to drive every day. However, housing that is convenient for walking usually has higher rents and low vacancy rates.

If you are willing to accept a 10–15 minute drive (or take the university’s free shuttle), you can move into residential areas like West Windsor, which have multi-family houses and apartment communities that may be more affordable. Additionally, Princeton Junction station provides frequent train service for those who need to commute to New York or Philadelphia—but for daily classes, you still need the bus or a bicycle for the connection.

Key trade-offs:

1. **Within walking radius**: Ideal, but competitive; start looking for housing 3–4 months in advance.
2. **Bicycle/bus commute**: Lower cost, but you need to adapt to weather and schedule.
3. **Car commute**: Requires consideration of parking permits (university parking is limited and paid) and fuel costs.
