### City Context: Nijmegen

Nijmegen is the oldest city in the Netherlands, located in the eastern part of the country near the German border. It has a strong student population, largely due to Radboud University and HAN University of Applied Sciences. The city centre is compact and walkable, with a mix of historic buildings, shops, restaurants, and green spaces.

From the reviews, we know:

- The city centre is “very near” the university (AKS review).
- A large Albert Heijn XL supermarket is located nearby, which is useful for groceries.
- The university medical school is famous, drawing many healthcare students.

Nijmegen is known for its bicycle-friendly infrastructure. Most students cycle to campus, but walking is also feasible if you live in the immediate neighbourhoods such as **Heyendaal** (where the campus is situated), **Galgenveld**, or **Stadscentrum**. Public transport includes buses and a train station (Nijmegen Heyendaal) that connects to the main station and other cities.

The city’s atmosphere is relatively relaxed compared to Randstad cities like Amsterdam or Utrecht. Living costs – including rent – are generally lower, though prices have risen in recent years due to housing shortages in Dutch student cities.

### Walk vs Transit Trade-off

The campus is located in the Heyendaal district, about 1.5–2 km from the city centre. According to the review by AKS, the city centre is “very near.” Walking takes roughly 15–20 minutes from the centre to the main university buildings. Cycling is the standard mode: 5–10 minutes. Buses connect the central station to the campus (lines 2, 10, 33, 300) in about 10 minutes, but most students prefer to bike for cost and convenience.

**If you live close to campus (Heyendaal, Galgenveld):** You can walk to lectures, the library, and the Albert Heijn XL. Rent may be slightly higher, but you save on transport time and costs.

**If you live in the city centre or beyond (Stadscentrum, Bottendaal, Wolfskuil):** You will be closer to nightlife, shops, and the main train station. Commuting to campus by bike takes 10–20 minutes. Walking is possible but less common for distances over 1.5 km.

**If you live in outer neighbourhoods (Lindenholt, Dukenburg) or neighbouring towns (Groesbeek, Beek):** You will need a bike or bus, and travel times can be 20–40 minutes. Rent may be lower, but factor in transport costs and schedule constraints.

Because Nijmegen is relatively small, most students find a bike adequate. The trade-off often comes down to budget: cheaper outer areas require more commuting time, while campus-adjacent housing is pricier but more convenient.
