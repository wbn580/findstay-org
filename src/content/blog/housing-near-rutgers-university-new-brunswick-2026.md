## What Campus Reviews Reveal

Rutgers University – New Brunswick is the flagship public research university of New Jersey, ranked 328 in the QS World University Rankings. It has a Google composite rating of 4.4 (from 925 reviews), with student feedback ranging from high praise to sharp criticism. Understanding these voices can help future tenants more clearly assess how well their housing needs match campus life.

Citation from alumnus Johnathan Kraus (5 stars): “I was very fortunate to be admitted to Rutgers University and graduate in 2006. It truly became one of the most influential experiences of my life. As an undergraduate living on campus, Rutgers shaped me personally, professionally, and socially in ways I never anticipated.” This narrative of holistic development suggests a high correlation between campus life and the living experience—living within walking distance or a short bus ride facilitates participation in courses, clubs, and social activities.

Another user, Raju Singh (5 stars), notes: “The best university in New Jersey, and also one of the top public institutions in the country. It is huge, with 5 different campuses in the New Brunswick/Piscataway area. There are many great days on College Ave and Douglass, and there is a shuttle service connecting each campus, each offering different things.” This statement directly highlights a key variable in housing decisions: the multi-campus layout means that “where you live” directly determines your daily commute pattern. If your main classes are on College Avenue, living close to that campus saves time; if your classes are spread across campuses like Busch, Livingston, Cook/Douglass, the shuttle system becomes critical.

But not all voices are positive. User Neha Desai (1 stars) writes: “It is regrettable to see their stance on DEI (diversity, equity, and inclusion) and the dissolution of related programs. I had considered applying for a graduate program, but I will apply elsewhere. As a school with such a diverse background, it is frustrating to see this. Please do better, Rutgers.” This negative review reminds us that campus cultural atmosphere can affect the sense of belonging for some students, especially those concerned with social justice issues. When choosing housing, finding communities or student apartments known for inclusivity may partially alleviate this concern.

Overall, the reviews reveal three key points: the dispersion of campuses determines the commute mode; the distance of residence affects the depth of the student experience; and the alignment of campus culture with personal values is worth understanding in advance.

## City Background and Budget Range

New Brunswick is located in central New Jersey, with a population of approximately 5 ten thousand, adjacent to the Raritan River, and about a 40-minute train ride from New York City. The city houses Rutgers' 5 main campuses (College Avenue, Busch, Livingston, Cook, Douglass), along with numerous restaurants, cafes, theaters, and parks. The city's public transportation primarily relies on Rutgers Campus Buses and NJ Transit, with biking and walking also being common options.

## Walking vs. Bus Trade-offs

![Rutgers University – New Brunswick](https://img.unistay.cn/housing/p/amber-290745.jpg)

Since the Rutgers University–New Brunswick campus is composed of 5 campuses scattered across New Brunswick and Piscataway, the choice of address directly affects daily commute time.

**Walking-first scenario:** If most of your classes are concentrated on one campus (e.g., College Avenue or Douglass), choosing an apartment within walking distance of that campus can maximize time savings. Walking 10–15 minutes gives you access to the library, dining halls, and fitness center, and makes it convenient to attend evening activities and group discussions. The downside: when you have classes across campuses, you still need to rely on the campus bus or a bicycle; and apartments in the walking zone tend to have higher rent.

**Bus-first scenario:** If your classes are spread across multiple campuses, or if you prefer a quieter, lower-rent neighborhood, you can live near a major stop along a campus bus route. Rutgers Campus Buses are free, cover all campuses, and run frequently (every 5–10 minutes during peak hours). These apartments are relatively affordable, but you need to factor in waiting and transfer time (typically 15–25 minutes one way). However, note that bus service is reduced late at night and on holidays, so check the schedule in advance.

**Hybrid approach:** Many students choose to live near the College Avenue or Livingston campuses, as these are activity hubs and have concentrated campus bus hubs. This allows both walking in the main areas and convenient bus travel to other campuses.

Before making a decision, it is recommended to use Google Maps or the commute tool provided by the operator to simulate the time from your apartment to classrooms on each campus. Each campus has different campus bus routes and stops; understanding them in advance can avoid the rush after the semester starts.
