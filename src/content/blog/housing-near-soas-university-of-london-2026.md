For students moving to London to study at SOAS, finding accommodation that balances convenience, cost and quality is a critical first step. This guide draws on student reviews, city-wide rental data, and a selection of nearby student residences to help you make an informed choice.

### City Context: London Student Housing

London is one of the most expensive student cities in the world. For the 2026/27 academic year, average weekly rent for a student room in London ranges from around £180 to £400, depending on location, room type, and amenities. SOAS is located in Zone 1, close to Russell Square, Goodge Street, and Tottenham Court Road stations. This central position means that housing immediately adjacent to campus — within a 10-minute walk — tends to be at the higher end of the budget spectrum. A typical en-suite room in a purpose-built student accommodation (PBSA) near Bloomsbury can cost £300–£400 per week. Shared houses or flats in the same area are often even more expensive relative to space.

Many SOAS students choose to live slightly further out — in zones 2 or 3 — where rents drop by 20–40%. The trade-off is a longer commute, but London’s tube, bus, and rail network makes travel manageable. The university is also close to several major transport hubs: King’s Cross St Pancras, Euston, and Waterloo are all within a 15-minute journey.

The following bands give a rough idea of what you might pay for a single room in a student residence or shared house near SOAS. All prices are indicative; always check directly with the accommodation provider.

- **Mid budget (£200–£300/week):** En-suite or studio rooms in PBSA in zones 1/2 with a 15–25 minute commute. Some older halls in Bloomsbury or nearby (e.g. near King’s Cross) also fall in this range.
- **High budget (over £300/week):** Premium studios or en-suites within a 0–15 minute walk of SOAS. These often include gyms, study lounges, and social spaces. Many of the well-known central London residences fall here.

### Walk vs Transit Trade-Off

Living within walking distance of SOAS (0–1 mile) saves you time and transport costs (a Zone 1–1 journey on the tube currently costs £2.80 single peak). A walk of 10–15 minutes from Russell Square, Goodge Street, or Holborn can get you to the main building easily. Properties in this radius include purpose-built residences near Euston Road or Bloomsbury Square.

If you choose to live further out – for example, near Hammersmith (Zone 2), Kingston (Zone 6), or Brent Cross (Zone 3) – the commute can take 30–60 minutes each way. Your weekly transport cost would be around £30–£50 using a Student Oyster photocard (discounted travel). The benefit is lower rent (often 20–30% less than central options) and potentially a quieter, more local atmosphere.

Which option is better depends on your priorities. If you have a packed schedule of lectures, language classes, and library sessions, living close by frees up time. If you prefer a larger room or private kitchen and are comfortable with a commute, suburban living can be more cost-effective.
