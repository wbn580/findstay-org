### Housing Near South China University of Technology: A Practical Guide

South China University of Technology (SCUT) is a well-known public university located in the Tianhe district of Guangzhou. With a QS World University ranking of 377, it attracts a significant number of domestic and international students. Understanding where to live near campus can be challenging, especially when balancing budget, commute time, and lifestyle preferences. This guide uses recent student reviews and practical city context to help you make an informed choice for the 2026–2027 academic year.

#### What Campus Reviews Reveal

Current and former students have shared mixed feedback about life at SCUT. Their comments highlight both academic strengths and practical concerns for anyone planning to live nearby.

One graduate noted the university’s reputation: *“This University is one of the prestigious universities in China. I have graduated in MBA at Businesses Administration faculty. Lecturers and staff is very friendly and polite.”* — Nethma Dissanayake, 5 stars, 2 years ago.

Another reviewer pointed out a physical challenge of the campus: *“One of the highest ranked universities in Guangzhou. But be prepared to climb a lot of stairs, most of the buildings don’t have elevators.”* — Shawn Sagar, 5 stars, 6 years ago.

A third review touched on dormitory conditions: *“SCUT is an outstanding university. The dormitory conditions are relatively poor because most funds are used for academic research. You can enjoy China’s first-class educational resources in here.”* — Bowei ZENG, 5 stars, 4 years ago.

However, not all feedback is positive. A critical review stated: *“I don’t know why this university is so famous, but once you get in, you will realize that this university gives you a very low quality of life, whether it’s the chaotic teaching schedule or the insufficient and dirty public motorbikes.”* — AQ HIGH, 1 star, a year ago.

These reviews suggest that while academic quality is recognised, on-campus dormitories may not suit everyone. Many students choose to rent off-campus for better living conditions. The lack of elevators in some buildings also means that if you attend classes on the upper floors, you may prefer accommodation close to the main teaching blocks to reduce walking up stairs.

#### City Context: Guangzhou’s Tianhe District

SCUT’s main campus is at 381 Wu Shan Lu, Tianhe District. Tianhe is the central business and commercial hub of Guangzhou, one of China’s largest and most dynamic cities. The area is well connected by metro (Line 3 and Line 6 serve stations near the university), buses, and taxis. Guangzhou’s public transport network is extensive, making it possible to live farther away if you prefer lower rent or a quieter environment.

The cost of living in Tianhe is higher than in some other parts of Guangzhou, but still moderate compared to Beijing or Shanghai. You will find a wide range of grocery stores, restaurants, and services within walking distance of the campus. For shopping and entertainment, the Tianhe City mall and surrounding commercial areas are about 15–20 minutes by metro.

#### Budget Bands for Student Housing (2026–2027)

Rental prices near SCUT vary depending on distance, room type, and amenities. Below are typical monthly bands for the 2026–2027 academic year. All figures are indicative — confirm with the operator.

- **Entry-level (CNY 1,500 – 2,500 per month)**  
  Shared apartments in older buildings within a 20–30 minute walk or a short bus ride. These often have basic furniture, shared kitchens and bathrooms, and limited soundproofing. Suitable for students on a tight budget who prioritise affordability over modern facilities.
- **Mid-range (CNY 2,500 – 4,000 per month)**  
  Private rooms in shared apartments closer to campus (10–15 minutes by foot) or in newer residential compounds. Many include air conditioning, a private bathroom, and a simple cooking area. This range also covers some studio apartments in small buildings near the university metro station.
- **Upper-range (above CNY 4,000 per month)**  
  Studio or one-bedroom apartments in modern gated communities within a 5–10 minute walk. These often come with a separate bedroom, living area, and fitted kitchen. They are ideal for couples or students who require more space and quiet study conditions. Some also have gyms or security.

#### Walk vs Transit Trade-off

Choosing between walking distance and relying on transit involves practical trade-offs.

**Walking distance (under 15 minutes)**  
Living within a 15-minute walk of the main campus gate gives you flexibility for early classes and late study sessions without worrying about metro schedules. Areas along Wushan Road and the streets immediately north and south of the campus are popular. The trade-off is higher rent and potentially older building quality. Noise from traffic or nearby restaurants can also be a factor.

![South China University of Technology](https://img.unistay.net/housing/p/amber-240725.jpg)

**Transit-oriented (15–40 minutes by metro or bus)**  
Neighbourhoods along metro Line 3 (e.g., Wushan station, Tianhe Coach Terminal station, or even as far as Canton Tower area) offer more affordable housing and newer apartments. A 20–30 minute commute is common for many SCUT students. The cost savings can amount to CNY 1,000 or more per month, which may be significant over the academic year. However, you will need to factor in metro fares (around CNY 2–6 per trip) and the time spent queuing during peak hours. Guangzhou’s metro is reliable but can be crowded.

For students who cycle, Guangzhou’s bike-sharing services (e.g., Mobike or Hellobike) are widely available. Cycling 15–20 minutes can open up many more housing options without relying on transit, though you should check whether your route has dedicated bike lanes.

#### How to Shortlist Your Housing Options

When you start looking for accommodation near SCUT, follow a logical shortlist process. Since no specific residences are listed in this guide, the principles below will help you evaluate any property.

1. **Define your maximum commute** – Decide whether you want to walk or take transit. Mark a 15-minute walking radius and a 30-minute transit radius on a map.
2. **Set your budget band** – Use the indicative bands above to filter listings. Contact operators to confirm actual prices and what is included (utilities, internet, cleaning).
3. **Check the building condition** – Reviews of the campus itself mention stairs and poor dormitory conditions. For off-campus housing, inspect the building’s lift availability, security, and cleanliness. Ask for recent photos or a video tour.
4. **Read multiple reviews** – Look for feedback from current or former residents about noise, maintenance, and landlord responsiveness. Use platforms like Google Maps or other review sites (though you can only use the data provided here for SCUT itself, you can apply similar scrutiny to any property you consider).
5. **Confirm legal documents** – Ensure the lease agreement is in Chinese and English if needed. Verify that the rental is legal and the landlord has the right to sublet. This is especially important in Guangzhou where some properties are short-term rentals not authorised for long-term stays.
6. **Visit at different times** – If possible, visit the neighbourhood once during daytime and once during evening to assess noise, lighting, and safety.

By following this checklist, you can systematically reduce options and avoid rushing into a commitment.

#### Frequently Asked Questions

**1. What is the typical rent range for a private room near SCUT in 2026–2027?**  
A private room in a shared apartment within a 15-minute walk typically costs between CNY 2,500 and CNY 4,000 per month, indicative — confirm with the operator. If you are willing to commute 20–30 minutes by metro, you may find rooms from CNY 1,800 to CNY 2,500.

**2. Are on-campus dormitories available for international students, and what are the conditions?**  
SCUT does offer on-campus dormitories for international students, but according to reviews, conditions are relatively poor, as funding prioritises academic research. Some students report shared facilities and limited maintenance. For a more comfortable living environment, many choose off-campus accommodation.

**3. How good is the public transport connectivity from the university to other parts of Guangzhou?**  
The university is well served by Guangzhou’s metro system. Wushan station on Line 3 is a few minutes’ walk from the campus. Line 6 also passes through the nearby Tianhe Coach Terminal station. It takes roughly 30 minutes to reach the city centre (Zhujiang New Town) and about 40 minutes to Guangzhou East Railway Station. Buses also run along Wushan Road.
