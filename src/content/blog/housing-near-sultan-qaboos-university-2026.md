## City Context: Muscat and Al Khoudh

Sultan Qaboos University is located in the Al Khoudh area of Sib, a district in the greater Muscat region. The address is Al Seeb Al Khoudh SQU SEPS Muscat OM, Sib 123, Oman. The university’s position places it roughly 30–40 kilometres west of Muscat’s central business district (Muttrah and Ruwi). Sib is a large, sprawling wilayat (province) that includes both residential suburbs and industrial zones. Al Khoudh itself is a relatively quiet, planned area dominated by the university campus and related facilities.

Muscat is a linear city that stretches along the coast. Public transport is limited; most residents rely on private cars or taxis. There is a public bus network (Mwasalat) that connects some parts of Sib to central Muscat, but frequency and coverage can be uneven. For students, owning or sharing a car is common. However, for those living within walking distance of the campus gates, daily commuting is much simpler.

The climate in Muscat is hot and humid for much of the year, particularly from May to October. Air conditioning is not a luxury — it is a necessity in any rental property. When evaluating housing, check for the age and efficiency of the AC unit, and whether electricity is included in the rent. The academic year at SQU typically runs from September to June, with a summer break. That means you will be living through the milder months (September–October and March–May) as well as the hotter peak (November–February is relatively pleasant, with daytime temperatures around 25–30°C).

## Walk vs Transit Trade-off

Choosing between a residence within walking distance of SQU and one that requires a commute involves a few straightforward trade-offs.

**Walking distance (roughly within 1–2 km of the main gate)**

Advantages: no morning traffic stress, no parking fees (campus parking can be limited), no fuel costs, and you can return to your room between classes or to the library late at night. The reviews praise the campus as “spacious” and “well-maintained”, so being close means you can use it as an extension of your living space — jogging in the grounds, visiting research centres on weekends.

Disadvantages: properties immediately adjacent to the university may command higher rents. The supply of such units can be limited, so you may need to start searching several months before the academic year begins. Also, walking under the Omani sun can be uncomfortable in the hotter months; a shaded pathway or a short drive might be preferable.

**Transit (car or bus, 5–15 km radius)**

Advantages: a wider choice of apartments and villas, often at lower rents than the immediate campus vicinity. This can be a way to secure a larger room or better amenities (pool, gym, compound security). Driving time to campus is usually 10–20 minutes, depending on traffic on Sultan Qaboos Highway.

Disadvantages: car ownership or regular taxi costs add up. Omani traffic can be heavy near the university during peak hours (8:00–9:00 am and 4:00–5:00 pm). Bus routes do serve the area but may not run late into the night, limiting your flexibility if you stay on campus for evening events or study groups.

Most students eventually adopt a hybrid: they find a flat within a 10-minute drive and keep a car, or join a carpool with classmates living nearby.

## Shortlist Logic for Nearby Residences

When building a shortlist of housing options near SQU, you can follow a simple step-by-step logic. Because no specific residence names are provided in the dataset, this logic helps you evaluate any property you find through general listings or contacts.

1. **Define your radius.** Decide whether you will walk or drive. For walkers, map the boundaries of the campus — the main entrance is on the road leading from Al Khoudh roundabout. Any building within 1.5 km of that gate is a strong candidate. For drivers, expand to 5 km or even 10 km along the Sultan Qaboos Highway and nearby roads.
2. **Verify the property’s condition and utilities.** Given the heat, check that the air conditioning is central or wall-mounted and well-maintained. Ask about recent maintenance history. The campus itself is praised for its modern facilities — your housing should meet a similar standard.
3. **Inspect the neighbourhood.** Al Khoudh is mostly residential, but some areas have small grocery shops, cafes, and laundries. Walk around at different times of day to assess noise levels and street lighting. Safety is generally not a major concern in Sib, but always check for secure entry gates and proper locks.
4. **Check the lease terms.** Many student-friendly landlords in the area offer contracts running from September to June. If you need a 12-month lease, ask about early termination clauses. Some properties require a full year’s rent in advance, so budgeting for that is essential.
5. **Compare with campus housing.** SQU does offer student accommodation on campus for some undergraduate and postgraduate students. If you are eligible, on-campus housing may be the simplest and most integrated option. Off-campus living gives you more independence but requires more planning. Review the university’s housing office website or contact them directly for availability and pricing.
