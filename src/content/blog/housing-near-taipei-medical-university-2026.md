## Campus Reviews: What Students Say About Taipei Medical University

Taipei Medical University (TMU) is located in the Xinyi District of Taipei City, at No. 250, Wuxing Street. With a global QS ranking of 597 and a Google rating of 4.0 from 1,167 reviews, the university draws both local and international students. Student feedback highlights the practical side of campus life — from ease of access to nearby amenities.

One reviewer, Erna Dwi susanti, wrote: “Access to the hospital is very easy, the service is excellent, and the doctors and nurses are friendly and always helpful when there are difficulties. However, I choose not to come here again.” This note hints that while the medical facilities are top-notch, the overall experience may not be for everyone. Another student, Mr Ko, commented: “It’s alright - got a 7-Eleven 😂 the grass pitch outside is nice to walk around. I mean NTU etc is better in general, but it’s nice that there is a place to chill with grass.” This reflects a simple, functional campus environment with a convenience store and a small green space for relaxation.

Nguyen Tien Cuong noted: “Taipei Medical University has established for around 60 years until now. The university has a good tradition in educations and researchs for Medical students.” The institution’s long history and focus on medical education are clearly valued. Finally, Raphael Onuku shared: “This is a top-notch University in Taiwan and my research experience in the field of medicinal chemistry as a graduate student is near perfection. The professors are kind and easily approachable. The local students and staff are always so kind 🎯.” This points to a supportive academic environment, especially for graduate researchers.

These reviews suggest that TMU is a well-regarded institution with good hospital access, basic campus amenities, and a friendly atmosphere. For incoming students — particularly those in medical or health science fields — the immediate question becomes: where to live nearby? The rest of this guide breaks down the housing landscape.

## City Context: Living in Taipei’s Xinyi District

TMU sits in Xinyi District, Taipei’s commercial and financial hub. The area is home to Taipei 101, numerous shopping malls, corporate offices, and a dense network of restaurants and services. Public transport is excellent: the Taipei Metro’s Taipei 101/World Trade Center Station (Red Line) and Xiangshan Station (also Red Line) are within a 15–20 minute walk from campus. Many bus routes also serve Wuxing Street.

Xinyi District is considered one of the more expensive residential areas in Taipei, but it offers convenience. Students can find a mix of older apartment blocks (walkups) and newer high-rises. The neighbourhood around TMU includes traditional markets, convenience stores (the 7-Eleven mentioned in reviews is just outside campus), and clinics. For daily needs, you will not need to travel far.

The broader Taipei City has a well-integrated Metro, bus, and YouBike (public bicycle) system. While living on the Metro Red Line is the most direct option, students on other lines can still commute within 30–40 minutes by combining bus and train.

## Budget Bands for Student Housing Near TMU

![Taipei Medical University](https://img.unistay.net/housing/p/amber-1427067.jpg)

All prices below are **indicative — confirm with the operator**. Figures are based on typical rental listings in 2026 and 2027 for the area within a 1–2 km radius of Taipei Medical University. Rental costs vary by building age, floor level, and furnishings.

**Budget Band 1: Economy – TWD 8,000–12,000 per month**

- Shared apartments (合租套房) — typically a bedroom in a 3–4 bedroom unit with shared bathroom and kitchen.
- Older walkup buildings (4–5 floors, no lift) within 15–20 minutes’ walk to campus.
- Often includes basic furniture (bed, desk, air conditioner, shared fridge).
- Utility bills (electricity, water, internet) are usually split among flatmates.

**Budget Band 2: Mid-range – TWD 13,000–18,000 per month**

- Private studio or small 1-bedroom apartment (獨立套房) with own bathroom and small kitchenette.
- Newer walkups or lower-floor units in apartment blocks with lift.
- Located 10–15 minutes on foot from TMU.
- Furnished or semi-furnished; some include internet.

**Budget Band 3: Comfort – TWD 19,000–28,000 per month**

- Spacious 1-bedroom or small 2-bedroom apartments in modern buildings with lift and management.
- Often within 5–10 minutes’ walk of campus.
- Fully furnished, includes utilities such as water and basic electricity.
- May come with in-unit washer, air conditioning, and security.

**Budget Band 4: Premium – TWD 29,000+ per month**

- Larger 2–3 bedroom apartments or serviced apartments in high-end residential towers.
- Located along major roads like Xinyi Road or near Taipei 101 — slightly longer commute (15–20 min walk or one Metro stop).
- Full amenities (gym, concierge, parking) but rarely necessary for a student.

Remember that deposit amounts (usually two months’ rent) and agency fees (one-half to one month’s rent) add to upfront costs. Most leases run for one year; short-term rentals (6 months) are possible but may command a premium.

## Walk vs. Transit: Balancing Commute and Cost

TMU’s location is moderately walkable from the nearest Metro stations (about 1–1.5 km). Many students choose to walk 15–20 minutes to class rather than take a bus or Metro — the exercise is free and reliable. However, living within a 10-minute walk (about 800 metres) tends to push rents into the mid-range or comfort bands.

**Walking distance zones and typical rent impact:**

- **Within 500 metres** (5–7 minute walk): Prices are highest; most apartments are in the comfort-to-premium range (TWD 18,000+). Very few economy options exist directly on campus streets.
- **500 metres – 1 km** (8–12 minute walk): A good middle ground. You can find mid-range studios (TWD 13,000–18,000) in buildings slightly off the main road or in older lanes.
- **1 – 2 km** (15–25 minute walk): Economy and mid-range options dominate. You might share a flat for TWD 9,000–12,000, or rent a basic studio for TWD 12,000–15,000. Walking is still feasible but you may also use a bus or YouBike (2–3 minutes).
- **>2 km**: You will rely on bus or Metro. A one-way trip to campus takes 20–40 minutes. Rents drop further, but commuting time adds up. A monthly transport pass (TPASS TWD 1,200) covers unlimited Metro and bus rides.

**Transit trade-off:** If you choose a cheaper apartment in, say, Songshan or Da’an District (still on the Red Line), you can reach TMU in 25–30 minutes by MRT plus a 10-minute walk. That reduces rent by 20–30% compared to a Xinyi District walkable option. For students with small budgets, this is often the best value.

**YouBike:** Many students use Taipei’s bike-sharing system. Stations are abundant around TMU. A short ride from home to campus can cut a 20-minute walk to 5–7 minutes.

## How to Shortlist Your Housing Options

Since no specific nearby residences are provided in the source data, the following is a general logic you can apply when evaluating listings from student housing platforms or rental agents. Use these steps to create your own shortlist:

1. **Set your non-negotiable radius.** Decide the maximum walking time you can tolerate. For TMU, a 15-minute walk is typical for mid-range budgets. If you need to save, extend to 25 minutes or one bus/Metro stop.
2. **Define your budget band.** From the figures above, decide whether you want a private studio or are open to sharing. Include utility costs in your monthly total — ask if electricity and water are included.
3. **Check transportation links.** If you cannot walk, confirm that a bus stop or MRT station is within 5 minutes of the apartment. Look for routes that serve “Taipei Medical University” or “Wuxing St.” The 266, 284, and 650 buses are common.
4. **Inspect the building amenities.** For walkup buildings, check if the apartment is on a high floor (carrying groceries up 5 flights is tiring). For older units, ask about air conditioner condition, hot water heater, and noise insulation — reviews mention the hospital area can be busy.
5. **Read Google reviews of the building or management company** (if listed). Look for comments on maintenance response, security, and cleanliness.
6. **Compare at least three options before committing.** Use online platforms that list rent, deposit, and photos. Schedule a live viewing — video call is fine if you’re abroad — to check room size and natural light.
7. **Factor in upfront costs.** Two months’ deposit + one month’s rent + agency fee (often half month) = about 3.5 months’ rent upfront. Ensure you have that cash ready.
