## City Background and Pace of Life

Tokyo is one of the most densely populated cities in the world, but Bunkyo-ku is a residential and academic mixed area, with a more relaxed pace of life than commercial districts like Shinjuku or Shibuya. Around Yushima, there are cultural facilities such as Ueno Park and the Tokyo National Museum, within walking distance. Near the main gate of the university are Yushima Station on the Tokyo Metro Chiyoda Line, and Okachimachi Station on the JR Yamanote Line. Commuting to major Tokyo stations (Tokyo Station, Shinjuku Station) takes 15–25 minutes. The district is well-equipped with supermarkets, convenience stores, clinics, and pharmacies, suitable for students' daily needs. For international students, Bunkyo-ku has good public safety, clean streets, and is close to Akihabara Electric Town and the historic Asakusa area, balancing convenience and sightseeing.

- Okachimachi Station: about 8 minutes walk

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do international students need a guarantor to sign a rental contract?

Most private apartments in Japan require a guarantor (usually a Japanese national or permanent resident). If you cannot find a guarantor, you can use a 'guarantor company' service (costing about 50%–100% of monthly rent). Some student dormitories or international student apartments accept overseas applications and do not require a guarantor, but spaces are limited. It is recommended to first consult the accommodation options recommended by the university's international student office.

### What supermarkets or daily shopping points are near the university?

Around Yushima Station there are small supermarkets (such as Life, Maruetsu), convenience stores (7-Eleven, FamilyMart), and drugstores. Near Ueno Station, there is 'Ueno Ameyoko Market' (Ameyoko), where you can buy cheap fruits, vegetables, and daily necessities. Within a 15-minute walk, there is a large supermarket 'Olympic', suitable for weekly shopping. All prices are at Tokyo's general level for 2026, with no special discount data.

### What is the actual time difference between walking to school and taking public transit?

Centered on the university, the area within a 15-minute walk includes: Yushima, around Ueno Park, Hongo-Todaimae, and Nezu. Taking a bus with one transfer can expand the range to Asakusa, Akihabara, and Kanda, but total time is about 25–40 minutes (including walking to the stop). Given the high frequency of Tokyo Metro (every 2–3 minutes during peak hours), taking public transit does not significantly save time unless you choose a property more than 20 minutes away on foot. It is recommended to try walking the morning rush hour route once before deciding whether to rely on public transit.
