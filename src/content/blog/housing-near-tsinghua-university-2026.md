## What Campus Reviews Reveal

Tsinghua University’s campus consistently earns high marks from visitors and alumni. On Google, the university holds a 4.6 rating from 582 reviewers. The feedback highlights several recurring themes that directly affect where students choose to live.

Ziyang Jin, rating the campus 5 stars, wrote: “It’s a huge and beautiful campus. It offers a mysterious study atmosphere. To get in, you either need to know someone who studies/works in Tsinghua or draw a lottery from the official website. Weekdays between classes are busy and you get to see continuous bike flow. I like the garden inside the cam” (review from three months ago). This reveals two practical points: access to the campus is restricted (students need a pass, visitors need a reservation), and the campus is so large that cycling is the dominant mode of internal transport. A residence near a campus gate saves the effort of biking across the whole university.

Doctors Clan gave a 5-star review one year ago: “Tsinghua University is one of the best universities in the world, known for its academic excellence, cutting-edge research, and stunning campus. The university excels in engineering, technology, business, and political science, attracting top students and faculty globally.” This underscores the academic intensity – students often spend long hours in labs or libraries, so housing that minimises commute time is a priority.

Dr. Abdul Ghaffar, a PhD alumnus, wrote five years ago: “My Alma matter, the best University of China. I stayed there for four and a half years during PhD studies from September 2014 to January 2019. There are countless memories, events, celebrations, achievements at Tsinghua University. I feel it as my second home.” Long-term residents like Dr. Ghaffar often develop strong preferences for neighbourhoods near their department buildings, which may lie on the east or west side of the sprawling campus.

Sumit Roy’s review from six years ago adds: “The best university in China. A dream of every Chinese student. One of the best academic and research facilities in world. Inside there is a whole new world. You will feel you have left Beijing and came to a new place in China. There’s a historical part with parks and lakes which came as a legacy…” The mention of “historical part with parks and lakes” refers to the western section of campus near the Old Summer Palace. Students who enjoy quiet study spots may prefer housing on the west side, close to those gardens.

Taken together, the reviews tell future students to prioritise: (1) proximity to a campus gate to reduce biking time, (2) a neighbourhood that fits the academic lifestyle (quiet, with study-friendly cafes or libraries nearby), and (3) access to the parts of campus where their department is located.

## City Context: Beijing as a Student City

Tsinghua University is located in the Haidian District of northwest Beijing. Haidian is the city’s university district, home to dozens of higher-education institutions. This concentration creates a vibrant student ecosystem: cheap eateries, bookstores, bike repair shops, and late-night convenience stores are common. The district is also relatively green, with several large parks including the Old Summer Palace (Yuanmingyuan) and the Summer Palace (Yiheyuan) within walking or cycling distance from Tsinghua.

Public transport is well developed. The nearest subway station to Tsinghua’s main gate is Wudaokou on Line 13, and the Zhichunli station on Line 10 also serves the southeast corner of campus. Buses connect the university to other parts of Beijing, though traffic during peak hours can be heavy.

Housing prices in Haidian vary greatly depending on distance from campus and the type of building. Older residential complexes (built in the 1980s–1990s) are cheaper but may lack modern amenities like elevators or central heating. Newer apartment towers near Wudaokou command higher rents. Shared apartments (hezu) are popular among students, as are studios or single rooms in purpose-built student residences. However, no specific student residence names or ratings are provided in this dataset – students should consult official university housing offices or licensed rental agencies for current listings.

## Walk vs. Transit Trade-off

Tsinghua’s campus is roughly 3.5 km from north to south and 2 km from east to west. The main teaching building clusters are near the east and south gates. Your choice of residence depends heavily on where your classes are held.

- **Walking distance (≤1 km from gate)** – Ideal if you are based near the south or east gate. The walk to classrooms is under 15 minutes. The trade-off is higher rent and limited availability; many flats in this zone are older because the area is built up.
- **Cycling distance (1–3 km)** – Bikes are the default mode for most Tsinghua students. The campus itself has dedicated bike lanes and parking. Living 2–3 km away adds a 10–20 minute ride each way, which is acceptable for most. Rents drop noticeably beyond the 2 km ring.
- **Transit-dependent (3–5 km)** – If you choose a cheaper apartment in Wudaokou or further south, you will need the subway or bus. Line 13 at Wudaokou station connects to the east gate area (about 10 minutes walk from the station). The commute can be 30–40 minutes door-to-door. The savings in rent may offset the time loss for some students, but the reviews emphasised that campus life is busy, so a shorter commute is generally preferred.

4. **Check building age and condition.** Older buildings (pre-2000) in the Lanqiying neighbourhood may have lower rents but also older plumbing and no elevator. Newer complexes near Wudaokou are pricier but often include furnishings and maintenance services.

Apply this logic to any listing you find on housing platforms or through agents. Always inspect the unit in person before signing a lease.
