### Kuala Lumpur: a student-friendly city

Kuala Lumpur is Malaysia’s capital and a major regional education hub. The city offers a mix of modern infrastructure and affordable living costs compared to other international study destinations. Public transport includes the MRT, LRT, Monorail, and bus networks, making it possible to commute from various neighbourhoods.

UCSI University’s campus is situated in Taman Connaught, a residential area in the Cheras district. This location is well served by the Cheras–Kajang highway and the nearby MRT station (Taman Connaught MRT, part of the Kajang line). The area is known for its variety of food stalls, shops, and local markets, providing daily necessities within short distances.

For international students, the government’s visa processing is handled by the university’s Visa Processing Unit (VPU), which several reviewers praised for efficiency. This suggests that the university is well-equipped to handle administrative needs, reducing one barrier when moving to Kuala Lumpur.

### Walk vs transit: choosing your commute

UCSI University’s main campus sits on a hill – UCSI Heights – so walking from nearby housing requires some uphill effort. Students living within a 1 km radius can walk to class in 10–20 minutes, depending on fitness and heat. The area has footpaths, but not all are shaded. Morning walks may be more comfortable than afternoon ones due to tropical temperatures.

For those living farther away, the MRT Kajang line is a reliable option. The nearest station is Taman Connaught MRT, which is about a 10–15 minute bus ride or a 20-minute walk from the university. Regular shuttle buses (free for UCSI students) connect the campus to the station. The university also operates internal shuttle services to nearby student housing clusters.

Driving or using ride-hailing apps (e.g., Grab) is common. Parking on campus is available but can be limited during peak hours. Students who own motorcycles often find it easier to park and navigate the hill.

**Trade-off summary**

- Walking: free, healthy, and time-efficient for those within 1 km. Summer heat and uphill sections are drawbacks.
- Public transport: cost-effective (MRT fare to city centre around RM 2–RM 4 per trip). Buses and shuttles add waiting time.
- Private transport: convenient for grocery runs and late-night travel, but parking fees and fuel cost must be considered.

Most students choose accommodation within a 2–3 km radius to balance rent and commute time. The university’s shuttle service makes medium-distance options viable.

### Shortlisting residences near UCSI

When searching for housing near UCSI, the following logic helps narrow down choices:

1. **Define your budget and room type.**  
   Based on the indicative bands above, decide whether you prefer a shared room (lowest cost), private room (mid-range), or a studio (higher privacy). This immediately filters out properties outside your range.
2. **Consider commute preference.**  
   If you want to walk, look for properties within 1 km of the UCSI Heights campus. Use Google Maps to check walking routes – avoid steep side roads if mobility is an issue. If you prefer transit, focus on apartments within 500 m of an MRT station or along the shuttle bus route.
3. **Check lease terms and inclusions.**  
   Some residences require a 6-month or 12-month contract. Confirm whether utilities (water, electricity, internet) are included in the rent. Many purpose-built student accommodations offer inclusive packages with Wi-Fi and regular cleaning.
4. **Visit the property in person or use virtual tours.**  
   Photos can be misleading. Always request a video walkthrough or schedule a viewing before committing. Check the condition of furniture, air conditioning, and water pressure.
5. **Read recent reviews of the building or management.**  
   Look for feedback on security, maintenance response times, and noise levels. Since UCSI has a dedicated VPU for visa matters, the university itself may not manage off-campus housing, so independent reviews matter.
