## Housing Near United Arab Emirates University: A Practical Guide for Students

The university, ranked 229th globally, is located on Sheikh Khalifa Bin Zayed Street in the Al Asharij area of Abu Dhabi. While the campus itself is well-equipped, student reviews reveal significant administrative challenges that may affect your housing decisions. This guide uses real student feedback, neutral cost estimates, and practical trade-offs to help you shortlist accommodation.

### City Context: Living in Abu Dhabi

UAEU’s campus lies in the Al Asharij district of Abu Dhabi, close to the intersection of Sheikh Khalifa Bin Zayed Street and other major roads. Abu Dhabi is the capital of the United Arab Emirates, known for its modern infrastructure, high standard of living, and significant expat population. The city offers a range of housing options, from shared villas to studio apartments, with price levels generally higher than in other emirates like Sharjah or Ajman.

Public transportation in Abu Dhabi includes buses operated by the Department of Transport, as well as taxis and ride-hailing services. The bus network is expanding but may not cover all residential areas directly. Most students rely on private transport (car, taxi, or shared rides) to commute. Bicycle infrastructure is limited in many parts of the city.

Cost of living in 2026: Utilities, groceries, and entertainment are relatively expensive compared to global averages. A reasonable monthly budget for a single student (excluding tuition and housing) is around 2,000–3,500 AED (indicative — confirm with the operator). This includes food, transport, mobile, and personal items.

### Budget Bands for Student Housing

Housing costs in Abu Dhabi vary widely depending on proximity to campus, unit type, and sharing arrangements. Below are typical monthly rent ranges (indicative — confirm with the operator for exact current pricing).

- **Economy (1,500–2,500 AED/month per person):** Shared rooms in older apartment buildings or partitioned villas, usually located 20–30 minutes by car from campus. Expect basic furnishings, shared bathrooms, and limited privacy. Suitable if you are on a tight budget and do not mind a longer commute.
- **Standard (2,500–4,500 AED/month per person):** Shared two- or three-bedroom apartments in mid-range complexes near Al Asharij or along Sheikh Khalifa Bin Zayed Street. Often include a private bedroom, shared living/kitchen, and access to building amenities like a gym or pool. Walking distance to campus is possible in the immediate vicinity.
- **Premium (4,500–7,000+ AED/month per person):** Studio or one-bedroom apartments in newer developments with high-end finishes, parking, and security. May include utilities and WiFi. These are best for those who value independence and want to live within a 15-minute walk or short drive.

When budgeting, factor in a security deposit (usually 5–10% of annual rent), agency fees (one month’s rent), and utility connection charges. Many landlords require annual rent cheques, though some offer monthly or quarterly payment plans.

### Walk vs. Transit Trade-off

**Walking:** Living within a 15–20 minute walk of UAEU’s campus saves commuting time and cost. The immediate neighbourhood around Sheikh Khalifa Bin Zayed Street has a few apartment buildings, but availability is limited. If you find a place within walking distance, you eliminate transport expenses and can easily attend evening classes or use campus facilities. The downside is that such units are often in higher demand and may command a premium rent.

![United Arab Emirates University](https://img.unistay.cn/housing/p/amber-287682.jpg)

**Transit:** For most students, living further out and using public transport (bus, taxi, or shared car) is more affordable. Abu Dhabi’s bus network connects major residential areas like Khalifa City, Al Raha, and Al Reem Island to the city centre. A monthly bus pass costs around 80–120 AED (indicative — confirm with the operator). Taxi rides from, say, Al Nahda to campus could cost 30–50 AED one way. Ride-hailing services are widely available.

The trade-off is time. A 20-minute drive from a cheaper area becomes a 45–60 minute bus journey, plus waiting times. If your schedule is flexible, the savings could be worth it. If you have early morning classes or require reliable transportation, living closer or owning a car (with associated fuel and parking costs) may be better.

**Summary comparison:**

- *Walk:* High rent, low time, zero transport cost, convenience.
- *Transit:* Lower rent, higher time, moderate transport cost, dependent on schedule.

### Shortlist Logic Using Nearby Residences

How to build your shortlist of potential housing options:

1. **Define your must-haves.** Do you require a private bathroom? Are you willing to share a bedroom? Must the unit be furnished? These criteria narrow down the pool quickly.
2. **Set a realistic walk zone.** Use a map to see which residential blocks are within a 1.5 km radius of the university entrance. Check pedestrian safety and lighting, especially if you will walk late.
3. **Evaluate commute time.** If you cannot find suitable options near campus, look along bus routes that service the area. Use the Abu Dhabi Department of Transport website to see which bus lines stop at “UAEU” or nearby junctions.
4. **Check reviews of buildings and agents.** Just as the university’s administration has mixed feedback, local landlords and agencies vary. Look for reviews of specific apartment complexes on Google Maps or housing forums. Avoid payment of large deposits before physically inspecting the unit.
5. **Consider dropping proximity in exchange for a better lease.** If a community 10–15 km away offers newer construction, included utilities, and a supportive management team, the extra commute may be worthwhile. Especially given the administrative challenges mentioned in reviews, a smooth rental experience matters.
6. **Budget for the first year.** New students often underestimate set-up costs: security deposit, agency fee, furniture (if unfurnished), internet installation, and DEWA utility deposits. Have at least 3 months’ rent ready upfront.

When browsing listings, ask the operator:

- Is the rent per person or per unit?
- Are utilities (electricity, water, cooling) included or separate?
- What is the cancellation policy?
- How much notice is needed to vacate?

Because student feedback indicates that UAEU’s own administration can be slow and unresponsive, independent housing (off-campus, private landlord) may give you more control. However, on-campus halls exist — if considering them, confirm contract terms in writing and ask about the procedure for resolving disputes.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is it cheaper to live on campus or off campus?**
On-campus housing may include utilities and meals in a single fee, but pricing varies each academic year. For 2026–2027, request a rate sheet from the university’s accommodation office. Off-campus sharing typically costs 1,500–3,500 AED per person per month (indicative — confirm with the operator), which can be lower than on-campus rates if you split a larger unit. Factor in transport cost and stress when comparing.

**Q: What is the best area for a student without a car?**
Neighbourhoods close to campus, such as Al Asharij itself or areas along Hamdan Bin Mohammed Street, have a higher density of bus stops and taxi availability. Alternatively, living near a major bus interchange (e.g., Al Wahda Mall or Abu Dhabi Central Bus Station) gives you more route options, though you will need to transfer to reach UAEU.

**Q: How early should I start looking for housing?**
Start searching at least 2–3 months before the start of the academic year (around July for September intake). Peak demand in August–September pushes rents higher and reduces choice. For January intakes, begin by November. Remember that many leases are for 12 months, so graduating in the middle of a contract may require early termination fees.
