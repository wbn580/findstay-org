## What Campus Reviews Reveal

Universidad de Zaragoza holds a global QS rank of 638 and has a Google rating of 4.2 from 1,067 reviews. The comments are mixed, but they point to real concerns that can shape your housing decision.

If you are in the same situation, living close to the main campus at Calle de Pedro Cerbuna, 12, can make it easier to visit the international office in person and get quick answers.

Another review from As Asif (4 stars, 10 months ago) reads: “I’m a international student .. reading bachelor’s .. and thinking of cancelling running and start new bachelor’s here.” This suggests some students consider transferring mid-course. If you are planning to start a new programme, you may want a flexible rental contract – many student residences in Zaragoza offer semester-based leases, which is worth checking.

A less relevant but still present review from rockyroy 999777 (1 star, 6 months ago) complains about long waiting times, though it appears to refer to a hospital rather than the university. Such mismatched reviews remind you to cross-check information. For housing, rely on operator confirmations, not opinion posts.

Tung Nguyen (5 stars, a year ago) asks: “I want to study here but I don’t know if the school has graphic design or not.” This again shows a need for clear academic guidance. If you enrol in a programme with irregular class schedules (like design studios), living within walking distance may give you more flexibility than relying on bus timetables.

Overall, the reviews underline the importance of proximity to campus and access to administrative support. A short commute can save time for sorting out paperwork, attending orientation events, and settling in.

## Zaragoza: A Student-Friendly City

Zaragoza is the fifth-largest city in Spain, located roughly halfway between Madrid and Barcelona. The city has a population of about 700,000 and a relatively low cost of living compared to other major Spanish university cities. Public transport is efficient, with buses and trams connecting most neighbourhoods. The city centre is compact, and many university buildings are within a 20-minute walk from the main square, Plaza del Pilar.

The university’s main campus (Campus Plaza San Francisco) is situated near the city centre, so you have access to supermarkets, restaurants, and cultural venues without needing a car. The Ebro River runs through the city, offering green spaces for relaxation.

Zaragoza experiences a semi-arid climate: hot summers (often above 35°C) and cool winters (around 5°C). If you choose a residence with air conditioning, that is a plus for the summer months – something to confirm with the operator, as not all student housing includes it.

## Walk vs Transit: Choosing Your Location

The main Universidad de Zaragoza campus is at Calle de Pedro Cerbuna, 12. This is in the San Francisco neighbourhood, right next to the large green area of the same name. Living within a 15-minute walk means you can skip public transport costs and enjoy a short stroll through the park. This is ideal if you have early classes or labs.

If you prefer a quieter and cheaper neighbourhood, options further out (e.g., near the Delicias train station or Parque Goya) may offer lower rents but require a bus or tram ride of 20–30 minutes. A monthly transport pass in Zaragoza costs about EUR 40 (indicative — confirm with the operator). Walking also reduces your carbon footprint and gives you daily exercise, which many students appreciate.

Trade-off summary:

- **Walkable (<15 min):** Higher rent, less commute stress, easy access to campus facilities.
- **Transit (15–30 min):** Lower rent, need to plan around bus schedules, and pay for a monthly pass.

Your choice depends on your budget and daily routine. If you have evening classes or plan to use the library late, living closer might be safer and more convenient.
