## City Context: São Paulo (SP)

USP’s main campus is located in the Butantã neighborhood, in the western zone of São Paulo. São Paulo is Latin America’s largest city, with a population exceeding 12 million. Public transport includes a metro (São Paulo Metro), bus network, and the CPTM commuter rail system. The nearest metro station to USP’s main entrance is Butantã (Line 4 – Yellow), which connects to the broader network. Traffic congestion is heavy during peak hours, so travel time estimates should include buffer.

The city’s rental market varies widely by district. Butantã itself is a mixed residential area with mid-range apartments and houses. Nearby neighborhoods such as Pinheiros, Vila Madalena, and Perdizes are more expensive due to better nightlife and commercial amenities. Farther out, districts like Osasco or Taboão da Serra offer lower rents but longer commutes.

Crime statistics are uneven: areas immediately around campus are generally safe during daytime, but students are advised to exercise caution in quieter streets after dark. University shuttles and on-campus security services operate 24/7.

## Walk vs Transit Trade-off

USP’s campus is large — about 7.5 km² — so “walking distance” depends on which building you need. The most frequented areas (main library, central classrooms, and student restaurant) are clustered near the main entrance on Rua da Reitoria. Living within a 1–2 km radius of that entrance can make walking feasible for most daily activities. Beyond that, a bicycle or scooter is useful.

If you rely on public transport, the Butantã metro station is about 1.5 km from the main gate. Buses run along Avenida Professor Almeida Prado and Avenida Vital Brasil, serving deeper parts of campus. During peak hours, bus frequency drops and journeys can be crowded.

The trade-off: walking saves money and time but limits your housing options to a small, often pricier radius. Transit expands your search area to lower-cost neighborhoods but adds 30–60 minutes of daily commute and a monthly transport pass (currently about BRL 180–230 for unlimited metro/bus, indicative — confirm with the operator).

## FAQ

### Does USP provide on-campus accommodation for international students?

USP does not own or operate dormitories for the general student body. There are small housing arrangements for exchange students through specific departments, but availability is limited and not guaranteed. Most students — both local and international — find private rentals in the surrounding neighborhoods. Some student cooperatives exist, such as “Repúblicas” (shared houses) organized by student groups; these are advertised through word of mouth or university notice boards.

### What is the typical commute time if I live in Pinheiros versus in Butantã?

Butantã is adjacent to the campus; living there usually means a 10–20 minute walk or 5-minute bus ride. Pinheiros (about 4 km away) takes 25–35 minutes by bus or 20 minutes by metro (line 4) plus a walk. However, Pinheiros offers more shops, restaurants, and nightlife, and rents are often 20–40% higher. The decision depends on whether you value proximity to campus more than access to city amenities.

### Are there any specific areas to avoid when renting near USP?

As a general rule, avoid areas with poor street lighting and low pedestrian traffic after dark. In Butantã, the eastern side near Avenida Eliseu de Almeida can be less safe at night. The neighborhood surrounding the metro station is generally safe during the day. Always visit the area in the evening before signing a lease. The university’s Department of Social Services (assistência social) can provide updated safety maps upon request.
