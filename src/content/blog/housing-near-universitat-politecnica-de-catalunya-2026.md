### Walk vs. Transit: How to Trade Off Time and Money

Choosing between a walking distance and a commuting distance depends on your budget, schedule, and tolerance for daily travel.

**Walking (15–20 minutes to campus)**  
Properties within a 1‑km radius of UPC (e.g., Carrer De L’Equador 77 or Sant Pau) allow you to walk to class, saving metro fares and time. This is ideal for first‑year students who want to drop by the library or sports centre easily. However, rents in Les Corts near the university tend to be higher (€500+ per room). Street noise and limited grocery options near the immediate campus are minor drawbacks — the area is quiet but not very lively at night.

**Transit (20–40 minutes one way)**  
Neighbourhoods such as Sants‑Montjuïc (where Rambla Del Brasil 7 is located) or lower Eixample offer cheaper rents and more dining options. Metro L3 or bus H6 run directly to UPC, with a monthly travel pass costing around €40–€60 (2026 youth fare). If you have a tight budget (€300–€400), this trade‑off is often necessary. The extra 20 minutes each way can be used for reading or listening to lectures, but it also reduces flexibility to attend evening events on campus.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How far in advance should I start looking for housing near UPC?**  
A: For the 2026/2027 academic year, begin searching at least 2–3 months before your intended move‑in date (e.g., May for September start). Barcelona’s rental market moves fast — popular listings near UPC are often rented within a week. Starting early gives you time to visit flats (or do video tours) and avoid last‑minute price surges.

**Q: Is it cheaper to live in a shared apartment or a student residence?**  
A: Shared apartments (piso compartido) are typically 15–30% cheaper than purpose‑built student residences, especially in areas like Sants‑Montjuïc. However, residences often include all bills, cleaning, and sometimes a gym, which can be worth the premium if you value convenience. For 2026, a shared room near Les Corts may cost €350–€500, while a residence room could be €600–€900. Always compare the total monthly cost (rent + utilities + transport).

**Q: What should I check in a rental contract to avoid problems?**  
A: Read the contract language (ideally in Spanish or Catalan; you may need a translation). Verify: the exact monthly rent, deposit amount (usually 1–2 months), whether utilities are included, cancellation policy, and who handles repairs. Take photos of the apartment’s condition before moving in. Given the administrative complaint in UPC reviews, ensure the contract is signed by a genuine landlord or company — demand a copy of their NIE or CIF. Avoid paying anything before seeing the property in person or via live video.
