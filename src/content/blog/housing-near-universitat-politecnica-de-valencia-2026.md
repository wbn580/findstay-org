## What Campus Reviews Reveal

The Universitat Politècnica de València (UPV) holds a Google rating of 4.5 from 1,680 reviews. Students consistently highlight the campus environment and practical facilities. Dr. Hafiz Muhammad Waqas Badar describes it as “truly one of the best universities in Spain. The campus is stunning — peaceful, green, and full of charm. You can hear birds singing as you walk through the lush lawns, and the atmosphere is perfect for studying. It also has one of the largest libraries”. The library network and green spaces are a recurring theme, making on-campus or nearby accommodation appealing for those who value study-friendly surroundings.

For international students, Souvik Kanti Roy notes: “Amazing place for your ERASMUS+ program. Great faculty! UPV offers a large number of subjects in English. Also, if you are an incoming student for your ERASMUS, you will also be offered a Spanish language course free of cost.” This indicates that UPV actively supports exchange students, and housing close to campus can reduce barriers to participation in these language courses and social activities.

Alexandre Van Calster adds: “Awesome campus, all you need can be found here. Very high quality university. There are many libraries, café’s, print stores, etc…even a pharmacy, optic stores, etc.” The self-contained campus reduces the need to travel far for daily errands, which means accommodation within walking or short cycling distance can be particularly convenient.

Carol O Byrne’s 4-star review mentions: “I had nothing to complain about. It is a very nice place. The room was basic but enough. I had my own bathroom. There were a couple of cafes. Very good transport options. The staff was lovely and spoke English. I would stay there again.” This hints that while campus life is good, transport links are also reliable, giving students flexibility in where they live.

Overall, the reviews paint a picture of a well-equipped, green campus with strong support for international students. Proximity to campus is a definite advantage, but the city’s transport network makes living further out viable.

## Valencia: City Context for Students

Valencia is Spain’s third-largest city and a popular destination for students. It combines a Mediterranean climate, a historic centre, and a modern infrastructure. The cost of living is generally lower than Barcelona or Madrid, which is reflected in accommodation prices. The city has a well-developed metro, tram, and bus network, plus a bike-sharing system (Valenbisi) that many students use.

The UPV main campus is located at Camí de Vera, s/n, in the Algirós district, about 3 km northeast of the city centre. This area is predominantly student-oriented, with several residence halls and student apartments nearby. The beach (Playa de la Malvarrosa) is about 4 km east, reachable by bus or tram. The city centre (Plaza del Ayuntamiento) is about 20–30 minutes by bus or tram.

Valencia’s student population is diverse, and the city hosts numerous cultural events, including the famous Fallas festival in March. For students, the key considerations are housing cost, commute time, and access to campus facilities.

## Walk vs Transit Trade-Off

### Walking distance to UPV

The UPV campus is large, and the main entrances are on Camí de Vera. Accommodation within a 15-minute walk (roughly 1 km) includes residences on Avenida de los Naranjos, Calle Ingeniero Fausto Elio, and parts of Algirós. Walking saves money on transport and time — you can easily pop home between classes. With 1,680 reviews mentioning a beautiful campus, many students choose to live close to enjoy the green surroundings and library access at any hour.

The drawback is that rents in immediate campus proximity may be slightly higher. The sample residence “SPC 46005” is likely located in the 46005 postal code (Algirós / Camí de Vera area), which is walkable. Similarly, “Micampus Galileo Galilei” is a known residence hall in the area, within walking distance.

### Transit options

Valencia’s tram line 4 stops at “Universitat Politècnica” (just outside campus). Buses 9, 30, 40, and 99 also serve the area. If you live further out — for example, in the city centre (e.g., around 46020 or 46130) — you can reach campus in 20–30 minutes by public transport. The “SPC 46011” and “SPC 46130” samples are in more distant postal codes, indicating they may be better suited for students who prefer a livelier urban setting and don’t mind a commute.

The trade-off is lower rent for a longer journey. A monthly transport pass (MetroBus) costs about €30–40 for students (2026 rates, indicative). The bike-sharing pass is very cheap (about €30 per year). Many students cycle from central neighbourhoods like Ruzafa or El Carmen — 15–20 minutes to campus.

### Recommendation

If your priority is study convenience and you can afford slightly higher rent, choose accommodation within walking distance of campus. If you prefer a more vibrant city life or need to save money, consider areas along tram line 4 or bus routes, but factor in commuting time and cost.
