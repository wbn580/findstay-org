## What campus reviews reveal

The Universität Regensburg holds an average rating of 4.0 out of 5 on Google, based on 413 reviews. That solid score matches the teaching quality and helpful attitude that multiple reviewers highlight — but it also points to physical infrastructure that students say could use attention.

One reviewer, Nutan Savale (5 stars, 7 years ago), described the university as “extremely friendly” and noted that “all professors are very helpful. Teaching and research facilities are also very nice.” This kind of consistent positive feedback about academic support matters when choosing where to live: if you plan to spend long hours on campus for classes, labs, or studying, you will want a place that allows you to get there easily and comfortably.

Another reviewer, Shridhar Shanbhag (4 stars, a year ago), wrote: “It’s great. My only complaint is that the washrooms in most buildings don’t have warm water taps.” That specific observation — cold washroom taps — may seem minor, but for daily use it can be a frustration. If you are sensitive to such things, consider whether your future housing will have its own warm water or if you might need to rely on campus facilities. Some students prefer flats with private bathrooms to avoid depending on campus amenities.

Russell Spencer (5 stars, 3 years ago) offered a more poetic review: “Various humans with esoteric knowledge described aspects of their arcane studies with audiences of aspiring purveyors of the esoteric. The descriptions were interesting and informative. Many of them utilized an ancient language, whose symbols and inscriptions capture the powers of nature itself.” That unique perspective speaks to the range of academic topics available and the passionate atmosphere. For a student, being immersed in that environment can be energising — and living nearby means you can join evening lectures or study groups without a long commute.

Ali Nadwah (4 stars, 5 years ago) gave a balanced view: “Generally its nice. But the building and decor need maintenance I would say.” That comment confirms what the overall rating suggests: the university delivers good education in surroundings that are functional but not always modern. When you are evaluating rental options nearby, think about whether you prefer new, renovated apartments or older, cheaper flats. The campus itself might not set a luxury standard, so your accommodation could become your main comfort zone.

Taken together, these reviews paint a picture of a university strong in teaching and research, with a friendly and intellectually stimulating environment, but with some dated facilities. Your housing choice should complement that: find a place where you can study comfortably, access campus easily, and perhaps compensate for the cold washroom taps with warm water at home.

## City context: Regensburg

Regensburg is a historic city in Bavaria, situated at the confluence of the Danube, Naab, and Regen rivers. Its medieval Old Town is a UNESCO World Heritage site, featuring landmarks like the Stone Bridge and the Regensburg Cathedral. The city has about 150,000 inhabitants, of whom roughly 25,000 are students. This makes Regensburg a true student city: there is a lively cultural scene, numerous cafés and restaurants, and plenty of green spaces.

The university itself is located in the Galgenberg district, on the northwestern edge of the city. Its address is Universitätsstraße 31, 93053 Regensburg-Galgenberg. The campus sits slightly outside the city centre — about 3 to 4 kilometres from the main square. That distance defines the housing search: you can either live right next to the university in Galgenberg or in a more central neighbourhood and commute. Both options have advantages.

Regensburg is relatively affordable compared to other German university cities such as Munich or Frankfurt. But prices have been rising in recent years due to high demand. The public transport system is efficient; buses connect the university to the city centre and other districts. Cycling is also very popular, thanks to flat terrain and well-maintained bike paths.

## Budget bands for student housing

All prices below are indicative — confirm with the operator before committing to any contract.

- **Student dormitories (Studentenwerk)**: The local student services organisation offers single rooms in residence halls for roughly €250–€400 per month, including utilities, internet, and often basic furniture. These are the most affordable option. Waiting lists can be long, so apply as early as possible.
- **Shared flats (Wohngemeinschaften or WGs)**: In a shared flat, you rent a private room and share kitchen, bathroom, and sometimes living space. Monthly rent in Regensburg for a room in a WG ranges from about €300 to €500, depending on location, size, and condition. A room near the university (Galgenberg or nearby districts) may cost €350–€450, while a room in the city centre might be €400–€550.
- **Private single apartments**: If you prefer to live alone, a one-bedroom or studio apartment will cost roughly €450–€700 per month. Prices near the university tend to be higher because of convenience. A small apartment in the city centre can be similarly priced.
- **Short-term or furnished options**: Temporary housing (for a semester or less) may cost €400–€800 per month. These are often advertised as “Zwischenmiete” (sublets). Check the terms carefully.

Remember that utility costs (electricity, heating, water) are usually included in student dormitory rents but may be billed separately in private flats. Internet is often extra. Budget an additional €50–€100 per month for these expenses if not included.

## Walk vs Transit trade-off

Living within walking distance of the university means choosing a flat in the Galgenberg district or adjacent areas (e.g., Königswiesen, Kumpfmühl, or parts of the inner northeast). Walking time to the lecture halls and library can be as little as 5–15 minutes. This saves money on public transport tickets and gives you flexibility for late-night study sessions. However, the housing supply in these immediate surroundings is limited, and prices can be higher. Galgenberg itself is a quiet, residential area with fewer shops and entertainment options than the city centre.

The trade-off for living in the city centre or other districts (e.g., the Altstadt, West, or East) is a longer commute. Bus lines such as 6, 11, 31, and night lines serve the university. A bus ride from the city centre takes about 15–20 minutes, plus waiting time. The monthly semester ticket is already included in your university fees (around €70 per semester for the whole region), so the direct cost of public transport is negligible after enrollment. The main trade-off is time: 20 minutes one-way becomes 40 minutes daily, which could be used for studying or relaxing.

Cycling is a strong middle ground. From most parts of central Regensburg, you can reach the university in 10–20 minutes by bike. The city is bike-friendly, and many students prefer this option. It is cheaper than public transport (no ongoing ticket cost) and faster than walking. If you cycle, you can live in a wider radius and still have a reasonable commute. Bike theft is a concern, so invest in a good lock.

Ultimately, the decision depends on your priorities. If you value immediate access to campus and shorter days, pay more for a flat in Galgenberg. If you prefer vibrant neighbourhoods with shops, bars, and historic atmosphere, choose the city centre and commute by bike or bus.

## Shortlist logic using given nearby residences

Although this guide does not list specific residential buildings by name, the logic for shortlisting is based on location relative to the university, budget, and your lifestyle preferences.

**Step 1: Define your acceptable commute.**  
If you want to walk to class, focus on the postal code area 93053 (Galgenberg) and bordering parts of 93049 (Kumpfmühl) and 93055 (Königswiesen). Use a map to check walking distances — 1 km or less is ideal. For commuting by bike or bus, you can widen the search to all of central Regensburg (93047–93059).

**Step 2: Set a budget range.**  
Use the band you identified above. Exclude anything outside your absolute maximum. Remember the indicative nature of prices.

**Step 3: Check availability and contract terms.**  
Many student dormitories require you to be enrolled and sometimes a minimum stay of one semester. Private flats often require a deposit (usually three months’ cold rent) and proof of income or a guarantor. Read the lease carefully, especially regarding utility costs and notice periods.

**Step 4: Consider amenities.**  
Given the campus washroom feedback, you may value having a private bathroom with warm water. That is more common in private single apartments and some newer dorms. Shared flats may have shared bathrooms that are well-maintained but not guaranteed to have heated water in all cases — ask the landlord.

**Step 5: Verify distance to daily needs.**  
Even if you live close to the university, check whether supermarkets, laundromats, and bus stops are within walking distance. Galgenberg has limited shopping, so many students still bike or bus a few stops to buy groceries.

**Step 6: Visit in person or use virtual tours.**  
Pictures can be misleading. If possible, visit the room or flat before signing. If you are abroad, ask a friend or use a video call.

By applying these steps, you can create a shortlist of 5–10 options. Contact the landlords or housing offices early, especially for student dorms, which fill up quickly for the winter semester (October start).

## FAQ

Start with the university’s own housing service (look for “Wohnen” on their website). They provide a list of dormitories and can help with private market listings. You can also search on online student housing platforms and local classifieds. Many students find rooms through word of mouth, so ask in university Facebook groups or forums. Another option is to contact the Studentenwerk (student services) directly for dormitory applications. All prices quoted should be considered indicative — confirm with the operator.

### What is the average rent for a room in a shared flat near the university?

A typical room in a shared flat (WG) in the Galgenberg area or near campus costs between €350 and €500 per month (indicative — confirm with the operator). In the city centre, prices may be slightly higher. The total includes heating and other utilities in some cases, but not always; check before signing. Student dormitory rooms are cheaper, usually €250–€400 per month.

### Is it better to live on campus or off campus?

There is no single right answer. Living on campus (i.e., in the Galgenberg area) saves commute time and lets you use the library and sports facilities easily. Off-campus living in the city centre puts you closer to shops, restaurants, and historic sights, but requires a commute of 15–20 minutes by bike or bus. Consider your personal schedule, budget, and tolerance for travel. Try to visit both types of neighbourhoods to see which feels more comfortable.
