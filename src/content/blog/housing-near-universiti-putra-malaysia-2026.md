## Campus Reviews and Surroundings

Universiti Putra Malaysia (UPM) is located in Seri Kembangan, Selangor, approximately 20 km from Kuala Lumpur city center. Based on 1643 user reviews on Google, the university has an overall rating of 4.5. The comments convey the scale and atmosphere of the campus: one user, Tutut Indah Widyawati, who attended an international conference as a campus representative, wrote: "the university seems to be incredibly vast," but due to the tight conference schedule, they could not fully explore the campus. Another user, after attending "Hari Pentauliahan Diraja Palapes kali ke 43," commented: "Good view, beautiful and peaceful university. Facilities was good. Landscape also beautiful." These reviews suggest that the UPM campus is large and beautifully landscaped, suitable for walking or cycling, but commuting between different faculty areas may take some time.

However, some users also provided negative feedback regarding services. A user named Danny Debois gave only 1 star, commenting: "The administration teams are unprofessional, unwilling to help and rude. No one takes responsibility just transfer you to another department." Although administrative experiences vary by individual, for students planning long-term residence, it is advisable to understand communication channels in advance when dealing with the university and surrounding rental management.

Another review from Noora Zuffa mentioned that she enjoyed a lunch reception at PPDK Batu Burok Ar Raudhah (located in Terengganu, not on campus), which is not directly related to housing but reflects the diversity of UPM's community engagement.

Combining these feedback points, when choosing accommodation, consider the walking distances due to the large campus size and the convenience of living near Seri Kembangan.

## Housing Budget Ranges

Housing prices around Malaysian public universities vary significantly by type, distance, and facilities. Due to frequent fluctuations in the rental market and different packages offered by operators (whether utilities, furniture, internet, etc. are included), the following are only general reference ranges. Specific prices should be confirmed with the operator (indicative — confirm with the operator).

- **Budget option**: Usually refers to a private room shared with others, or on-campus/nearby student dormitories. Monthly rent for such options is around a few hundred ringgit, but facilities are basic, and kitchen and bathroom may be shared.
- **Mid-range option**: A single room in a private apartment or a small studio, usually with a private bathroom and basic furniture. Monthly rent for such options is relatively higher, but offers better privacy and convenience, such as proximity to supermarkets, restaurants, and bus stops.
- **Spacious option**: A whole apartment (one or two bedrooms) or an entire floor in a terrace house, suitable for sharing with friends. Rent is highest for these, but space is larger and parking is often available. Note that terrace house areas may be far from main roads, making walking to campus difficult.

Specific prices should be obtained directly from local agents or online rental platforms (note: this article does not recommend any specific website). It is advisable to ask first which costs are included (water, electricity, internet, property management fees) and keep written quotations.

## Walking vs. Commuting Trade-offs

![Universiti Putra Malaysia (UPM)](https://img.unistay.cn/housing/p/amber-2173382.jpg)

The UPM campus covers a large area, with the main entrance on Jalan Universiti. The surrounding residential areas can be roughly divided into two types: areas adjacent to the campus edge (e.g., Taman Serdang Jaya, Taman Universiti Indah) and areas slightly farther but with convenient public transport (e.g., Seri Kembangan town center, Balakong).

- **Walkable (15-30 minutes)**: Living in areas just outside the campus fence, you can walk to the nearest gate or faculty building. The advantage is saving on transport costs, but there may be fewer supermarket and dining options (some areas are mainly residential). Based on comments that the campus is "vast," even living at the gate, you may still need an on-campus bus or bicycle to reach specific faculties.
- **Public transport commute (15-40 minutes)**: Choosing housing near the KTM Serdang station or RapidKL bus routes. Malaysian trains and buses have acceptable punctuality, but are crowded during peak hours. Commuting costs are low (one-way about RM1-4), but you need to allow waiting time. Seri Kembangan town center has more shopping malls (e.g., AEON, The Mines), restaurants, and entertainment, offering higher living convenience.
- **Driving/carpooling**: Many graduate students and staff choose to drive. Parking on campus is limited and requires a permit. Living in areas farther away but with lower rent (e.g., Kajang, Putrajaya) is also an option, but daily commute time may exceed 45 minutes.

When choosing, it is recommended to first check actual walking/cycling routes on Google Maps and assess your class schedule (comments mentioned tight conference sessions; daily class schedules may be similar). An ideal balance is being within a 10-minute walk to a bus stop or a 15-minute bike ride to the main lecture buildings.

## Logic for Filtering Housing

When filtering housing, you can follow these three steps to narrow down options:

1. **Determine budget and roommate needs**: First, list the maximum monthly rent you can afford (including utilities). If willing to share, consider budget options; if privacy is important, move to mid-range and above. When reading reviews, note mentions of "peaceful" and "good facilities" environments, indicating the campus is generally quiet and suitable for study; however, the comments on administrative efficiency suggest that when renting, you should confirm contract details (e.g., deposit refund terms, maintenance response time).
2. **Define geographic priorities**: Use map software to mark the UPM campus boundary and identify three types of areas:

   - **Campus walking circle (radius about 1.5 km)**: Includes Taman Universiti, Taman Serdang Perdana, etc. Walking to the main campus gate is about 10-20 minutes, but you need to walk along main roads and pay attention to traffic safety.
   - **Bus/train connection circle (radius 3-5 km)**: For example, Seri Kembangan town center, Balakong Jinjang. Near KTM Serdang station (within 10-minute walk), one train stop to UPM station (the on-campus station name is "UPM").
   - **Driving/carpooling circle (radius over 8 km)**: Rent may be lower, but commuting cost and time increase.
3. **Conduct site visits or video viewings**: Be sure to check the actual condition of the property, especially:

   - Internet stability (many students need online learning)
   - Whether appliances such as water heaters and air conditioners are aging
   - Roommate living habits (if sharing)
   - Community safety (nighttime streetlights, access control)
   - Proximity to mosques or prayer rooms (if needed)

Meanwhile, referring to the positive impression of "beautiful landscape" in the reviews, you can prioritize neighborhoods with better greenery. Regarding the "unprofessional" feedback, before signing a contract, it is best to ask the landlord to provide identification and property documents to avoid future disputes.

Additionally, you can use UPM student organizations or social media groups (e.g., Facebook student groups) to find sublets or shared housing information, as these channels are often more tailored to student needs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How far in advance should I apply for on-campus housing (student dormitories)?

On-campus dormitories are usually prioritized for new and international students. The application window typically opens 2-3 months before the start of the semester, and spaces are limited. It is recommended to confirm the application deadline and fees with the UPM housing office as early as possible (indicative — confirm with the operator). If you are unable to get a dormitory, start looking for off-campus housing at least 3 weeks in advance.

### How safe is Seri Kembangan? Is it safe to walk alone at night?

Overall, Seri Kembangan is a mid-range residential area with relatively good security, but caution is still needed. At night, it is advisable to choose apartments with security guards or walk in groups. Some main roads are well-lit, but avoid dark alleys. Comments mention a "peaceful university," so the campus is relatively safe, but off-campus you still need to watch your personal belongings.

### Who is responsible for water, electricity, and internet costs?

Most off-campus rental contracts stipulate that water, electricity, and internet fees are paid separately by the tenant (based on usage or fixed split). A few apartments include some of these costs, but this must be clearly stated in the contract. It is advisable to ask the landlord for reference utility bills from the previous three months before signing, so you can estimate your total monthly expenses. All costs should be based on the latest operator quotes (indicative — confirm with the operator).
