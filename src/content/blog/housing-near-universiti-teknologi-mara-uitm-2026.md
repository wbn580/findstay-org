For students planning to enroll in 2026 or 2027, understanding accommodation options around the campus, budget ranges, and commuting trade-offs is a key step in planning student life abroad.

### Shah Alam City Background

Shah Alam is the capital of Selangor, about 25 km from the Kuala Lumpur city center. The city is known for its planned layout, with many residential areas, shopping malls, and parks. The UiTM main campus is located in the Seksyen 7 area of Shah Alam, surrounded by residential neighborhoods and commercial facilities. In terms of public transport, the KTM commuter rail and RapidKL bus services cover some areas, but the shuttle network on and near campus may be more suitable for students' daily travel. For students without a private car, it is advisable to prioritize accommodation options within walking or cycling distance.

### Budget Ranges (Indicative Prices, Confirm with Operator)

Since the UiTM main campus is located on the outskirts of a large city, accommodation costs are slightly lower than in downtown Kuala Lumpur. The following are common budget categories. All prices are indicative; please confirm with the landlord or dormitory management.

- **Low Budget (Economy)**: Usually on-campus student hostels or shared rooms near campus. These options have lower monthly rent but may require shared bathrooms and kitchens, and space is relatively compact. On-campus dormitories are competitive and are generally prioritized for new students or those in specific programs.
- **Medium Budget (Standard)**: Single rooms or master bedrooms in private apartments near campus, with private bathroom and basic furniture. Walking distance or a short bus ride to the campus. Such options are common in areas like Seksyen 7 and Seksyen 9 in Shah Alam.
- **High Budget (Comfort)**: Entire apartments (studio or 1-bedroom) or upscale serviced apartments, usually a bit farther from campus but with better facilities such as swimming pool, gym, 24-hour security. Suitable for students who want a quiet study environment or those accompanied by family.

### Walking or Commuting: Trade-off Analysis

When choosing accommodation, the commuting method directly affects daily time management and study efficiency.

![Universiti Teknologi MARA - UiTM](https://img.findstay.org/housing/p/amber-2360447.jpg)

- **Walking Distance (approx. 1-2 km)**: If you live in on-campus dormitories or private apartments right next to the campus gate, walking to lecture halls takes only 10-20 minutes. This saves transportation costs and time, and makes it easy to use the library in the evening or attend campus events. The downside is limited choices, and some on-campus dormitories may require early application or have restrictions.
- **Short Public Transport (bus or shuttle)**: Many students live in apartment areas in Seksyen 7 or Seksyen 9 and take RapidKL buses or campus free shuttles (if operating) to the campus. Shuttle frequency is usually every 15-30 minutes during peak hours, with longer intervals off-peak. You need to get familiar with routes and schedules in advance.
- **Bicycle or E-scooter**: In Shah Alam, many students use electric scooters or bicycles for the "last mile" problem. The campus is flat and suitable for cycling. However, pay attention to traffic safety on main roads and ensure that the accommodation provides secure parking space.
- **Private Car or Ride-hailing**: For students living farther away (e.g., near Guthrie Highway, Subang Jaya, or Petaling Jaya), driving or using ride-hailing services like Grab is common. But consider fuel costs, parking fees (whether student parking is available on campus), and peak-hour congestion. One-way ride-hailing cost is about 8-15 MYR (indicative price); if commuting daily, the monthly transport cost will increase significantly.

Overall, most international students will prioritize accommodation within walking distance or reachable by a short bus ride to balance cost and convenience.

### Practical Logic for Filtering Accommodation

Without a unified university recommendation, you can narrow down options by following these steps.

1. **Determine Your Main Mode of Transport**: First ask yourself: Am I willing to walk more than 20 minutes every day? Will I buy a car or motorcycle? If the answer is "I don't want to walk," then focus your search on apartments within 1 km of the campus. If you are willing to take the bus, you can expand the range to nearby terraced houses or apartment areas.
2. **Check Budget and Facilities**: According to the budget ranges above, use map mode on rental platforms (e.g., iProperty, Mudah.my, etc. — note: do not use specific domain names, only general advice) to filter listings near "UiTM Shah Alam." Focus on whether furniture is included, how utilities are calculated, and whether internet is available. Ask for photos or videos of the unit, especially the condition of the kitchen and bathroom.
3. **Check Safety and Community Reviews**: Shah Alam is generally safe, but it is advisable to choose apartments with 24-hour security or gated communities. You can ask current students about specific buildings or refer to ratings and comments on Google Maps. The UiTM main campus itself has a high rating (4.5 stars), but the safety of surrounding residential areas needs to be verified on your own.
4. **Do a Site Visit of the Commute Route**: After identifying 2-3 candidate areas, it is recommended to make a personal trip before enrollment (e.g., early 2026). Take a bus or walk once to feel the actual time and road conditions. If you cannot arrive early, use Google Maps' public transport planning feature to estimate the time.
5. **Confirm the Contract and Deposit**: In Malaysia, renting usually requires an advance payment of 2+1 (two months deposit and one month rent) or similar terms. Read the contract carefully, especially clauses regarding early termination, repair responsibilities, and the landlord's identity. If not through the university's official channels, it is advisable to review the contract with an experienced senior student.

### On-Campus Dormitories vs. Off-Campus Apartments: How to Choose

The advantage of on-campus dormitories is proximity, ease of socializing with classmates, and relatively fixed costs. However, spaces are limited and it may not be guaranteed for the second year. Off-campus apartments offer more independent space and freedom of choice, with prices varying by location and configuration. For students enrolling in 2026/2027, it is recommended to contact the university's housing office as soon as you receive your admission letter to learn about the dormitory application timeline and available spots. Also prepare a backup off-campus plan to avoid last-minute room hunting before the semester starts.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does it usually take to walk from an off-campus apartment to the UiTM main campus?

Walking time depends on the exact location. For private apartments near the campus (such as those right in the Seksyen 7 area), walking to the campus gate usually takes 5 to 15 minutes. If you choose a slightly farther residential area (such as Seksyen 9 or 11), walking may take 25 minutes or more. It is recommended to walk the route in person when viewing a property, or use map tools to measure the specific distance (indicative – actual walking time is affected by internal campus roads and pedestrian bridges). Considering the large campus size, even if you live at the campus gate, it may take another 10 minutes to reach a specific lecture hall.

### How can I find reliable off-campus rental information? Are there any official university recommendations?

UiTM itself may offer limited dormitories but does not provide an official off-campus housing list. You can search through the following channels: ask current students or alumni (e.g., via pre-enrollment online groups); use general real estate websites or mobile apps, selecting the area around "UiTM Shah Alam" on the map; contact local rental agents in Shah Alam, identifying yourself as a student. Do not trust any advertisement that requires a deposit before viewing the property. All prices are indicative; before signing the contract, be sure to confirm with the landlord or management what fees are included (utilities, internet, maintenance, etc.).

### Is it feasible to commute to UiTM from a farther city (such as Kuala Lumpur)?

Theoretically feasible, but the actual cost is high. Taking the KTM commuter rail from KL Sentral to Shah Alam KTM station takes about 30-40 minutes, then you still need to transfer to a bus or ride-hailing service to reach the campus — the total commute time may exceed 1 hour. The round-trip transport cost (indicative) together with about 2 hours of commuting time each day may affect study and rest. For the 2026/2027 academic year, it is recommended to prioritize Shah Alam itself or adjacent areas like Subang Jaya or Petaling Jaya. Cross-city commuting is not recommended unless you truly cannot find suitable housing near the campus.
