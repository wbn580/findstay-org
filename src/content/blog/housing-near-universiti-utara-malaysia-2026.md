## About UUM Campus – Experiences Revealed by Reviews

Universiti Utara Malaysia (UUM), with a Google rating of 4.6 (855 reviews), is located in Sintok, northern Kedah, and its campus is surrounded by natural scenery. From the feedback left by students, it is clear that the environment is its most prominent feature. D. Chow wrote in a 4-star review: "Nestled in an area with fresh air and natural surroundings, it provides a calm and healthy environment that makes studying both effective and enjoyable." Another user, ZURAIDI KHALID, gave 5 stars, describing it as "Best place for education and also a best view around the unive". Sarawut Maddamrongnon also gave 5 stars: "UUM,Very nice place and good view university."

These reviews point to a key piece of information: UUM's campus atmosphere is primarily tranquil and natural, suitable for immersive study. However, the campus is also large in reality. Nurhanisah Noh mentioned in a review from 4 years ago: "So big..must take bus. Their law faculty are so great, comfortable moot court and can fill a lot of us. The go cart is rm5 for students and rm10 for outsiders. Must try if you haven’t one. Nice and breath taking views. If you wanna walk here, recomme" – although the sentence is incomplete, "must take bus" and "nice and breath taking views" highlight the duality of campus transportation and scenery. This environment has a direct impact on housing choices: regardless of whether you live on or off campus, transportation options must be considered.

## City Background: Sintok and Kedah

UUM's main campus is located in the small town of Sintok in northern Kedah, about 15 km from the Malaysia–Thailand border and about 50 km from the state capital Alor Setar. Sintok itself is an area that has developed around the university, with a small number of commercial facilities, such as the "zoos, malls" mentioned by Nurhanisah. But overall, it is a combination of rural and town, rich in natural resources but limited in man-made facilities. The nearest notable towns are Bukit Kayu Hitam (a border crossing) and Changlun (about a 15 km drive), the latter of which has more supermarkets, restaurants, and daily goods stores.

For students, daily necessities – such as groceries, medicine, and electronics – can be obtained on the Sintok campus or in Changlun, but major shopping requires a trip to Alor Setar or Hat Yai across the Thai border. For transportation, Alor Setar has a train station and an airport (Sultan Abdul Halim Airport), connecting to Kuala Lumpur and other major cities. The campus has a shuttle bus service, but the schedule and coverage should be confirmed with the Student Affairs Office.

## Housing Budget Range (2026/2027, Indicative Prices)

![Universiti Utara Malaysia (UUM)](https://img.findstay.org/housing/p/amber-3155392.jpg)

Since housing prices are adjusted annually, the figures below are **indicative – please confirm with the operator**. Based on the general situation around Malaysian public universities, the accommodation costs near UUM for 2026/2027 can be roughly divided into three tiers:

**Budget Tier (RM250–RM450 per month)**

- On-campus dormitories: UUM offers various types of dormitories, from double rooms to quadruple rooms. Fees usually include utilities and basic furniture. Standard monthly fees are around RM250–RM400, depending on the room type and whether meals are included. Advance application is required, and places are limited.
- Off-campus shared housing: Sharing a simple apartment or terrace house in Sintok or Changlun, the monthly rent per single occupant is about RM300–RM450. Utilities and Wi-Fi are extra.

**Mid-Range Tier (RM500–RM800 per month)**

- On-campus single rooms/apartment-style units with private bathroom: Some newer dormitories or college buildings offer more private space, with monthly rent around RM500–RM700.
- Off-campus independent apartments: Independent apartments in Changlun or Jitra (about a 20-minute drive), one-bedroom units with monthly rent around RM600–RM800. Includes basic furniture, but usually does not cover utilities.

**Comfort Tier (RM800–RM1,200 per month)**

- High-end apartments or private terrace houses near campus: Fully furnished, with air conditioning and internet, monthly rent around RM800–RM1,200.
- Apartments in Alor Setar city: If willing to commute about 45 minutes daily, comfortable apartments in Alor Setar have monthly rent around RM900–RM1,200.

All prices do not include a deposit (usually 2 months' rent) and a one-time administrative fee. **The figures above are estimates only; actual prices must be confirmed directly with the dormitory management or landlord.**

## Walking vs. Transportation Trade-Offs

UUM's campus covers a vast area; the main campus is over 1,600 hectares. As the review says "So big..must take bus", walking can only cover short distances between classroom buildings, dining halls, and dormitories. If you live in an on-campus dormitory, walking to class usually takes 10–25 minutes, depending on the location of the dormitory and the college. Some dormitories are close to the library and main administrative buildings, but most students still rely on the campus shuttle (free or low-cost).

**Advantages of living on campus:**

- No need for commuting tools, saving time and transportation costs.
- Easier to participate in evening campus activities and use the gym and sports facilities.
- Quiet environment, in line with the "fresh air and natural surroundings" study atmosphere.

**Considerations for living off campus:**

- If you choose an apartment in Sintok, Changlun, or Jitra, the main commuting methods are three: driving yourself (motorcycle or car), carpooling/ride-hailing, or campus bus (some off-campus apartments have shuttle buses, but frequency may be sparse). Motorcycles are the most common means of transport for local students; buying a second-hand motorcycle costs about RM2,000–RM4,000, plus fuel and insurance.
- Walking is basically impractical unless the apartment is adjacent to the campus gate (for example, there are a few houses within walking distance in Sintok town).
- Off-campus life offers more variety: supermarkets, restaurants, markets, and even the "go cart" track and zoo mentioned by Nurhanisah are within a few kilometers, but the trade-off is daily commuting.

**Short-term trade-off advice:**

- Prioritize applying for on-campus dormitories in the first semester to get familiar with the campus and transportation network before deciding whether to move off campus.
- If your budget allows, purchasing a second-hand motorcycle can significantly increase flexibility, but you must hold a valid international driver's license and understand Malaysian traffic rules.

## Logic for Shortlisting Housing Options

There is no single "best" option, but you can filter based on personal priorities. The following is a recommended ranking logic based on reviews and geographic characteristics:

1. **First confirm whether on-campus dormitories are available.** Since UUM is a public university, dormitories are usually given priority to new and international students. If your application is successful, and you have a high need for a tranquil environment (as D. Chow said "calm and healthy environment"), on-campus dormitories are an economical and convenient first choice. Lower cost, no need to worry about transportation.
2. **If dormitories are full or you want more privacy,** next consider private homestays or small apartments within Sintok town. These are usually 15–30 minutes' walk to the campus gate, or a short campus shuttle ride. Mid-range rent, but facilities may not be as complete as on campus.
3. **If you pursue convenience and entertainment in daily life,** consider Changlun or Jitra. These towns have supermarkets, restaurants, laundries, and pharmacies (the "malls" Nurhanisah mentioned are likely located in Changlun or Alor Setar). Commuting time is about 15–25 minutes (by car), and you need your own transportation. Mid-range to comfort tier rent.
4. **If your budget is sufficient and you want a better living environment,** apartments or terrace houses in Alor Setar city are an option. But the round trip is nearly 1 hour by car (one way), so the time and fuel costs need to be factored in. Suitable for students who have a car and do not mind a long commute.

**When looking at specific listings, make sure to confirm the following three points:**

- Whether utilities/internet are included, and whether cooking is allowed.
- The actual walking/cycling distance to the nearest campus bus stop or campus gate.
- Whether the lease term matches the semester (most contracts are 12 months, but negotiable).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does UUM provide on-campus dormitories? What is the approximate cost for the 2026/2027 academic year?

Yes, UUM provides various types of on-campus dormitories, including double rooms, quadruple rooms, and apartment-style units with private bathrooms. The current **indicative** cost is RM250–RM700 per month, depending on the room type and whether meals are included. To confirm the application result and latest prices, please contact the UUM Student Accommodation Office (official channel) directly. New students are usually given priority, but places are limited; it is recommended to submit the application as soon as possible after receiving the admission notice.

### Is it necessary to own a car or motorcycle when living off campus? Is there a bus or campus shuttle available?

It is not necessary, but personal transportation (especially a motorcycle) is strongly recommended to increase freedom of movement. The campus provides free or low-cost shuttle bus services, mainly operating on fixed routes within the campus and nearby areas (such as Sintok town and Changlun), but frequency may be sparse, especially in the evenings and on weekends. If you do not have a vehicle, you can choose to live within walking distance in Sintok town, or rely on carpooling/Grab (local ride-hailing, one-way from Sintok to Changlun about RM8–15). Shared bicycle or e-scooter programs are currently not widespread off campus.

### Are there supermarkets, restaurants, and entertainment facilities nearby? Can I walk there?

On campus, there are small convenience stores, restaurants, and fast-food outlets where basic daily necessities can be purchased. For large supermarkets, cinemas, or more dining options, you need to go to Changlun (about a 15-minute drive) or Alor Setar (about a 40-minute drive). The "zoos, malls" mentioned in Nurhanisah's review likely refer to the Kedah Zoo and shopping plaza located in Changlun. These places are not reachable on foot and require transportation. On campus, there is a student-run go-kart track, RM5/time (student price), which is one of the few entertainment options within walking distance.
