Walking from Westwood to the core campus area takes about 10-20 minutes, while cheaper options may be located in Palms, Culver City, or Koreatown, requiring a transfer on public transit or driving (note parking costs).

The budget range can be roughly divided into three tiers:

## Walking vs Public Transit Trade-offs

![University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA)](https://img.unistay.net/housing/p/amber-1953356.jpg)

For UCLA students, the key variables in choosing a residence are daily commute time and total cost.

**Walking advantages**: Living close completely avoids parking hassles, allows an extra 30 minutes of sleep in the morning, and makes it convenient to return to the dorm for a break at noon. Rentals within walking distance (e.g., 840 Hilgard Ave, 832 Hilgard Ave) are almost all on the north or west side of campus, adjacent to the dorm area. The downsides are higher rent and slightly higher prices at restaurants and supermarkets in Westwood Village.

**Public transit options**: If you accept a 15-25 minute bus commute (including waiting time), you can find rentals that are 15-25% cheaper. For example, living in Palms (928 S. Broadway near downtown, but 928 S. Broadway is actually in Downtown LA, quite far from UCLA, about 12 miles—commute time 40-60 minutes—suitable for those who don't mind a long commute or have some online classes). More common options are in Culver City or Sawtelle, which have direct bus lines to UCLA (Metro 12/20/720, etc.). The drawbacks are needing to adapt to the bus schedule, reduced evening service, and the U-Pass, though included in tuition, has limited coverage.

**Bicycles and electric scooters**: The terrain in Los Angeles is mostly flat but has some hills, and electric scooters are a popular tool on campus. Many apartments allow bike parking, but tenants need to buy their own locks. From a residence within 2 miles of campus, biking usually takes 10-15 minutes.
