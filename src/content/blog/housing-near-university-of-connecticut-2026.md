## City Background

The UConn main campus is located in Storrs, a relatively small college town in eastern Connecticut. Administratively, Storrs belongs to the town of Mansfield, which has a population of about 25,000, a large proportion of whom are university students and employees. Nearby towns such as Coventry also offer some student housing options.

Storrs itself does not have a large city center, but the campus has dining, libraries, sports facilities, etc. Supermarkets, pharmacies, and a few restaurants are located on the edge of campus and in Mansfield town center. The nearest city is Hartford (about 35 km away), with bus connections but limited frequency. If students need to travel to a large city frequently (e.g., for internships, shopping), they should consider having a car or relying on CT Transit's fixed routes.

Since the campus occupies a large part of Storrs, off-campus housing is mainly distributed in Mansfield, Storrs township, and along a few main roads near campus. Coventry also has some residential areas, but they are farther from the main campus.

## Walking vs. Transportation Trade-offs

Choosing housing within walking distance can save time and reduce transportation expenses. Several streets on the east and south sides of campus (such as Route 195, South Eagleville Road) are lined with many student apartments, and walking to the main teaching buildings usually takes 10–25 minutes. The comment mentioning "dorms itself spans 3 cities" indicates that the campus buildings are loosely distributed, so even on-campus dormitories may require a short bus ride or walk to some lecture halls. Students living off campus in the same direction can also typically use the campus bus system.

The campus bus system (UConn Bus) is free for all students and covers the campus and major surrounding residential areas. During peak hours, buses run frequently (about every 5–10 minutes); off-peak and on weekends the intervals are longer. Some students choose to bike; the campus terrain has some gentle slopes but is generally bikeable.

If you combine rent savings with commute time, living in a farther area (such as Coventry) can save $200–$400 per month, but you will need your own car or rely on less frequent public transportation (CT Transit Route 674 connects Storrs to Hartford). All things considered, most first-year and lower-division students choose areas within walking or biking distance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I live off campus and rely entirely on walking to school?

Yes, but you need to choose your location carefully. The main teaching buildings are concentrated in the center of campus (library, student union, science buildings), and there are many apartments within about 1.5 km of this area. According to Google Maps data, walking from the campus boundary to the farthest classrooms takes at most 25 minutes. If your chosen residence is on the south side of Route 195 near Storrs Road, you can cover most of your daily commute on foot. Note that snowfall is heavy in winter, so you will need warm clothing and slip-resistant shoes.

### Which is more cost-effective: on-campus dorms or off-campus renting?

The stated cost of on-campus housing (including meals) is about $8,000–$10,000 per academic year, but it includes utilities, internet, and meals, and does not require a year-long lease. For a 12-month off-campus lease, the actual expense is: assuming monthly rent of $700 (including utilities), 12 months totals $8,400, plus food and transportation (about $400 per month), brings the total to about $13,200. Therefore, on-campus housing is more hassle-free for budget-conscious students. If you sublet your room or find a short-term lease during summer break, off-campus may be slightly cheaper, but it requires more arrangement on your part.

### Do I need a car?

Not necessarily. The campus bus is sufficient for daily classes and trips to nearby supermarkets (such as Big Y). However, if you need to go to downtown Hartford regularly (for internships, medical appointments, or entertainment), not having a car can be inconvenient. In that case, you can take CT Transit Route 674 or carpool with classmates who have a car. A few students use Uber/taxis, but a one-way trip costs about $20–$30, which is not economical for frequent use.
