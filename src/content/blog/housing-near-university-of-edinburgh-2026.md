## Campus Reviews: What Students and Visitors Say

Student and visitor reviews of the University of Edinburgh consistently point to a campus that blends historic charm with a focused academic atmosphere. One reviewer, AJay Sadh, gave a 5-star rating and described the experience as “learning wrapped in legend. Historic buildings, sharp minds, and a calm academic energy all come together beautifully. Walking through the campus, you sense centuries of ideas still whispering forward—serious about knowledge, yet quietly inspiring.” This sense of tradition is echoed by many, and the physical setting of the Old College and surrounding areas forms a backdrop that many students find motivating.

For those considering short-term accommodation near the university, visitor Carol Dirksen shared a practical perspective after staying in university student dormitory rooms during the Edinburgh Fringe Festival. She noted that four nights in a single room with a shared bathroom and breakfast included cost about $125 USD per night. She added, “Beautiful campus, clean and comfortable room, plenty of hot water. Highly recommend.” This suggests that university-operated housing can be a viable option for visitors, but international students seeking longer-term stays will have a wider set of choices in the private student accommodation market.

A parent of an international student, Tatiana Gomez, gave the university a 5-star rating and said, “My daughter is an international student there, and she is having the best time of her life! The school is beautiful and has a rich history.” While her only complaint concerned the souvenir shop’s service and merchandise variety, the overall environment is clearly appealing to families. Another recent review by Angela highlighted the refurbished student union: “The world’s oldest student union, and following a recent refurbishment, this modern hub makes for a vibrant, buzzing social space to hang out for students and members of the public. Beautifully decorated and retaining original features, various bars are located on different levels offering good prices.” This indicates that social infrastructure on campus is strong and welcoming.

These reviews offer a picture of a university that values heritage and community, which should inform your accommodation decisions – being close enough to enjoy that atmosphere while balancing cost and convenience.

## City Context: Edinburgh for Students

Edinburgh is the capital of Scotland, a city known for its UNESCO-listed Old Town, the Edinburgh Castle, and a year-round calendar of cultural events including the famous Fringe Festival in August. The University of Edinburgh’s main campus is located on South Bridge, a short walk from the Royal Mile and the city centre. Edinburgh’s compact layout means many student residences are within walking distance of the university, and public transport – particularly Lothian Buses – covers the city well.

The student population in Edinburgh is large and international, estimated at over 50,000 across the city’s higher education institutions. The city offers a mix of historic tenement flats and modern purpose-built student accommodation. Local amenities include supermarkets, cafes, restaurants, and green spaces such as The Meadows and Holyrood Park, which are popular with students.

- **Haymarket Yards** is a newer development near Haymarket train station, roughly 20–25 minutes on foot from Old College. It is well served by trains and buses, making it convenient for commuting.
- **Snt Straits Meadow** (likely a misspelling of “Strait’s Meadow” or similar) – its exact location is unclear, but based on the name, it may be near Straiton Park or a green area outside the city centre; students should verify distance carefully.

Walking is the most popular option for many students because the campus is central. A 20-minute walk in Edinburgh can take you from Old College to the periphery of the student residential zones. If you prefer not to walk, an extensive bus network covers the city; a single journey costs approximately £2.00 with an adult single fare (or cheaper with a Ridacard monthly pass). Cycling is also viable, though the city has some steep inclines.

When choosing between walking and transit, consider your tolerance for daily walking distance and weather (Edinburgh can be rainy and windy). Living closer to campus generally commands a price premium, but it saves time and bus fares.
