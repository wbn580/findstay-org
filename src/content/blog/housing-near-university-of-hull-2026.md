## University of Hull: What Campus Reviews Reveal

The University of Hull holds a QS World University Ranking of 526 (2025) and a Google rating of 4.1 from 635 reviews. Student feedback paints a mixed picture that prospective residents should consider when planning where to live.

One 5-star review from Michael Brown notes: “An excellent university central to hull and with a wide variety of subjects. I found the Campus clean, tidy and accessible…also close restaurants and entertainment.” This highlights the campus’s location and the convenience of having amenities nearby.

However, other reviews reveal frustration with administrative processes. Daud Pervez wrote: “I applied to the University of Hull for the September 2025 intake with high hopes for my future. Unfortunately, my UK student visa was refused on 6 October 2025 after the interview. Following this, I immediately contacted the university to request a refund of my tuition fees. I sent multiple emails [truncated].” This points to the importance of having clear support channels, and for students relying on visa processes, having accommodation with flexible cancellation policies may be wise.

Another review from Mia says: “My personal supervisor is extremely helpful. The University is beautiful and has many good staff members. However some of them are very rude and have bad attitudes. The help email should basically not be used, they reply within months. I sent an email when I first started and still haven’t got a res [truncated].” The contrast between helpful personal interactions and slow official communications suggests that when dealing with housing or administrative issues, visiting in person may be more effective than relying on email.

Samrah Ishaq’s review (1 star) simply states: “Kindly issue CAS letter I have paid fee and done with interview as well . I have to apply for visa. Please why taking so long.” Delays in official documents can directly affect a student’s ability to secure accommodation, especially if visa timelines are tight. For housing decisions, this reinforces the value of booking accommodation that allows late arrivals or early move-ins.

Overall, while the campus environment and academic support receive praise, administrative responsiveness is a recurring pain point. Students should factor this into their housing search — for example, by choosing providers known for quick communication.

## City Context – Hull as a Student City

Hull (formally Kingston upon Hull) is a port city in East Yorkshire with a population of around 260,000. It is relatively affordable compared to other UK university cities. The university is located on Cottingham Road, about 1.5 miles north of the city centre. The city offers a compact urban layout: the main shopping area, restaurants, and nightlife are within walking distance or a short bus ride from campus.

Public transport includes buses operated by East Yorkshire, with frequent services along Cottingham Road connecting the university to the city centre and Hull Paragon Interchange (the main train and bus station). Hull also has a growing cultural scene — The Deep aquarium, Hull Maritime Museum, and various galleries are popular.

Because housing costs are lower than in larger cities, students can often afford better quality accommodation for the same budget. However, availability of purpose-built student accommodation (PBSA) is limited compared to cities like Manchester or Leeds. Most students live in shared houses (HMOs) in the Newland Avenue, Beverley Road, or Cottingham areas. The university offers its own halls of residence on or near campus, but competition for these can be high.

For students who prefer newer, managed accommodation, several private PBSA schemes have been built in the city centre in recent years. These tend to be more expensive but offer en-suite rooms, studios, and amenities like gyms and social spaces.

## Walk vs Transit Trade-off

The University of Hull campus is not in the city centre but is well-connected. Here’s how the trade-off works:

**Walking**

- The campus itself is compact. Most teaching buildings, the library, and the student union are within a 10-minute walk from the centre of campus.
- Walking from the city centre to campus takes 25–30 minutes.
- Halls of residence like Needler Hall (university-owned) are adjacent to campus.
- Living in Newland Avenue or Beverley Road puts you within a 10–20 minute walk.

**Transit (bus)**

- Buses run every 10–15 minutes from the city centre to the university during term time.
- A single fare is around £2.20 (2025 price); weekly passes are available.
- If you live further out (e.g., Cottingham village, cost-effective houses), the bus journey is 15–20 minutes.

**Which to choose?**
If your budget allows and you value time, living within walking distance of campus (Newland Avenue, halls) is ideal. If you prefer a livelier city-centre atmosphere with shops and nightlife, you’ll rely on the bus or a 30-minute walk. For lower rent, consider houses on the outskirts with bus connections — just factor in the travel time and cost.

## Shortlist Logic – Choosing Where to Live

When shortlisting accommodation options near the University of Hull, consider the following decision framework:

1. **Distance to campus** – If you have early morning lectures or labs, minimise walking distance. University halls and PBSA on Cottingham Road are best. Private houses on Newland Avenue (about 15 minutes walk) are also popular.
2. **Budget** – Shared houses are cheapest but require more effort (bills, contracts, maintenance). PBSA provides all-inclusive packages with less hassle but costs more. Decide your weekly rental cap.
3. **Contract length** – University halls typically offer 40-week contracts (academic year). PBSA often demands 51-week contracts. If you plan to stay over summer, a longer contract may be fine; if you go home, you’ll pay for empty weeks.
4. **Amenities and support** – PBSA often includes gym, study rooms, social events, and on-site management. Private houses rely on private landlords – check reviews and inventory reports carefully.
5. **Visa/document contingencies** – Based on the campus reviews above, if you are an international student awaiting a CAS or visa, seek accommodation that offers flexible move-in dates or refund policies in case of delays.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How far is the University of Hull from the city centre?

The main campus on Cottingham Road is about 1.5 miles (2.4 km) north of Hull city centre. Walking takes approximately 25–30 minutes. A bus journey is around 10–15 minutes. Cycling is also feasible (10 minutes) — there are bike racks on campus.

### What is the typical rent for student accommodation in Hull?

Rents vary widely. Indicative 2026/27 weekly prices (confirm with operator): shared houses £70–£110; university halls £80–£150; private PBSA en-suite £120–£200; studios up to £250. Bills are often separate in private houses. For a 40-week year, budget £3,000–£8,000 depending on type.

### Are there affordable options near campus?

Yes. The Newland Avenue area is a popular student corridor directly south of campus, with many Victorian houses converted to shared student accommodation. Weekly rents here typically fall in the £75–£95 range for a room. These are often within a 10–15 minute walk. Always check the property condition and landlord reputation before signing.
