## University of Limerick Student Housing Guide 2026

University of Limerick (UL) sits on a modern, well‑kept campus in Castletroy, about 5 km east of Limerick city centre. With a Google rating of 4.6 from 654 reviews, the university is consistently praised for its safe environment, sports facilities, and welcoming atmosphere. Below we look at what student reviews reveal, the local city context, typical budget bands, the walk‑versus‑transit decision, and a logical way to shortlist accommodation options. All prices mentioned are indicative — confirm with the operator.

### What Campus Reviews Reveal

Current students and visiting families highlight three themes: safety, facilities, and community diversity.

Derrick Ferrao, whose daughter started at UL, wrote: “The campus itself is breathtaking, but what truly stands out are three things: the world‑class facilities, the sense of safety, and the remarkable diversity of the student community.” This sense of security is echoed by Haydee B. Lopez: “From the moment we arrived, everything felt welcoming, organized, and incredibly clean. The campus is modern, peaceful, and beautifully designed—perfect for students to focus, grow, and feel at home.”

Sports facilities are a major draw. Ursula Mullane describes a “wonderful campus with fantastic sport and leisure facilities.” Even alumni appreciate how the campus has evolved — Gerry F, who graduated in 1979, notes: “Has grown a little since I graduated in 1979! Very nice campus. Still love the Stables and The Hurlers.” (The Stables and The Hurlers are on‑campus bars and social venues.)

These reviews suggest that students who live close enough to easily access campus benefit the most from the facilities and community. Safety is repeatedly mentioned, making on‑campus or nearby housing particularly attractive for new international students.

### City Context: Limerick and Castletroy

Limerick is Ireland’s third‑largest city, with a population of roughly 100,000. The university is located in Castletroy, a suburban area that has grown around the university. Most student‑oriented housing — purpose‑built student accommodation (PBSA) and private rented houses — is within a 1‑3 km radius of campus.

The city centre is about 15 minutes away by bus or 20 minutes by bike. Regular bus services (UL runs its own shuttle, and public buses connect Castletroy with the city) make it possible to live in the centre, but most students choose Castletroy for convenience.

Living in Castletroy means you are within walking distance (10‑20 minutes) of most university buildings, shops, and a small number of pubs and restaurants. For groceries, there is a large supermarket near the university entrance. Limerick city centre offers more nightlife, cultural venues, and the main shopping streets.

### Budget Bands (Indicative — Confirm with the Operator)

Rents in Limerick are lower than in Dublin but have risen in recent years. The figures below are typical for the 2025/2026 academic year and may change for 2026/2027. Always check current rates directly with landlords or accommodation providers.

- **On‑campus residences (University‑owned or affiliated)**: €6,000 – €9,000 per academic year (approx. 36 weeks). Includes utilities, internet, and sometimes a meal plan. Rooms are usually single with shared common areas. Demand is high – apply as early as possible.
- **Purpose‑built private student apartments (PBSA) within 1‑2 km of campus**: €700 – €1,100 per month (€7,700 – €12,100 for a 10‑month lease). Typically includes bills and internet. Many offer en‑suite rooms with shared kitchen and living space.
- **Shared houses / apartments in Castletroy**: €500 – €800 per person per month (excluding bills). Bills (electricity, gas, internet) add roughly €100 – €150 per month per person. Leases are usually 12 months, but some accept 10‑ or 11‑month contracts. Houses often require a deposit of one month’s rent.
- **Living in Limerick city centre (commuting)**: €550 – €850 per person per month for a shared house, plus travel costs. Bus monthly pass is about €80 (student rate) for unlimited travel on public buses. UL also runs a free campus shuttle from the city centre, but timings vary.

### Walk vs Transit Trade‑Off

Most UL students choose to live within walking distance of campus — the Castletroy area is the most popular choice.

**Walking**: If you live within a 15‑minute walk, you save on transport costs and time. Castletroy village and the campus perimeter have a good supply of student houses and apartments. The trade‑off is that you may need to walk to the nearest grocery shop (about 10 minutes from most Castletroy locations) and rely on buses or taxis for city centre trips.

**Cycling**: A bike is a practical option if on‑campus or Castletroy accommodation is full. The terrain is flat, and bike parking is plentiful. Cycling from the city centre takes 20 minutes. Bikes can be purchased second‑hand for €100‑€200.

![University of Limerick](https://img.unistay.cn/housing/p/amber-3132480.jpg)

**Transit**: If you rent in the city centre, the bus journey is typically 15‑20 minutes. The UL campus is served by Bus Éireann routes 304, 304A, and 310, plus the university’s own shuttle (free for students with a valid ID). Buses run every 15‑30 minutes during term, less frequently on weekends and evenings. The monthly student bus pass costs about €80. Some students also drive, but parking permits on campus are limited and cost around €200 per year.

**Trade‑off summary**: Walking/cycling saves money and time but limits your housing options. Transit gives you more variety (city centre or suburbs) but adds daily travel of 30‑50 minutes round‑trip and monthly costs.

### Shortlist Logic Using Nearby Residences

Because no specific residence names are provided in this guide, the shortlist logic below applies to any accommodation option near UL. Follow these steps to create your own shortlist:

1. **Determine your maximum acceptable commute time** — 15 minutes walking, 20 minutes cycling, or 30 minutes by bus. This will narrow your search area.
2. **Set a monthly budget (including bills)** — convert the yearly on‑campus fee to monthly (divide by 9 months for term‑time only, or 12 for full‑year). For private housing, subtract bills from your budget.
3. **Check availability for your start date** — on‑campus accommodation and PBSA often have dedicated application windows (e.g., May–June for September entry). Private houses may be advertised on property websites or through university‑approved lists from July onwards.
4. **Visit or request a virtual tour** — photos can be misleading. Ask about dampness, heating, and WiFi reliability.
5. **Read the contract carefully** — look for break clauses, deposit return conditions, and whether bills are included.
6. **Consider your needs**: single room vs sharing, en‑suite vs shared bathroom, parking, laundry, social spaces.
7. **Apply to at least two options** — if on‑campus is oversubscribed (which it often is), have a PBSA or private house as backup.

**Typical shortlist examples** (indicative, not exhaustive):

- On‑campus: safe, convenient, but limited space and higher annual cost.
- Close private PBSA: flexible lease lengths, often bills included, but can be more expensive per month than a shared house.
- House share in Castletroy: lower rent, more space, but you pay bills separately and need to organise utilities.
- City centre: more nightlife and cultural options, but commuting adds costs and time.
