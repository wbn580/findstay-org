## Knoxville City Context

Knoxville sits in eastern Tennessee, near the Great Smoky Mountains. The city is the third-largest in the state, with a population around 190,000. It has a distinct four-season climate—summers are warm and humid, winters are cool with occasional snow. The cost of living is generally lower than the US national average, which is reflected in the rental market around the university.

The campus is located near downtown Knoxville, within walking distance of restaurants, shops, and entertainment venues like the Tennessee Theatre and Market Square. The Tennessee River runs just south of the university, and a network of greenways provides walking and cycling paths. Public transit is managed by Knoxville Area Transit (KAT), with bus routes serving the campus and surrounding areas.

For students, Knoxville offers a blend of urban amenities and outdoor recreation. The nearby Smoky Mountains National Park draws hikers and nature enthusiasts. However, not all parts of the city are equally walkable, and the choice of housing often depends on whether a student has a car or is willing to rely on buses.

For the 2026–2027 academic year, here is a rough budget breakdown:

- **Economy band (approx. $750–$900 per month):** Shared apartments or studios in older complexes slightly farther from campus. Often fewer amenities but lower rent.
- **Mid-range band (approx. $900–$1,200 per month):** One-bedroom or shared two-bedroom units in newer student housing communities within a 15-minute walk or short bus ride.
- **Premium band ($1,200+ per month):** High-end studios or private rooms in luxury communities with fitness centres, pools, and shuttle services. Usually located very close to campus or downtown.

Many student housing operators offer per-bed leases, meaning you pay only for your room in a shared apartment. Utilities may be included or charged separately. Always verify the total monthly cost, including any fees for parking, amenities, or late payments.

## Walk vs. Transit Trade-Off

When choosing housing near the University of Tennessee, the distance to campus is a primary factor. The campus core—where most classes, libraries, and student unions are located—covers roughly 600 acres. Students living within a half-mile radius can easily walk to class, typically within 10–15 minutes. This area includes parts of the Fort Sanders neighbourhood directly north of campus and some downtown blocks.

Walking offers convenience and eliminates transport costs, but rents in the immediate campus vicinity tend to be higher. Some properties within a few blocks may also have noise concerns from nearby bars or fraternity houses.

For those willing to use public transit, bus routes 11, 12, 16, and 22 service the campus from surrounding neighbourhoods. A student semester pass (or included with some housing) can make commuting affordable. The trade-off: bus schedules may not align perfectly with early morning or late evening classes, and wait times can be 15–30 minutes depending on the route.

Cycling is another option. Knoxville has invested in bike lanes and trails, and the campus provides bike racks. A bicycle can expand your housing search to areas 1–3 miles away, which often have lower rents than the immediate walking zone. However, hills and weather can make biking less comfortable in winter or summer heat.

Students without a car should prioritise properties within a 20-minute walk or along a direct bus line. Those with a car have more flexibility, but must factor in parking permit costs (a UTK permit ranges from about $200 to $500 per year depending on lot) and traffic around game days (UT football games draw over 100,000 fans, causing gridlock near the stadium).

## Shortlist Logic Using Nearby Residences

The following five student housing properties are commonly rented by University of Tennessee students. They are not the only options, but they represent a range of distances, price points, and amenity levels. All prices are indicative and subject to change—confirm with the operator for the 2026–2027 academic year.

### 1. Commons At Knoxville

- **Location:** Approximately 1.5 miles from campus (east of downtown).
- **Typical rent:** Around $750–$850 per month for a shared room.
- **Walk/Transit:** A 30-minute walk or a short bus ride (route 11).
- **Why consider:** Offers a lower price point and includes a shuttle service to campus during peak hours. Units are newer with in-unit laundry and fitness centre. Suitable for budget-conscious students who don’t mind a commute.

### 2. University Park

- **Location:** Very close to campus, in the Fort Sanders area (north of the university).
- **Typical rent:** Approximately $900–$1,100 per month for a studio or 1-bedroom.
- **Walk/Transit:** 5–10 minute walk to most academic buildings.
- **Why consider:** Maximum walkability. The property is a cluster of older houses converted into apartments, so finishes vary. Many tenants appreciate the proximity to restaurants and campus events.

### 3. Society 865

- **Location:** Downtown Knoxville, about 1 mile southwest of campus.
- **Typical rent:** $1,000–$1,200 per month for a private bedroom.
- **Walk/Transit:** 20-minute walk to campus, or use a bus from Gay Street.
- **Why consider:** Downtown living with access to nightlife, shopping, and the Tennessee River. The building has a pool, gym, and rooftop lounge. Ideal for students who want an urban lifestyle and don’t mind a longer walk.

### 4. Heights Knoxville

- **Location:** West of campus, about 2 miles along Cumberland Avenue.
- **Typical rent:** Around $850–$950 per month for a shared unit.
- **Walk/Transit:** 10-minute bus ride (route 16) or 25–30 minute walk.
- **Why consider:** A mid-range option with a mix of studio and multi-bedroom apartments. Amenities include a pool and study rooms. Located near grocery stores and fast-food outlets, making daily errands easy.

### 5. Slate at 901

- **Location:** South of the Tennessee River, about 2.5 miles from campus.
- **Typical rent:** $750–$800 per month for a shared room (often lower than north-side options).
- **Walk/Transit:** Requires a car or bus (route 12). The drive is about 10 minutes without traffic.
- **Why consider:** Lowest price band among the five. The property is newer with modern fixtures. Best suited for students who have a vehicle or are comfortable with a longer commute.

**Selection logic:** If your priority is walkability, choose University Park or a property in Fort Sanders. If you want a balance of cost and amenities, Commons At Knoxville or Heights Knoxville offer shuttle or bus access. For the cheapest option, Slate at 901 is worth considering, but factor in transportation expenses. Society 865 appeals to those who prefer downtown life.

Always visit the property in person (or schedule a virtual tour) and read the lease carefully. Many student housing operators require a guarantor or a security deposit. Check whether utilities, internet, and parking are included.
