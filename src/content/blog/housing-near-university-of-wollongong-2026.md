## What student reviews say about the University of Wollongong

The University of Wollongong (UOW) holds a QS World University Ranking of 184 (2026) and a Google rating of 4.2 out of 5 based on 693 reviews. While the overall rating is positive, several recent reviews point to concerns that prospective students should weigh when choosing where to live and study.

Natasha Mann, who started in 2021, gave a 1-star review saying: *“I am writing with genuine concern for future students considering enrolment at this university. When I commenced my studies in 2021, I believed I was joining a supportive and reputable campus. Unfortunately, over the years, my experience has been overwhelmingly negative.”* Her review highlights a gap between initial expectations and the lived experience — something that can affect how you feel about campus life.

Another reviewer, P N P, also gave 1 star and noted: *“I expected that enrolling in an autism program specialising in inclusive and special education would mean these principles were actually put into practice. Instead, anyone with an access plan is treated as a burden as too many students in the program have access plans.”* This suggests that support services may not always match the stated policies, which could influence your choice of accommodation if you need specific campus access.

Jack Wilson (2 stars) wrote: *“It’s not horrible, but it really shouldn’t be your first choice. It feels like the students come second to profit. Course requirements change often, and a shocking amount of subjects are gutted because there’s not enough money being made from it.”* Course instability may affect how long you need to stay near campus or whether you prefer flexible rental agreements.

C C (1 star) added: *“THE PROGRAM HANDBOOK CHANGED TOO MUCH JUST IN A SHORT PERIOD, MAKING PEOPLE DOUBT ITS DESIGN OF LEARNING COURSES.”* For housing, this reinforces the value of short lease terms or month-to-month options, which are not always available in standard student apartments.

Despite these critical voices, the 4.2 average suggests many students have a satisfactory experience. Still, the reviews make clear that you should verify current conditions — especially support services and course stability — before committing to a long-term housing contract.

## Wollongong city context

Wollongong is a coastal city about 80 km south of Sydney, with a population of roughly 300,000. It offers a quieter, less expensive alternative to Sydney, while still providing shops, restaurants, and recreational areas. UOW’s main campus is located in the suburb of Gwynneville, around 3 km north-west of the Wollongong city centre.

The city has a mild climate year-round, with warm summers and cool winters. Public transport includes buses and a train line that runs to Sydney in about 90 minutes. Many students choose to live close to campus or along the bus routes that serve the university.

Because Wollongong is a relatively compact city, it is feasible to walk or cycle to campus if you live within 2–3 km. For those on a tighter budget, living further out and taking a bus or train can reduce rent — but adds commuting time and cost.

## Budget bands for student housing (2026/2027 indicative)

![University of Wollongong](https://img.unistay.net/housing/p/amber-3049635.jpg)

Rental prices in Wollongong are generally lower than in Sydney, but they have risen in recent years. The figures below are indicative — confirm with the operator before signing any agreement.

- **Budget: AUD 150 – 230 per week**  
  This range typically covers a room in a shared house (3–5 bedrooms) in suburbs such as Gwynneville, Keiraville, or North Wollongong. Utilities may be included or separate. Shared houses often require a 6–12 month lease.
- **Mid-range: AUD 230 – 320 per week**  
  For this amount you can expect a room in a newer shared apartment, a studio in a purpose-built student accommodation (PBSA), or a small one-bedroom flat in the city centre. Some PBSAs include bills, Wi-Fi, and access to common areas.
- **Higher range: AUD 320 – 450+ per week**  
  This covers dedicated one-bedroom apartments or larger studios close to campus or in the CBD. On-campus residential colleges (catered or self-catered) may fall into this bracket. Lease terms are often semester-based or annual.

All figures are indicative for 2026 and 2027 — always request a written quote from the housing provider.

## Walk vs transit trade-off

Choosing between walking distance and using public transport involves balancing cost, convenience, and time.

**Walk to campus (0–2 km)**  
Areas like Gwynneville, Keiraville, and parts of North Wollongong are within a 15–30 minute walk of the main campus. Walking saves money on bus fares and gives you flexibility. Rent in these zones tends to be higher — expect the mid-range or higher band. Housing supply is limited, so you may need to start searching early.

**Short bus or bike ride (2–5 km)**  
Suburbs such as Wollongong CBD, Fairy Meadow, and Mount Pleasant offer more housing options and slightly lower rents. A bus trip to campus takes 10–20 minutes. A bicycle can be a good alternative, but the hilly terrain in some parts is worth considering. Bus fares (Single Trip Concession AUD 2.40–3.20 in 2026, indicative) add weekly costs but may be offset by lower rent.

**Train plus bus (5+ km)**  
If you choose to live in areas like Dapto, Unanderra, or even the far northern suburbs (Thirroul, etc.), you will need both a train and a bus. The commute can take 40–60 minutes each way. Rent can drop to the lower end of the budget band, but travel costs and time increase. This option works best if you have classes only a few days a week or prefer a quieter living environment.

The trade-off is straightforward: the closer you live to campus, the more you pay in rent but the less you spend on time and transport. Make a clear weekly budget that includes rent, utilities, and transport to compare options.

## How to shortlist your accommodation

When evaluating housing options near the University of Wollongong, use the following logic:

1. **Set your budget (indicative)**  
   Know your maximum weekly rent including bills. If you need to stay within AUD 200–250, shared houses in Gwynneville or Keiraville are the most realistic. If you can go above AUD 300, you can consider studio apartments or PBSAs.
2. **Decide your lease flexibility**  
   The reviews above mention course changes and program handbook updates — if you anticipate changing subjects or even universities, look for residences that offer semester-length leases or break clauses. On-campus colleges may allow semester contracts; private apartments often require 12 months.
3. **Choose your walking distance tolerance**  
   If you want a 10-minute walk, focus on Gwynneville and Keiraville. If you are okay with a 20-minute walk, North Wollongong is also feasible. For longer commutes, include Fairy Meadow or CBD in your shortlist.
4. **Check transport links**  
   Use Google Maps to check bus routes from the address to UOW. The free Gong Shuttle bus (operated by the university) covers some suburbs, but its routes are limited. Ensure the bus stop is within 5 minutes of your front door.
5. **Read recent reviews and visit**  
   Before signing, ask current tenants about noise, maintenance, and management. If possible, view the room in person or via video call. This is especially important for shared houses and smaller private rentals.
6. **Consider inclusive vs exclusive utilities**  
   Some PBSAs include all bills; most shared houses divide bills separately. Add up total costs — not just rent.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is on-campus accommodation guaranteed for first-year students?

UOW does offer on-campus residences, but availability is limited and not guaranteed for all applicants. You should apply as soon as offers are released. For 2026 and 2027, check the university’s accommodation portal for application dates. Off-campus private housing often requires a separate search — start 3–4 months before your move-in date.

### How far is the city centre from the university?

The Wollongong city centre is about 3 km from the main campus. By bus, the trip takes 10–15 minutes. Walking takes around 30–40 minutes. Biking is also an option on mostly flat roads. If you want easy access to shops, restaurants, and the beach, living in the CBD and commuting to campus is a common choice.
