### City Context: Wellington in 2026-2027

Wellington is New Zealand’s political and cultural hub, with a population just over 200,000 in the city proper. The city is built on hills, which means many suburbs are connected by winding roads, cable cars, and bus routes. The main VUW campus is at Kelburn, a short walk from the central business district but up a steep incline. The university also has campuses in Pipitea (law, business) and Te Aro (architecture, design), so students may need to factor in inter-campus travel.

The local rental market is competitive, especially near the university. Purpose-built student accommodation (PBSA), private flats, and shared houses are the main options. Many students aim for Kelburn, Aro Valley, or Mount Cook to minimise walking distance. Others choose more affordable suburbs like Newtown, Island Bay, or Karori, relying on buses or biking (electric bikes are popular on hills). The city’s public transport includes buses operated by Metlink, with a student discount available via a Snapper card.

Climate is mild but windy and rainy; a good rain jacket and sturdy walking shoes are essential. Most leases run for 12 months (February to February) to align with the academic year, though some PBSA offers shorter contracts. In 2026 and 2027, the housing supply is expected to remain tight, so early planning — ideally 3–6 months before arrival — is recommended.

### Walk vs Transit Trade-Off

Walking to campus is a significant consideration given the hilly terrain. Reviews highlight “the walk” as a notable part of the experience. Students living in Kelburn or Aro Valley can typically reach the main campus on foot in 5–20 minutes, depending on their exact street and fitness level. The cable car from Lambton Quay to Kelburn runs every 10 minutes and is a popular option for those living in the city centre or near its base — a single trip costs about 5 NZD with a Snapper card.

![Victoria University of Wellington](https://img.unistay.cn/housing/p/amber-254712.jpg)

For suburbs farther out (e.g., Newtown, Island Bay, Karori), bus travel time is 20–45 minutes each way. Buses run frequently during peak hours but less often late at night. The trade-off is clear: proximity often means higher rent and less space, while longer commutes offer more affordable housing and larger rooms but consume time and daily transportation fees.

Students who bike should be prepared for steep hills — electric bikes are common. Walking is generally safe, but Wellington is windy and rainy; a 20-minute walk in stormy weather can be unpleasant. Living within a 15-minute walk of campus is ideal for minimising transit costs and schedule flexibility, but such properties are in high demand and fill quickly.
