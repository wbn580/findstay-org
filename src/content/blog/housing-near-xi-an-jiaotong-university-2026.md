## Xi’an: A City of History and Innovation

Xi’an, the capital of Shaanxi Province, is one of China’s oldest cities, famous for the Terracotta Warriors and its role as the starting point of the Silk Road. Today, it is also a major education and technology hub. The university’s location in the Bei Lin district (the “Forest of Steles” area) places it near the city’s historical core, with access to shopping, dining, and public transport.

For students, Xi’an offers a relatively lower cost of living compared to Beijing or Shanghai. However, housing near universities can vary significantly in price depending on type (dormitory vs. private rental) and distance from campus. Given that XJTU’s main campus is in a lively commercial zone (“交大商业街区” as listed in the address), you will find a mix of older residential buildings and newer apartment complexes within walking distance.

## Walk vs. Transit Trade-off

Xi’an Jiaotong University’s main campus is compact and walkable — many lecture buildings, libraries, and dining halls are within a 10–15 minute walk of each other. If you choose accommodation within a 1-km radius of the address (28 Xian Ning Xi Lu), you can comfortably walk to class, saving time and transport costs. Commercial streets along Xian Ning Xi Lu and surrounding alleys offer convenience stores, eateries, and public transport stops (bus and metro Line 3).

However, living right next to campus often comes with higher rent for smaller spaces. Students on a tighter budget may look slightly further — 2–3 km away — where larger apartments are available at lower per-room costs. The trade-off is a 20–30 minute commute by shared bike or bus. Xi’an has an expanding metro network; Line 3 passes near the university area, connecting to other parts of the city. For those who prefer cycling, the city is relatively flat, making bike commutes feasible year-round (except during rare heavy snow).

Consider also the campus experience: living within walking distance allows you to easily use the library, sports facilities, and attend evening events. If you are an international student or new to Xi’an, the convenience of a walkable location may outweigh the cost savings of a longer commute — at least for the first semester until you become familiar with the city.
