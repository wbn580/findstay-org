Bristol combines top-tier universities, a legendary music scene (Massive Attack, Portishead), and the independent spirit of a city that voted to ban advertising for fossil fuels, fast food, and gambling. It's the UK's most countercultural student city — and it's not cheap. But for students who want creativity alongside their education, Bristol is unmatched.

### UoB (Clifton & Redland)

**Clifton**: One of the UK's most beautiful neighborhoods — Georgian terraces, the Suspension Bridge, independent bookshops, and Bristol's best restaurants. 5–15 min walk to campus. Rooms £130–210/week.

**Redland**: Next to Clifton, slightly more residential and slightly cheaper. Whiteladies Road has excellent cafes and pubs. 10 min walk. Rooms £120–190/week.

**Cotham & Kingsdown**: Between Clifton and the city centre. More terraced houses, less polished than Clifton. 10–15 min walk. Rooms £110–180/week.

### UWE (Frenchay Campus)

**Fishponds**: The main UWE student area. Diverse, affordable, with a proper high street and excellent international food. 10 min bus to Frenchay campus. Rooms £90–140/week.

**St Werburghs**: Bristol's hidden gem — a former industrial area turned bohemian enclave with city farm, climbing centre, and excellent pubs. 10 min bike to UWE. Rooms £95–145/week.

### City Centre & Harbourside

Modern apartments along the Floating Harbour. 15 min walk to UoB. Studios £180–300/week in PBSA (iQ Bristol, Unite Students, Student Roost).

### FAQ

**Why is Bristol so popular?** Unique culture, excellent music/arts scene, proximity to London (90 min train), and genuinely good universities. It's basically London for people who hate London.
**Is Bristol as expensive as London?** 20–30% cheaper for rent, 10% cheaper for going out. You can have a good student life on £900–1,200/month versus London's £1,200–1,700.
**Bristol's weird thing?** Banksy is from here. There's a street art walking tour. The independent streak is real — Bristol has a local currency (Bristol Pound) and voted to ban diesel cars.
