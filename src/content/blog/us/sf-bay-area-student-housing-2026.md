The San Francisco Bay Area is home to two of the world's most elite universities — Stanford and UC Berkeley — plus a constellation of smaller schools. It's also one of the most expensive housing markets in the world. Students need to be strategic, but there are ways to make it work.

## UC Berkeley

**Berkeley (Southside)** — The immediate area south of campus. Telegraph Avenue, cheap eats, and the highest concentration of students on the West Coast. Rooms USD 1,000–1,800/month.

**Berkeley (Northside)** — Quieter, more residential, closer to the hills and the fire trails. Slightly pricier. Rooms USD 1,100–2,000/month.

**Oakland (Rockridge/Temescal)** — Just south of Berkeley. Two BART stops from campus. Rockridge has great restaurants on College Avenue. Rooms USD 900–1,600/month.

**Albany & El Cerrito** — North of Berkeley along the BART line. Suburban feel with lower prices. Rooms USD 800–1,400/month.

## Stanford (Palo Alto)

Stanford guarantees on-campus housing for all four years of undergraduate study — a massive advantage. Graduate housing is also available but competitive.

**Palo Alto** — One of the most expensive real estate markets in America. Rooms USD 1,400–2,500/month outside of university housing.

**Menlo Park & Redwood City** — North of Stanford. 15–20 min by bike or Caltrain. Rooms USD 1,100–2,000/month.

**Mountain View** — South of Stanford, near Google's headquarters. Rooms USD 1,200–2,200/month.

## USF (San Francisco)

USF sits in the Inner Richmond, near Golden Gate Park.

**Inner Richmond**: Walking distance. Diverse food scene (Burmese, dim sum, Russian bakeries). Rooms USD 1,100–1,900/month.
**Sunset District**: 15 min bus through the park. Foggy but affordable by SF standards. Rooms USD 900–1,600/month.
**Daly City**: Just south of SF on BART. Cheaper, suburban, excellent Filipino food. Rooms USD 800–1,400/month.
